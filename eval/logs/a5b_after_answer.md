## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Level I evidence, non-surgical endodontic retreatment can be completed in either a single visit or two visits with calcium hydroxide interappointment medication, as both protocols yield equivalent periapical healing and success rates at 18–24 months [[PMID:34555421]] [[PMID:35488883]]. The choice should therefore be driven by case-specific factors — infection burden, presence of active exudate, patient tolerance, and operator workflow — rather than an expectation of a healing advantage for either regimen. Single-visit retreatment may produce a modestly lower incidence of mild post-operative pain at 1 day, and offers reduced chairside time [[PMID:33399930]] [[PMID:30054099]]; two-visit protocols remain preferable when disinfection cannot be adequately achieved in one session, in line with animal-model data suggesting superior histological repair with interim calcium hydroxide [[PMID:38407663]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**

The most recent Cochrane review synthesised 47 RCTs (n=5693 teeth) comparing single-visit versus multiple-visit root canal treatment, but was framed around primary treatment rather than retreatment [[PMID:36512807]]. It found no difference in radiological failure (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81–1.07; moderate certainty) or tooth extraction between regimens, though single-visit protocols were associated with a higher proportion of participants reporting pain within one week (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.14–2.09; moderate certainty) [[PMID:36512807]]. These findings inform, but do not directly answer, the retreatment question.

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

Three RCTs directly address single- versus two-visit retreatment. Karaoglan et al. randomised 100 single-rooted teeth with periapical lesions and reported healing rates of 88.6% (single-visit) versus 86.7% (two-visit with Ca(OH)₂) at 24 months, with no significant difference [[PMID:35488883]]. Toia et al. (n=40) found equivalent periapical lesion volume reduction on CBCT at 18 months between the two protocols, although the two-visit group showed lower lipoteichoic acid levels post-treatment [[PMID:34555421]]. For post-operative pain, Nunes et al.'s meta-analysis of 4 RCTs found single-visit retreatment produced less mild pain at days 1 and 30, with no difference for moderate/severe pain [[PMID:33399930]]; Erdem Hepsenoglu et al. (n=150) similarly reported significantly less pain in single-visit retreatment than in two-visit protocols using either Ca(OH)₂ or chlorhexidine as interappointment medicament [[PMID:30054099]]. Duzgun et al. (n=96) showed sealer chemistry (Ca(OH)₂, calcium silicate, or epoxy resin) did not modify single-visit retreatment pain outcomes [[PMID:41068727]], and Işık & Aydın found obturation technique did not affect 6-month PAI healing after single-visit retreatment [[PMID:39609817]]. The systematic review by Sabeti et al. of contemporary retreatment reported pooled success of 78–87% and identified number of visits as one of several outcome predictors, though not as a dominant factor [[PMID:38280514]]. A recent animal-model systematic review by de Oliveira Neto et al. concluded that two-visit protocols with calcium hydroxide produced superior histological repair (less inflammatory infiltrate, better apical sealing, fewer residual microorganisms) — a biologically relevant finding that has not translated into clinical superiority [[PMID:38407663]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort Studies**

Jurič et al. (n=1259 teeth, 13-year private-practice cohort) identified single-visit treatment as an independent negative prognostic factor (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.21–0.75) using strict radiographic success criteria — the only high-quality real-world dataset to flag a disadvantage for single-visit protocols [[PMID:38243912]]. Vieira et al. (n=304), by contrast, found the treatment outcome was negatively affected by a higher number of visits without intracanal medication, and reported retreatment success of 78% (rigid) and 83% (lenient) with no adverse effect attributable to single-visit protocols per se [[PMID:38214429]]. These cohorts disagree, reflecting the confounding of visit number with case selection, operator preference, and disinfection protocol in observational data.

**Overall synthesis**

Cochrane and RCT-level evidence for both primary treatment and retreatment converge on clinical equivalence between single- and two-visit protocols. The tension lies between (a) animal histology and one large retrospective cohort suggesting a biological advantage for two-visit protocols with calcium hydroxide, and (b) RCTs showing no clinical healing difference and slightly less mild post-operative pain with single-visit protocols. Because the human RCT evidence is more directly applicable, single-visit retreatment is defensible as a default when adequate disinfection is achievable in one session.
