# single-vs-multiple-visit

> Single-visit versus multiple-visit root canal treatment for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis

intent: None  |  papers: 96  |  cost: $1.4172

---
## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Cochrane-level evidence, single-visit and multiple-visit root canal treatment produce equivalent long-term outcomes for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis — there is no difference in radiographic healing, tooth extraction, swelling, or flare-up rate, though single-visit treatment carries a moderate-certainty increase in short-term (≤1 week) postoperative pain [[PMID:36512807]]. Choose visit strategy based on case-specific factors (ability to dry the canal, presence of acute exacerbation, patient logistics, operator time), not on an expectation of better healing with either protocol [[PMID:36512807]] [[PMID:41856391]]. When a multi-visit approach is chosen for a heavily infected canal, an interappointment calcium hydroxide dressing has biological rationale — it further reduces intracanal bacterial burden before obturation [[PMID:22794203]] — but this microbiological advantage has not translated into a demonstrable healing benefit in randomised trials.

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**

The 2022 Cochrane review by Mergoni and colleagues is the highest-tier evidence and directly addresses this question, pooling 47 RCTs with 5,693 teeth [[PMID:36512807]]. It found no difference between single- and multiple-visit protocols for radiological failure (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81–1.07; 13 studies, 1,505 teeth; moderate certainty) or tooth extraction (very low certainty, 2 studies). There was moderate-certainty evidence that single-visit treatment produced a higher proportion of patients reporting pain within one week (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.14–2.09), with subgroup analysis showing this effect was significant for vital teeth and when mechanical instrumentation was used. No difference was found in swelling/flare-up, analgesic use, or sinus tract presence, though certainty for these secondary outcomes was very low. The authors concluded — as in the 2007 and 2016 versions — that neither regimen is demonstrably superior.

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

The 2026 umbrella review by Gupta and colleagues synthesised 12 systematic reviews and concluded that higher-confidence reviews show no consistent or clinically important difference in postoperative pain between protocols, though heterogeneity in outcome definitions limits certainty [[PMID:41856391]]. Kumar and colleagues' 2025 systematic review similarly found inconsistent results across primary studies, with eight showing no difference and six favouring single-visit for less post-obturation pain [[PMID:39780175]]. The Schwendicke and Gostemeyer meta-analysis (29 trials, 4,341 patients) found no difference in long-term complications (RR 1.00) or pain (RR 0.99), but identified a significantly higher flare-up risk with single-visit treatment (RR 2.13, 95% CI 1.16–3.89) on very weak evidence, and trial sequential analysis showed firm evidence had not been reached for any outcome [[PMID:28148534]]. Su and colleagues' earlier systematic review reached similar conclusions on healing but found single-visit produced less short-term post-obturation pain [[PMID:21238790]].

The largest single RCT by Paredes-Vieyra and Enriquez (300 teeth with apical periodontitis, 2-year follow-up) found no statistically significant difference in healing (96.6% single-visit vs 89.0% multi-visit) [[PMID:22892729]]. Wong and colleagues (220 teeth, ≥18 months) reported success rates of 88.9% vs 87.4% with equivalent pain outcomes and significantly shorter chairside time for single-visit (62 vs 93 min) [[PMID:26687126]]. Molander and colleagues (101 teeth, 2 years) found 65% vs 75% healing with no significant difference, and importantly noted that teeth obturated after a negative bacterial culture healed at 80% versus 44% when cultured positive [[PMID:17889679]]. Penesis and colleagues (63 teeth at 12 months) found equally favourable periapical healing between one-visit and two-visit protocols using calcium hydroxide/chlorhexidine interim dressing [[PMID:18291270]].

**Level II — Prospective Studies**

The large Ng and colleagues prospective cohort (1,170 roots primary treatment, 1,314 roots retreatment) identified 11 prognostic factors for periapical healing but did not identify visit number as an independent predictor — rather, factors such as apical patency, extension of cleaning to the apical terminus, absence of flare-up, and coronal restoration quality dominated [[PMID:21366626]]. A histobacteriologic study by Vera and colleagues on 13 roots showed that two-visit protocols with calcium hydroxide medication produced superior microbiological outcomes: two cases were rendered bacteria-free versus none in the single-visit group, with residual bacteria more frequent in isthmuses, ramifications, and dentinal tubules when treated in a single visit [[PMID:22794203]]. This provides mechanistic support for interappointment medication but has not translated into demonstrably better clinical healing in the RCT evidence above.

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort**

The large Jurič and colleagues cohort (1,259 teeth, 13-year follow-up, single endodontist) identified single-visit treatment as one of 11 factors associated with reduced odds of success (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.21–0.75 against multiple visits) [[PMID:38243912]]. This retrospective finding conflicts with the Cochrane pooled RCT estimate; per the tier hierarchy, the Cochrane conclusion prevails, but the signal is worth noting for cases with complex anatomy or heavy infection. Vieira and colleagues (304 teeth) similarly found treatment outcome was negatively affected by higher number of visits when no intracanal medication was used [[PMID:38214429]]. Artaza and colleagues reported 89% success (loose criteria) for teeth with large lesions predominantly treated in a single visit [[PMID:39151889]].

**Level IIIb — Case-Control**

Işik and colleagues found equivalent healing after single-visit non-surgical retreatment in type 2 diabetics and healthy controls [[PMID:40497767]]. Baltieri and colleagues reported 92.5% success (loose criteria) for single-visit retreatment with 2% chlorhexidine gel and foraminal enlargement [[PMID:39313095]].

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews**

Gulabivala and Ng's comprehensive synthesis frames outcomes as dominated by the interaction between host/infection biology and the operator's ability to shift the microbial ecology, rather than by visit number per se — placing the single-vs-multiple-visit debate as secondary to overall disinfection efficacy and coronal seal [[PMID:36710532]]. The ESE S3-level guideline (2023) accommodates both single- and multiple-visit approaches, prioritising case-specific factors and aseptic protocol adherence over visit number [[PMID:37772327]].
