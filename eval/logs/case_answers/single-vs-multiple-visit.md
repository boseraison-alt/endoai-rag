# single-vs-multiple-visit

> Single-visit versus multiple-visit root canal treatment for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis

intent: None  |  papers: 119  |  cost: $1.6462

## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Cochrane-level evidence, single-visit and multiple-visit root canal treatment for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis produce equivalent radiographic healing and tooth-survival outcomes at ≥12 months, so the choice can be driven by case-specific factors (drainage, canal dryness, patient logistics) rather than an expectation of better periapical healing from either protocol [[PMID:36512807]] [[PMID:41856391]]. Clinicians should counsel patients that single-visit treatment carries a modestly higher risk of pain within the first week (moderate certainty) and possibly a higher flare-up risk, while offering the advantage of reduced chairside time [[PMID:36512807]] [[PMID:28148534]]. When canals cannot be dried, acute swelling is present, or a persistent exudate is encountered, multiple-visit treatment with a calcium hydroxide interappointment dressing remains the more defensible option [[PMID:9477818]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**
The 2022 Cochrane review by Mergoni et al. is the highest-tier synthesis available and included 47 RCTs with 5693 teeth analysed [[PMID:36512807]]. For necrotic teeth and those with apical periodontitis, there was no evidence of a difference between single- and multiple-visit RoCT for radiological failure at ≥1 year (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81–1.07; moderate certainty), tooth extraction, swelling/flare-up, or sinus tract persistence. However, moderate-certainty evidence showed a higher proportion of patients reporting pain within one week after single-visit treatment (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.14–2.09), with no difference in pain intensity at 72 h or analgesic use. The earlier Cochrane iteration (Figini et al. 2008) reached the same conclusion regarding radiological equivalence but flagged slightly more analgesic use and swelling after single-visit treatment [[PMID:18718362]].

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**
The 2026 umbrella review by Gupta et al. synthesised 12 systematic reviews and concluded that higher-confidence reviews do not show a consistent or clinically important difference in postoperative pain between single- and multiple-visit protocols, confirming the Cochrane conclusion at the meta-review level [[PMID:41856391]]. Schwendicke and Gostemeyer's meta-analysis with trial sequential analysis (29 trials, 4341 patients) found no significant difference in long-term complications (RR 1.00) or short-term pain (RR 0.99) but reported a significantly increased flare-up risk after single-visit treatment (RR 2.13, 95% CI 1.16–3.89, very weak evidence), suggesting multiple visits may be preferable for teeth with periapical lesions [[PMID:28148534]]. Su et al.'s earlier systematic review on specifically infected canals reached the same equivalence conclusion for healing but noted less short-term post-obturation pain in single-visit groups — a directional finding that conflicts with the newer Cochrane data [[PMID:21238790]]. Wong et al.'s RCT (n=220, mean follow-up 29 months) found identical success rates (88.9% vs 87.4%) with single-visit treatment saving ~31 min of chairside time [[PMID:26687126]]. Karaoglan et al.'s RCT in retreatment cases (n=100, 24-month follow-up) similarly found no difference in healing between single- and two-visit protocols [[PMID:35488883]]. AbdurRahman et al.'s RCT specifically in necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis found no significant postoperative pain difference between single-visit and multiple-visit with triple antibiotic paste dressing [[PMID:32047601]].

**Classic / Foundational**
Sjögren et al.'s landmark study demonstrated that periapical healing occurred in 94% of cases yielding a negative pre-obturation bacterial culture versus only 68% when the culture was positive — a statistically significant difference that historically underpinned the argument for multiple-visit treatment with an interappointment antimicrobial dressing in infected canals [[PMID:9477818]]. Sjögren et al.'s 1990 cohort reported that only 86% of teeth with pulp necrosis and periapical radiolucency healed at 8–10 years, versus >96% for vital cases, establishing that the preoperative periapical status is the dominant prognostic variable independent of visit number [[PMID:2084204]]. Byström and Sundqvist showed that even with meticulous mechanical instrumentation and saline irrigation, bacteria persisted in 7 of 15 canals through five appointments, providing the microbiological rationale for interappointment calcium hydroxide dressing [[PMID:6947391]].

**Level II — Prospective Studies**
Ng et al.'s large prospective cohort (n=750 for 2°RCTx, n=702 for 1°RCTx) identified interappointment flare-up and other technical variables as significant predictors of periapical healing, but did not identify visit number itself as an independent predictor when other factors were controlled [[PMID:21366626]]. The US endodontist survey by Tsai and Kim reported that 74.9% of US endodontists routinely complete necrotic cases in a single visit and 81% perceive no outcome difference, with multiple-visit treatment predominantly reserved for canals that cannot be dried (92.6%) or for diffuse swelling (79.9%) — patterns consistent with published evidence [[PMID:42214667]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort**
Jurič et al.'s single-operator cohort of 1259 teeth followed 1–4 years found that single-visit treatment was associated with reduced odds of success (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.21–0.75) compared with multiple visits in multivariate analysis — a finding that runs counter to the pooled Cochrane and umbrella review data [[PMID:38243912]]. This retrospective signal must be weighed against, and does not override, the higher-tier RCT-based evidence, but it does suggest operator- and case-selection effects may matter in real-world practice. Artaza et al.'s cohort of 199 teeth with large apical periodontitis lesions treated predominantly in a single visit reported 89% success under loose criteria and 67% under rigid criteria, demonstrating that single-visit treatment of complex necrotic cases can achieve high healing rates in expert hands [[PMID:39151889]].
