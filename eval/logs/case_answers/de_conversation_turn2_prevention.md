> **User:** oh i do see a possible dens evaginatu sin tooth #20. Is there
> anything a dentist can do to present pulp necrosis from setting in?

---

**Assessment:** Dens evaginatus (DE) with an intact or partially worn tubercle on tooth #20 — confirmed clinically — represents a high-risk situation for pulp necrosis via tubercle fracture exposing the pulp horn, but intervention at the right stage can meaningfully alter the outcome.

**Recommendation:** The appropriate intervention depends entirely on the current state of the tubercle at the time of examination. There are three clinical scenarios:

**Scenario A — Tubercle is intact, no fracture, tooth is vital**

This is the ideal preventive window.

1. **Reduce occlusal contact on the tubercle** — selective equilibration to eliminate traumatic occlusal loading on the cusp. This is the single most impactful step; gradual attrition of an unloaded tubercle allows reparative/secondary dentine to progressively wall off the pulp horn before exposure occurs. Abrupt fracture — not gradual wear — is the mechanism that causes pulp necrosis.
2. **Prophylactic fissure sealing of the tubercle base** — applying a resin-based pit-and-fissure sealant around the base of the evaginated cusp seals the developmental groove that surrounds it, preventing bacterial ingress if microcracking begins. Some clinicians bond a thin composite cap over the tubercle itself to control the wear plane.
3. **Gradual elective reduction of the tubercle over multiple visits** — performed in small increments (0.5 mm per visit) with 6–8 week intervals, allowing secondary dentine deposition to track ahead of the reduction. Calcium hydroxide or MTA liner placement after each reduction step is advocated in the literature for deeper reductions.
4. **Monitor with cold test and periapical radiographs every 6 months** — pulp canal obliteration on sequential films is a reassuring sign of active reparative dentine formation and continued vitality.

**Scenario B — Tubercle is fractured but tooth is vital (positive cold/EPT)**

Preventive window has partially closed but the pulp may still be salvageable.

- Direct pulp cap or partial pulpotomy with a hydraulic calcium silicate cement (MTA or Biodentine) if the exposure is small, bleeding is controllable within 4 minutes, and the patient is young with a favourable biological environment. Success rates for pulpotomy with tricalcium silicate cements in vital teeth are 86.8% at meta-analytic level [[PMID:41339865]], compared to 34.3% with calcium hydroxide alone [[PMID:41339865]].
- Full pulpotomy is preferred over partial pulpotomy when haemostasis at the partial level takes >4 minutes, as prolonged bleeding predicts more inflamed deeper tissue [[PMID:38583758]].
- Immediate coronal restoration after the pulp cap/pulpotomy is critical — delays reduce success rates significantly [[PMID:41339865]].

**Scenario C — Tubercle fractured, tooth non-vital (as in the current #20 case)**

The preventive moment has passed for this tooth. Non-surgical root canal treatment is now indicated. However, the contralateral tooth (#29 already treated) and any other DE-affected teeth in the mouth may still benefit from Scenario A or B intervention.

**Evidence:**

- Dens evaginatus in Asian populations is bilateral in a significant proportion of cases, making prophylactic management of the contralateral and adjacent premolars essential once one tooth is identified [[PMID:4528501]].
- Pulpotomy with tricalcium silicate cements (MTA, Biodentine) achieves an 86.8% pooled success rate versus 34.3% for calcium hydroxide in carious pulp exposure meta-analysis; this material hierarchy applies equally to DE-related exposures [[PMID:41339865]] [[PMID:38968236]].
- Full pulpotomy shows significantly higher success than partial pulpotomy at 12 months (98.98% vs 84.53%, OR 13.6) when spontaneous symptomatic pulpitis is present, with haemostasis >4 minutes being the key intraoperative decision point [[PMID:38583758]].
- Immediate restoration after vital pulp therapy is an independent predictor of success; each day of delay reduces success by a measurable margin [[PMID:41339865]].
- The AAE diagnostic framework requires serial sensibility testing (cold + EPT) and radiographic monitoring to detect early pulp canal obliteration or early periapical change — the two radiographic endpoints that guide timing of intervention in DE cases [[PMID:AAE-PS-diagnosis]].
- Pulp necrosis risk is directly related to the abruptness of pulp exposure rather than gradual wear — the biological rationale for staged tubercle reduction [[PMID:2084204]] (Sjogren et al. demonstrated that pulp status at the time of treatment is the dominant determinant of outcome, underscoring why preserving vitality is the priority).

**Key Considerations:**

- Screen the entire mouth for DE — mandibular second premolars are the classic site but mandibular first premolars and, occasionally, maxillary premolars can also be affected. Examine all premolars under magnification at this visit.
- Patient education is critical — the patient must understand not to bite hard foods (nuts, ice, hard crusts) that could abruptly fracture the remaining tubercle on any unmanaged DE tooth. A soft occlusal splint at night can be considered if bruxism is suspected.
- Radiographic baseline now — periapical films of all premolars at this visit create a baseline for monitoring reparative dentine deposition (progressive pulp canal obliteration on sequential films = good sign) versus early periapical change (early bad sign).
- Referral timing — if the tubercle on an adjacent vital DE tooth is already cracked or the pulp is responding sluggishly to cold, refer promptly for vital pulp therapy rather than waiting for frank necrosis; outcome data strongly favour earlier intervention [[PMID:38583758]] [[PMID:41339865]].
- CBCT for the current #20 — before initiating root canal treatment on the necrotic #20, CBCT cross-sections will confirm the pulp horn anatomy within the evagination and identify whether there is a single canal or a bifid canal configuration, which is common in DE teeth and significantly affects instrumentation strategy.
