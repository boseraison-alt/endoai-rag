## Laser physics, wavelengths, and endodontic photobiology

## Module 1 — Laser physics, wavelengths, and endodontic photobiology

**The physics of laser–tissue interaction in the root canal**

Laser is an acronym for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation. Every clinical effect — bactericidal, photoacoustic, or photobiomodulatory — depends on the match between the laser's wavelength and the dominant chromophore in the target tissue: water, hydroxyapatite, haemoglobin, or melanin. Four mechanisms govern endodontic photobiology: *photothermal* (heat-mediated protein denaturation and membrane disruption), *photoacoustic/cavitation* (pressure waves that stream irrigant into anatomical spaces), *photochemical/photodynamic* (reactive oxygen species generated by photosensitizer excitation), and *photobiomodulation* (low-level modulation of cellular metabolism) [[PMID:36978686]] [[PMID:42198922]].

**Wavelength families and their endodontic targets**

Four laser families dominate endodontic use, each with a distinct absorption profile:

- **Nd:YAG (1,064 nm):** near-infrared; absorbed primarily by pigmented tissue and dentinal chromophores. At 1.5 W / 15 Hz / four 5-second cycles, Nd:YAG achieved 99.7% reduction in *Enterococcus faecalis* load ex vivo through photothermal protein denaturation and cell membrane disruption — but not complete sterilization [[PMID:16776759]]. The 2025 PRISMA-compliant systematic review by Fiegler-Rudol et al. confirmed that Nd:YAG's ability to penetrate dentinal tubules and disrupt mixed-species biofilms constitutes significant adjunctive value, while emphasising that complete sterilization was not consistently achieved [[PMID:40565094]].
- **Er:YAG (2,940 nm) and Er,Cr:YSGG (2,780 nm):** mid-infrared; peak absorption by water and hydroxyapatite. A 2025 PRISMA systematic review confirmed that Er:YAG laser activation significantly improves root canal disinfection, biofilm disruption, and smear layer removal over conventional or ultrasonic methods, and enables use of lower NaOCl concentrations without sacrificing efficacy [[PMID:40136729]]. Er,Cr:YSGG at 2,780 nm exploits water and hydroxyapatite absorption to generate photoacoustic streaming, cavitation-like fluid movement, and localised photothermal effects within the root canal system [[PMID:42306383]]. In a direct ex vivo comparison, Nd:YAG (1.0–1.5 W) outperformed Er,Cr:YSGG (1.0–1.5 W) against *E. faecalis* (97–98% vs. 77–96% reduction, p < 0.05) [[PMID:17804322]].
- **Diode (630–1,064 nm, most commonly 810, 940, or 980 nm):** absorbed by haemoglobin, melanin, and pigmented bacteria; soft tissue affinity makes it suited for both direct bactericidal and photodynamic applications. At 940 nm paired with 2,780 nm Er,Cr:YSGG, the dual-laser protocol produced significantly lower mean log₁₀ CFU/mL of aerobic and anaerobic bacteria than conventional 2.5% NaOCl / 17% EDTA in an in vivo RCT (n = 30) [[PMID:38403667]]. However, a 128-tooth ex vivo study found that a diode laser alone — without a photosensitizer — was not more effective than 5.25% NaOCl against free-floating *E. faecalis* [[PMID:28068207]].
- **Antimicrobial blue light (≈410 nm):** an emerging non-laser modality. A micro-LED device (peak 410 nm, 15 mW) reduced planktonic *E. faecalis*, MRSA, and *P. intermedia* at 432, 36, and 1.35 J/cm² respectively, and outperformed calcium hydroxide on established biofilm at 3 and 7 days (n = 10 per group) [[PMID:33103954]]. This recommendation rests on a single ex vivo study of 30 roots (Zhang et al. 2020 [[PMID:33103954]]) — no RCT or prospective cohort has evaluated this device clinically; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.**

Regarding photodynamic therapy (PDT) against *E. faecalis*: a review of 17 studies (Siddiqui et al. 2013 [[PMID:24192536]]) reported that 12 found PDT effective while 4 found conventional irrigation superior, concluding that PDT efficacy "remains questionable." By contrast, Bergmans et al. (n = 38) showed that PAD at 15 J achieved 88.4% kill of *E. faecalis* and 93.8–98.5% kill of other species, though biofilm in deep dentinal layers resisted treatment and complete sterilization was not achieved [[PMID:18081808]]. The more recent narrative review by Plotino et al. 2019 [[PMID:30548497]] concludes that PDT is a promising adjunct, not a replacement for chemomechanical preparation. Current standard of care still places chemomechanical instrumentation and irrigant activation as the primary disinfection strategy.

**Biofilm as the key photobiological challenge**

All laser wavelengths share a fundamental limitation: single-species and polymicrobial biofilms substantially reduce bacterial eradication in deep dentinal layers. Nd:YAG irradiation was effective against planktonic bacteria and thin monolayers but showed substantially reduced efficacy against established *E. faecalis* biofilm at ≥ 2 days incubation [[PMID:16776759]] [[PMID:40565094]]. PAD using a diode laser and toluidine blue showed equivalent biofilm limitation in deep layers [[PMID:18081808]]. No laser system achieves complete canal sterilization — all function as adjuncts to, not replacements for, chemomechanical preparation [[PMID:40565094]] [[PMID:40136729]].

Post-laser canal flushing protocol was not reported in the retrieved evidence base.

## Laser systems, parameters, and irrigation activation techniques

## Module 2 — Laser systems, parameters, and irrigation activation techniques

**Laser Systems in Endodontic Irrigation Activation**

Four laser families dominate endodontic irrigation activation research: erbium lasers (Er:YAG at 2940 nm; Er,Cr:YSGG at 2780 nm), neodymium lasers (Nd:YAG at 1064 nm; Nd:YAP at ~1079 nm), and diode lasers (808–980 nm). Each interacts with root canal anatomy through distinct photothermal and photoacoustic mechanisms, and parameter selection drives outcome differences that are clinically meaningful.

**Erbium Lasers: The Evidence-Preferred Platform**

The highest-tier evidence favours pulsed Er:YAG delivery for irrigant activation. A 2026 meta-analysis of seven RCTs (n = 490 teeth) demonstrated that laser-activated irrigation (LAI) reduced postoperative pain significantly compared to ultrasonically activated irrigation (UAI), with an overall SMD of −0.58 (95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; p =.0016) [[PMID:40818665]]. Subgroup analysis identified the greatest pain reduction at 24–48 hours (SMD = −1.00), specifically with photon-induced photoacoustic streaming (PIPS; SMD = −1.10) and shock wave-enhanced emission photoacoustic streaming (SWEEPS; SMD = −1.57) modalities [[PMID:40818665]]. Diode laser activation showed no significant pain benefit (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]].

Cavitation is the driving force behind LAI efficacy, with growing and imploding vapour bubbles around the laser tip causing intense liquid dynamics throughout the root canal [[PMID:38340037]]. Key parameters influencing cavitation intensity — and therefore cleaning efficacy — include pulse energy, pulse length, repetition frequency, irradiation time, and fibre tip position [[PMID:38340037]] [[PMID:26861988]]. In a resin-block model varying one parameter at a time (n = 20 canals per parameter), shorter pulse lengths (50 µs versus 1000 µs), and tip placement position significantly affected debris removal; fibre tip shape and diameter did not reach significance [[PMID:26861988]].

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Regarding Er:YAG versus UAI for postoperative pain: a pre-registered RCT (NCT03981237; n = 56) using Er:YAG at 50 µs, 20 mJ, 15 Hz for 60 seconds per canal found pain intensity and incidence were significantly higher in the UAI group only at 6 hours (p < 0.05); no significant difference existed at 24, 48, or 72 hours, and analgesic consumption did not differ between groups [[PMID:33607828]]. By contrast, the 2026 meta-analysis pooling seven RCTs found a sustained, clinically meaningful LAI advantage at 24–48 hours [[PMID:40818665]]. The pooled estimate from seven RCTs carries greater statistical weight than the single-trial finding of equivalence beyond 6 hours — treat the meta-analysis as the lead reference for clinical decision-making [[PMID:40818665]].

**PIPS and SWEEPS: Advanced Er:YAG Delivery Modes**

PIPS positions the laser fibre tip at the canal orifice (pulp chamber level), operating at 20 mJ, 50 µs, 15–20 Hz [[PMID:26861988]] [[PMID:28756961]]. A systematic review of 15 studies (2015–2025) confirms that SWEEPS demonstrated the most consistent apical third cleanliness, including significantly improved smear layer removal versus syringe-needle irrigation (p < 0.05), and some studies reported reduced postoperative discomfort [[PMID:40136729]]. In a separate in vitro study (n = 114 single-rooted teeth), SWEEPS applied during root canal preparation alone achieved the highest push-out bond strength values overall and showed no significant difference from dual-stage activation (p > 0.05), suggesting single-stage SWEEPS may be sufficient for substrate optimisation [[PMID:41796933]].

**Er,Cr:YSGG Parameters**

An in vitro study (n = 160 premolars) using Er,Cr:YSGG at 25 mJ, 50 Hz with a radial-firing tip across four 15-second activation cycles with NaOCl + EDTA achieved the lowest smear layer scores across all root thirds, outperforming diode laser, PUI, and conventional syringe-needle irrigation (p < 0.05) [[PMID:41389357]]. Notably, Er,Cr:YSGG activation with saline alone outperformed NaOCl without activation, confirming the dominant role of mechanical cavitation effects over irrigant chemistry alone [[PMID:41389357]].

**Erbium LAI in Retreatment: Clinical Evidence**

A 40-patient RCT of retreatment cases (Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025) in mandibular molars with chronic periapical lesions applied Er:YAG LAI at parameters of 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs pulse duration, through a 300 µm tip diameter, with 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA as irrigants [[PMID:40135760]]. At 6 months, periapical lesion size was significantly smaller in the LAI group (mean 1.55 ± 0.51 mm) versus conventional treatment (1.95 ± 0.71 mm; p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]].

**Diode Lasers: Direct Irradiation Protocol**

Diode lasers (808–980 nm) are absorbed by chromophores in pigmented bacteria, producing photothermal bactericidal effects rather than cavitation-driven irrigant activation. A 30-patient RCT using a 980 nm diode at 1.5 W, pulsed mode, 100 Hz for 20 seconds via a 320 µm diameter fibre in helicoidal movements achieved 42.44% total microbial reduction and 53.14% E. faecalis reduction when applied before chemomechanical preparation, and produced 45% more healed periapical cases at 12 months compared to placebo irradiation (p < 0.05) [[PMID:36823417]]. Critically, diode laser did not demonstrate synergistic benefit when used after chemomechanical preparation in retreatment [[PMID:36823417]].

For endo-perio applications, Munteanu et al. (2025) used a 976 nm diode at 0.75 W, pulsed, 15 Hz via a 200 µm fibre delivering 15 J over 20 seconds per canal for the endodontic phase [[PMID:41470159]]. This recommendation rests on a prospective case series of 12 patients (Munteanu et al. 2025 [[PMID:41470159]]) with no control group — no RCT or prospective cohort has evaluated this multi-stage diode protocol; treat parameters as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**Nd:YAG and Nd:YAP: Adjunctive Parameters**

At 2.0 W, Nd:YAG irradiation produced temperature elevations within biologically tolerable limits while achieving smear layer removal in an in vitro study (n = 144 premolars), though higher energies produced melting and recrystallisation changes at the canal wall [[PMID:19382833]]. Nd:YAP at 280–360 mJ combined with 5.25% NaOCl completely removed E. faecalis biofilm from canal walls and reduced dentinal tubule bacterial counts by 51.5–53.7% compared to 31.9% for NaOCl alone in an in vitro model [[PMID:30069683]]. The Nd:YAG and Nd:YAP evidence base for this module is exclusively in vitro; no clinical trials in the evidence base address periapical healing outcomes for these wavelengths.

**Photodynamic Therapy: Irrigant Combination**

Antimicrobial PDT (aPDT) combines a photosensitiser with a matched laser wavelength to generate reactive oxygen species. A 30-patient primary tooth RCT using methylene blue at 0.005% applied via sterile paper cone for 3 minutes, followed by 660 nm laser at 100 mW, 4 J/cm² for 40 seconds, achieved 99% bacterial reduction versus 93% with conventional treatment alone — a difference that did not reach statistical significance [[PMID:32171875]]. Bordea et al. concluded that aPDT combined with antimicrobial irrigants may offer a synergistic effect, but heterogeneity across studies precludes standardised protocol recommendations [[PMID:31809911]]. Most PDT evidence remains laboratory-based (74.5% of 342 mapped studies) [[PMID:35907620]].

   Place a conical or PIPS-designated tip at the canal orifice / pulp chamber level — not inserted into the apical third. Settings: 20 mJ, 50 µs pulse duration, 15–20 Hz [[PMID:26861988]] [[PMID:28756961]]. Activate for 60 seconds per canal [[PMID:33607828]].

   Use a 300 µm diameter tip; settings 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs pulse duration. Replenish 2% NaOCl between activation cycles [[PMID:40135760]].

5. **Alternate to 17% EDTA and re-activate**
   After NaOCl activation, flush with 17% EDTA and perform a second activation cycle using the same Er:YAG settings. Two-irrigant Er:YAG activation with 17% EDTA and 2.5% NaOCl using a 300 µm fibre demonstrated effective smear layer removal including the apical third [[PMID:23212445]]. Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. used 17% EDTA as the chelating irrigant in their retreatment protocol [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** tooth is a primary RCT case (non-retreatment) with asymptomatic pulpal/periapical pathology
**THEN** use Er:YAG PIPS or SWEEPS at 20 mJ, 50 µs, 15–20 Hz, 60 s per canal with 5% NaOCl at the canal orifice
**BECAUSE** this modality produced the greatest postoperative pain reduction at 24–48 h (SMD −1.00; SWEEPS SMD −1.57) in the highest-tier evidence available [[PMID:40818665]]

**IF** tooth is undergoing retreatment with a chronic periapical lesion
**THEN** use Er:YAG LAI at 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs via 300 µm tip with 2% NaOCl + 17% EDTA
**BECAUSE** this protocol produced significantly smaller periapical lesions at 6 months versus conventional retreatment (1.55 ± 0.51 mm vs 1.95 ± 0.71 mm; p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]]

**IF** diode laser (808–980 nm) is the only available system
**THEN** use as pre-preparation direct irradiation (1.5 W, pulsed, 100 Hz, 20 s, 320 µm fibre in helicoidal motion); do NOT use it as an irrigation activator — diode LAI showed no significant postoperative pain benefit (SMD 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]
**BECAUSE** pre-preparation diode irradiation improved periapical healing with 45% more healed cases at 12 months versus placebo, but diode-mediated irrigation activation provides no measurable clinical advantage over UAI [[PMID:36823417]] [[PMID:40818665]]

**IF** smear layer removal is the primary objective (e.g., post-space preparation, retreatment with bonded restoration planned)
**THEN** use Er,Cr:YSGG at 25 mJ, 50 Hz, four 15-second cycles with NaOCl + EDTA; or SWEEPS Er:YAG applied during root canal preparation
**BECAUSE** Er,Cr:YSGG achieved the lowest smear layer scores across all root thirds versus all comparators (p < 0.05) [[PMID:41389357]], and SWEEPS during root canal preparation alone achieved the highest push-out bond strength with no significant difference from dual-stage activation [[PMID:41796933]]

**IF** endo-perio lesion is refractory to conventional treatment
**THEN** consider 976 nm diode at 0.75 W, pulsed, 15 Hz, 200 µm fibre, 15 J/20 s for the endodontic phase as a provisional adjunct
**BECAUSE** this rests on a prospective case series of 12 patients only (Munteanu et al. 2025 [[PMID:41470159]]); no RCT or controlled cohort has evaluated this multi-stage diode protocol — treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation

**IF** aPDT system is available and adjunctive disinfection is indicated
**THEN** apply methylene blue 0.005% via paper cone for 3 minutes, then activate with 660 nm laser at 100 mW, 4 J/cm² for 40 seconds
**BECAUSE** this protocol achieved 99% bacterial reduction, though the advantage over conventional treatment alone was not statistically significant in the available RCT (n = 30 primary teeth) [[PMID:32171875]]

## Clinical efficacy of laser disinfection against endodontic pathogens

## Module 3 — Clinical Efficacy of Laser Disinfection Against Endodontic Pathogens

**The microbial challenge in root canal disinfection** is defined by a polymicrobial biofilm dominated by obligate anaerobes, facultative species including *Enterococcus faecalis*, and — in approximately 9.1% of infections — fungal co-pathogens, predominantly *Candida* spp. [[PMID:34293029]]. *E. faecalis* is particularly resistant because it forms mature biofilms within dentinal tubules even in canals medicated with calcium hydroxide, with mushroom-shaped biofilm architecture confirmed by confocal laser scanning microscopy after 160 days of calcium hydroxide exposure [[PMID:12398165]]. Eliminating this protected microbial niche is the central problem that laser-assisted techniques attempt to solve.

**Laser-Activated Irrigation (LAI): Efficacy Evidence**

The strongest physical evidence for LAI comes from controlled laboratory models using Er:YAG lasers. In 3D-printed curved root canal models (60° curvature, radius 5 mm, dimension 25/.06) with lateral canals at 2, 5, and 8 mm from the apex, LAI operating at 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 μs with a P400FL tip achieved 89.3% ± 5.9% hydrogel removal from the entire canal system — significantly superior to sonically-activated irrigation EDDY (65.5% ± 3.3%), ultrasonically-activated irrigation (59.1% ± 4.7%), and conventional needle irrigation (p <.05) [[PMID:37586645]]. Critically, LAI removed significantly more debris from lateral canals than all other techniques at all three measured levels [[PMID:37586645]]. When isthmus structures were added to the model, LAI again achieved the greatest overall removal (p <.05), with Er:YAG outperforming ultrasonically-activated irrigation even in apical isthmuses [[PMID:38382735]].

In a combined in situ/in vitro study of 70 human premolars shaped to apical size #20, taper.07, contaminated with oral bacteria for 3 weeks, LAI with pulsed Er:YAG at non-ablative settings for a total of 60 seconds produced significantly more negative bacterial samples than 6% NaOCl alone or ultrasonically-activated irrigation (p <.05), and left significantly less apical biofilm at 1 mm from the apex (p <.05) [[PMID:21689561]]. However, none of the three techniques predictably sterilized the canal, confirming that no single modality achieves complete elimination [[PMID:21689561]].

A systematic review of Er:YAG laser-assisted irrigation confirmed that PIPS and SWEEPS protocols yielded superior bacterial reduction, especially apically, and permitted use of lower NaOCl concentrations without sacrificing efficacy [[PMID:40136729]].

**Antimicrobial Photodynamic Therapy (aPDT): Efficacy Evidence**

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Two PROSPERO-registered systematic reviews with meta-analysis address whether aPDT adds meaningful bacterial reduction beyond chemomechanical debridement alone. Quintana et al. (2023) found that aPDT significantly improved microbial load reduction versus standard protocols (p = 0.04; 95% CI −1.72, −0.05), with subgroup analysis showing superior effect on anaerobic microorganisms (p = 0.003; 95% CI −3.36, −0.69), though certainty of evidence was rated as needing improvement [[PMID:37981598]]. Nunes et al. (2022) similarly found aPDT significantly reduced microbial load in retreatment cases (OR 0.15 [0.07; 0.32], p < 0.0001), with GRADE-rated moderate certainty [[PMID:35840008]]. The earlier systematic review by Chrepa et al. (2014) — limited to 3 clinical studies — reported bacterial reductions of 91.3%–100% but could not pool results due to heterogeneity [[PMID:24935531]]. The higher-quality, larger, more recent reviews consistently favour aPDT as a beneficial adjunct, though neither demonstrates complete sterilization nor superior long-term clinical outcomes.

In clinical practice, aPDT using methylene blue (60 μM aqueous solution) plus 660 nm diode laser (6 minutes, 15 J) applied after conventional periapical surgery achieved a 5-log bacterial reduction versus 3.5-log for surgery alone, with 78% periapical lesion area reduction at 36 months [[PMID:26071387]]. In a 30-patient RCT of retreatment cases, 980 nm diode laser irradiation (320 μm fiber, 1.5 W pulsed, 100 Hz, 20 seconds) achieved 42.44% additional total microbial reduction and 53.14% *E. faecalis* reduction immediately post-irradiation, and improved periapical healing — with 45% more healed cases than placebo at 12 months (p <.05) [[PMID:36823417]].

**Photobiomodulation for Postoperative Pain**

A PRISMA-compliant systematic review of 3 RCTs demonstrated that photobiomodulation therapy (PBMT) significantly reduced postoperative pain in retreatment cases at 24 hours (mean difference 0.65, 95% CI 0.32–0.99; p = 0.0002), 48 hours (MD 0.46, 95% CI 0.05–0.87; p = 0.03), and 72 hours (MD 0.40, 95% CI 0.07–0.74; p = 0.02) [[PMID:37985344]]. In a prospective RCT (n = 40), adding Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs, 300 μm tip fluence 71.4 J/cm²) plus 635 nm PBM (400 mW, 5 seconds per point, 2 J/point, 4 J/cm², 8 mm applicator, at 2 apical points across 4 sessions on the treatment day and after 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h) to conventional retreatment produced significantly smaller periapical lesion size at 6 months (1.55 ± 0.51 mm vs. 1.95 ± 0.71 mm; p <.05) and significantly lower postoperative VAS pain scores [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Establish working length.** Confirm with electronic apex locator verified by periapical radiograph or CBCT. Shape canals to a minimum apical size #20, taper.07 before initiating any laser activation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base; the retrieved evidence uses this preparation as its baseline [[PMID:21689561]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

IF the case is a **primary root canal treatment in a mature permanent tooth with primary infection**
THEN perform Er:YAG LAI (25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 μs, P400FL tip at canal entrance, 3 × 20 s per canal) as the activation modality
BECAUSE LAI achieved 89.3% ± 5.9% debris removal from the entire canal system including lateral canals, significantly superior to all other tested activation methods (p <.05) [[PMID:37586645]]

IF the case is a **root canal retreatment with persistent periapical lesion and confirmed secondary infection**
THEN add aPDT (60 μM methylene blue, 660 nm diode, 15 J) after chemomechanical preparation AND apply postoperative PBM (635 nm, 400 mW, 2 J/point, 4 sessions)
BECAUSE adjunct aPDT achieved OR 0.15 microbial load reduction in retreatment (moderate GRADE certainty) [[PMID:35840008]] and PBM significantly reduced postoperative pain at 24, 48, and 72 hours [[PMID:37985344]]

IF the case involves **complex anatomy (isthmuses, lateral canals, curved roots)**
THEN prioritize Er:YAG LAI over ultrasonic activation for the final irrigation cycle
BECAUSE LAI demonstrated statistically superior removal from lateral canals and apical isthmuses at all measured levels versus UAI (p <.05) in both single-canal and dual-canal isthmus models [[PMID:37586645]] [[PMID:38382735]]

IF **no Er:YAG laser is available** and aPDT alone is considered
THEN use aPDT as an adjunct to standard chemomechanical preparation, not as a replacement for mechanical activation
BECAUSE aPDT improves microbial load reduction (p = 0.04) but no evidence in this base supports it replacing the physical debridement achieved by LAI [[PMID:37981598]]

IF the patient reports **severe postoperative pain after retreatment**
THEN apply PBM (635 nm, 400 mW, 2 J/point, 4 J/cm²) at 24, 48, and 96 hours post-procedure
BECAUSE PBMT reduced postoperative pain significantly versus controls at all three timepoints (MD 0.65 at 24 h, p = 0.0002) [[PMID:37985344]]

## Clinical outcomes, success rates, adverse effects, and complications

## Module 4 — Clinical Outcomes, Success Rates, Adverse Effects, and Complications

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Three high-tier sources address laser adjuncts in endodontic outcomes and reach discordant conclusions depending on the outcome measured. Meire et al., 2023 [[PMID:36156804]] — a PROSPERO-registered systematic review of 14 studies (13 RCTs, GRADE assessed) — concluded there is **insufficient evidence to recommend any adjunctive laser therapy for apical periodontitis**, with very low certainty for diode laser and low certainty for aPDT. Conversely, Sabeti et al., 2026 [[PMID:40818665]] — a meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n = 490 teeth) — demonstrated that laser-activated irrigation (LAI) **significantly reduces postoperative pain** versus ultrasonically activated irrigation (SMD = −0.58; 95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; P = 0.0016). The higher-quality GRADE assessment from Meire et al. [[PMID:36156804]] leads the evidence synthesis on **healing outcomes**; Sabeti et al. [[PMID:40818665]] is the lead citation on **postoperative pain** — these two endpoints must not be conflated.

**Healing of Apical Periodontitis**

Meire et al., 2023 found that meta-analysis was possible for only two UAI studies; no laser modality produced a statistically significant improvement in periapical healing at 12 months [[PMID:36156804]]. Diode laser canal irradiation (three RCTs) and Nd:YAG irradiation (two RCTs) both failed to demonstrate a healing benefit over controls, and the GRADE framework rated diode laser evidence as "very low strength" [[PMID:36156804]]. Conejero et al., 2021 found that periapical radiolucency resolved at 15 ± 9.33 months in the RCT + aPDT group versus 20.35 ± 22.1 months in the RCT-only group (P = 0.07 — not statistically significant), though aPDT significantly reduced the need for calcium hydroxide inter-appointment dressing (16.4% vs. 72.4% in primary cases, P < 0.01) [[PMID:33731503]]. This recommendation rests on a single retrospective cohort of 118 teeth (Conejero et al. 2021 [[PMID:33731503]]) — no RCT or prospective cohort has demonstrated statistically significant improvement in periapical healing attributable to laser adjuncts; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**Postoperative Pain — Where Lasers Show Clear Benefit**

Sabeti et al., 2026 [[PMID:40818665]] reported the greatest LAI pain reduction at 24–48 hours post-treatment (SMD = −1.00), with pulsed Er:YAG platforms showing the strongest modality-specific effect (PIPS: SMD = −1.10; SWEEPS: SMD = −1.57), while diode laser LAI showed no significant pain reduction (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. Jian et al., 2025 (n = 202, RCT) demonstrated that combined intracanal LAI plus external LLLT using an 810 nm semiconductor laser produced the lowest VAS scores through day 7, with the least analgesic consumption (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets vs. significantly more in the control group; P < 0.001) [[PMID:41063319]]. Del Fabbro et al., 2016 found no evidence that low-energy level laser therapy prevented postoperative pain in the surgical retreatment context (very low quality evidence) [[PMID:27759881]], reinforcing that the analgesic benefit demonstrated by Sabeti et al. [[PMID:40818665]] is specific to intracanal LAI during primary treatment.

**Laser-Assisted Pulpotomy: Success Rates**

For primary teeth, Şahin et al., 2025 (n = 81, 12-month RCT) reported Er,Cr:YSGG laser pulpotomy achieving 100% clinical success and 96% radiographic success at 12 months — statistically superior to ferric sulfate (76% radiographic success; P < 0.05) and herbal hemostatic agents (73%) [[PMID:40474353]]. Wang et al., 2022 (n = 100, 24-month RCT) reported Er:YAG laser pulpotomy maintaining 89.58% success at 24 months vs. 82.98% for conventional methods, though this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.386) [[PMID:36261616]]. The Cochrane review by Smaïl-Faugeron et al., 2018 (87 trials, n = 7,140) categorized laser treatment as a potential second-choice pulpotomy technique after MTA while acknowledging insufficient evidence to firmly rank it [[PMID:29852056]]. For permanent teeth, Ayhan et al., 2025 (retrospective cohort, n = 65, 12-month follow-up) found KTP laser hemostasis in MTA pulpotomy achieved 90.5% success — not significantly different from saline (86.4%) or NaOCl (86.4%) (P > 0.05) [[PMID:41121058]].

**Direct Pulp Capping: Laser Enhancement**

Deng et al., 2016 (meta-analysis, 5 studies, 4 laser systems: CO₂, diode, Er,Cr:YSGG, Er:YAG) found laser-assisted direct pulp capping achieved an 89.9% success rate versus 67.2% for controls (RR = 1.35; 95% CI: 1.23–1.49; P < 0.00001; fixed-effects, I² = 0%) [[PMID:27665108]]. Jasrasaria et al., 2025 (n = 42, RCT) showed Er:YAG + Biodentine outperformed Biodentine alone for dentin bridge formation at 12 months (P = 0.01) and reduced postoperative sensitivity (P = 0.03) [[PMID:40407835]].

**Adverse Effects and Safety Profile**

No adverse events were reported at the 12-month follow-up in the regenerative endodontic RCT by Brizuela et al., 2020 [[PMID:32202965]]. Munteanu et al., 2025 (pilot series, n = 12) reported only mild postoperative discomfort by 24 hours post-diode laser treatment, with no adverse events recorded in refractory endo-perio lesions [[PMID:41470159]]. Li et al., 2026 (retrospective cohort, n = 634 teeth) confirmed water laser adjunct therapy met the noninferiority criterion for safety (5% risk-difference margin) versus conventional treatment [[PMID:42482547]]. A specific contraindication: Seyyedi et al., 2024 reported that photobiomodulation should not be applied to teeth with active symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, as one included study demonstrated adverse consequences in this scenario [[PMID:38425318]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**Branch 1 — Inclusion: When to add LAI to standard root canal treatment**
IF patient presents with apical periodontitis in a permanent tooth AND goal is reduction of postoperative pain AND Er:YAG (PIPS/SWEEPS) equipment is available
THEN add LAI using pulsed Er:YAG protocol after chemo-mechanical preparation
BECAUSE LAI significantly reduces postoperative pain vs. UAI (SMD = −0.58; greatest effect at 24–48 h with SWEEPS SMD = −1.57; P = 0.0016) [[PMID:40818665]]

**Branch 2 — Exclusion: When NOT to apply photobiomodulation**
IF tooth is diagnosed with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis AND planned intervention includes photobiomodulation
THEN do NOT apply PBM; proceed with standard endodontic treatment without this adjunct
BECAUSE one study in the Seyyedi et al. review showed adverse consequences of PBM specifically in teeth with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis [[PMID:38425318]]

**Branch 3 — Modification: Diode laser LAI shows no pain benefit**
IF postoperative pain reduction is the clinical goal AND only a diode laser (810–980 nm) is available for intracanal LAI
THEN do not rely on diode LAI for pain reduction; redirect to external LLLT with 810 nm for adjunctive analgesia
BECAUSE subgroup analysis showed diode laser LAI produced SMD = 0.03 (not significant) versus UAI for pain [[PMID:40818665]], while external LLLT at 810 nm reduced analgesic consumption significantly [[PMID:41063319]]

**Branch 4 — Healing expectations: Do not conflate pain and healing outcomes**
IF clinician expects laser adjunct to improve periapical healing rates as well as pain scores
THEN counsel that current evidence does NOT confirm improved radiographic healing with any laser adjunct at 12 months
BECAUSE Meire et al., 2023 found insufficient evidence (GRADE: very low to low) for healing benefit from diode laser, aPDT, Nd:YAG, or Er,Cr:YSGG in apical periodontitis [[PMID:36156804]]

**Branch 5 — Surgical retreatment context**
IF tooth requires surgical retreatment AND adjunctive LLLT is considered for postoperative pain prevention
THEN do NOT substitute LLLT for standard analgesic protocols in the surgical setting
BECAUSE Del Fabbro et al. found no evidence that LLLT prevented postoperative pain in the surgical retreatment context (very low quality evidence) [[PMID:27759881]]

**Branch 6 — Endo-perio lesions with deep pockets**
IF combined endo-perio lesion has baseline probing depth ≥6 mm AND water laser equipment is available
THEN consider water laser as adjunct to conventional endodontic and periodontal therapy
BECAUSE Li et al., 2026 (n = 634) showed composite success risk ratio = 1.22 (95% CI: 1.10–1.36; P = 0.003), with greater benefit specifically observed in teeth with baseline probing depth ≥6 mm [[PMID:42482547]]
