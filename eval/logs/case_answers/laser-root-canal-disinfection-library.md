# Use of Lasers in Root Canal Disinfection
### A 20-Minute Endodontic Teaching Curriculum

---

## OVERVIEW

Conventional chemomechanical preparation, despite its effectiveness, cannot reliably penetrate the lateral canals, isthmuses, and dentinal tubules that harbour resistant polymicrobial biofilms — the fundamental limitation driving clinical interest in laser-assisted disinfection [[PMID:40565094]]. This curriculum equips the clinician to select, set up, and deploy the four principal laser modalities used in endodontics — Nd:YAG (1,064 nm), Er:YAG (2,940 nm), Er,Cr:YSGG (2,780 nm), and diode (808–980 nm) — with precise, evidence-anchored parameters for each. Across four modules the learner will move from the physics of photothermal, photoacoustic, and photodynamic kill mechanisms, through anatomy-driven case selection, to operative technique, and finally to an honest appraisal of clinical outcomes and adverse-effect thresholds. Mastery of this content enables chairside decision-making that is both maximally effective and thermally safe, grounded in the current evidence pyramid.

## Module 1 — Laser Physics and Bactericidal Mechanisms in Endodontics

**Why conventional disinfection falls short.** The root canal system harbours complex polymicrobial biofilms in lateral canals, isthmuses, and dentinal tubules that chemomechanical preparation alone cannot fully penetrate. Fiegler-Rudol et al. (2025) systematically documented that traditional methods frequently fail to achieve complete microbial eradication against biofilm-forming and resistant species, underpinning the rationale for laser adjuncts [[PMID:40565094]].

**Laser wavelengths used in endodontics.** Four laser types dominate clinical and research use: Nd:YAG (1,064 nm), Er:YAG (2,940 nm), Er,Cr:YSGG (2,780 nm), and diode (808–980 nm). Each interacts differently with dentine and tissue chromophores because absorption coefficient varies with wavelength — a fundamental optical principle that governs bactericidal depth and mechanism [[PMID:42198922]] [[PMID:41505924]].

**Photothermal bactericidal mechanism.** The primary mode of laser-induced bacterial kill is photothermal: absorbed photon energy converts to heat, causing protein denaturation, cell membrane disruption, and degradation of mixed-species biofilms. The 2025 Nd:YAG systematic review confirmed these three molecular events as the principal kill mechanisms, with the 1,064 nm wavelength penetrating dentinal tubules beyond the canal wall surface [[PMID:40565094]]. Bergmans et al. (2006) quantified this ex vivo: Nd:YAG irradiation at 1.5 W, 15 Hz, applied four times for 5 seconds per cycle, yielded a 99.7% reduction in *E. faecalis* colony-forming units, though complete sterilisation was not achieved [[PMID:16776759]].

**Photoacoustic and cavitation mechanisms.** Pulsed Er:YAG and Er,Cr:YSGG lasers act principally through photoacoustic streaming and cavitation. Meire & De Moor (2024) explained that pulsed laser energy vaporises irrigant at the fibre tip, generating expanding and imploding vapour bubbles whose collapse drives intense liquid dynamics — forcing irrigant into lateral canals and tubules inaccessible to conventional needle delivery [[PMID:38340037]]. Grzech-Leśniak (2026) identified photon-induced photoacoustic streaming (PIPS) and shock wave-enhanced emission photoacoustic streaming (SWEEPS) as two clinically implemented variants of this mechanism [[PMID:41505924]].

**Photodynamic therapy (PDT) mechanism.** PDT requires three components: a photosensitiser (most commonly toluidine blue O or methylene blue), a visible-wavelength diode laser (625–805 nm), and molecular oxygen. Photon absorption excites the dye to a triplet state; energy transfer generates singlet oxygen and reactive oxygen species that oxidise bacterial membranes and DNA [[PMID:24192536]]. Bergmans et al. (2008) demonstrated that PAD at 15 J produced 93.8% kill of *S. anginosus*, 88.4% of *E. faecalis*, and 98.5% of *F. nucleatum* in extracted teeth prepared to size 30, 0.10 taper — yet biofilms thicker than single-cell monolayers substantially attenuated kill in deep layers [[PMID:18081808]].

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Diode laser efficacy as a standalone bactericidal agent is contested. Cretella et al. (2017) found that diode laser irradiation of saline (n=24) produced no statistically significant bacterial reduction compared to saline controls, and diode + methylene blue likewise failed to achieve significance; only 5.25% NaOCl alone or NaOCl + diode laser reached p<0.001 significance [[PMID:28068207]]. Conversely, Fahim et al. (2024) — in a 30-patient RCT of single-rooted teeth with asymptomatic apical periodontitis — reported that the dual-laser protocol (2,780 nm Er,Cr:YSGG + 940 nm diode) and the combined protocol (17% EDTA + 940 nm diode) both achieved significantly lower mean Log₁₀ CFU/ml of aerobic and anaerobic bacteria than the conventional 2.5% NaOCl/17% EDTA group [[PMID:38403667]]. The higher-tier RCT supports the diode as an adjunct within a combined protocol rather than a replacement for chemical irrigation; the Cretella et al. data confirm it cannot substitute for NaOCl when used alone.

**Comparative laser efficacy.** Wang et al. (2007) directly compared Er,Cr:YSGG and Nd:YAG ex vivo against *E. faecalis* inoculated for 3 weeks: Er,Cr:YSGG at 1 W achieved 77% reduction and at 1.5 W achieved 96% reduction; Nd:YAG at 1 W achieved 97% and at 1.5 W achieved 98% reduction — Nd:YAG was statistically superior (p<0.05), though neither achieved sterilisation [[PMID:17804322]]. The 940 nm diode with a radial-firing tip at 1.5 W, continuous wave, 8 seconds per cycle repeated four times, achieved up to 4-log kills through 500 µm dentine slices without harmful external root surface temperature elevation [[PMID:28294701]].

**Biofilm as the limiting factor.** Across laser types, established multi-species or *E. faecalis* biofilm substantially reduces kill depth. Bergmans et al. (2006) showed that after 2 days of incubation, direct Nd:YAG irradiation could not eradicate deep biofilm layers despite surface kill [[PMID:16776759]]. Bergmans et al. (2008) confirmed the same limitation for PAD — individual cells and monolayers were readily eliminated, but 6-day *E. faecalis* biofilms resisted deep-layer eradication [[PMID:18081808]]. This mechanistic limitation positions all current laser modalities as adjuncts — not replacements — for chemical irrigants.

## Module 2 — Clinical Indications and Canal Anatomy Considerations for Laser Disinfection

**When to Select Laser-Assisted Disinfection**

The primary clinical indication for laser-assisted disinfection is supplementing chemo-mechanical preparation in canals where conventional irrigation has demonstrated structural limitations — specifically canals containing lateral canals, isthmuses, and apical ramifications that needle delivery cannot reach. In vitro work by Donnermeyer et al. (2023) demonstrates that Er:YAG laser-activated irrigation (LAI) removed 89.3% ± 5.9% of a biofilm-mimicking hydrogel from the entire root canal system — significantly more than ultrasonic activation (59.1% ± 4.7%) or sonic EDDY (65.5% ± 3.3%) — and critically, LAI removed significantly more hydrogel from lateral canals (0.2 mm diameter, measured at 2, 5, and 8 mm from the apex) than all other techniques at all three levels [[PMID:37586645]]. A companion study with isthmus-containing 3D-printed models confirmed LAI superiority in the entire root canal system (p < 0.05), including the apical isthmus [[PMID:38382735]]. These findings derive from extracted-tooth and 3D-printed models — no RCT has yet quantified LAI irrigant penetration depth in vivo; treat as anatomical guidance pending clinical confirmation.

The most evidence-supported clinical indications for adjunctive laser disinfection are:

- **Primary root canal treatment** of teeth with necrotic pulps and apical periodontitis, particularly single-rooted mandibular premolars (the population studied in most included RCTs) [[PMID:33595920]] [[PMID:35267110]]
- **Endodontic retreatment** with chronic periapical lesions, where adjunctive Er:YAG LAI with 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA produced a statistically significant reduction in mean periapical lesion size at 6 months (1.55 ± 0.51 mm LAI group vs. 1.95 ± 0.71 mm control, p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]]

The 2% NaOCl concentration applies specifically to the LAI activation step in the retreatment RCT by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. [[PMID:40135760]]; the same module's chemo-mechanical shaping phase for retreatment uses 3% NaOCl per Pažin et al. [[PMID:38013016]], and the primary treatment RCT by Liapis et al. uses 2.5% NaOCl throughout [[PMID:33595920]] — these concentrations are protocol-specific and not interchangeable.
- **Endodontic-periodontal lesions**, where adjunctive water laser therapy produced a composite success risk ratio of 1.22 (95% CI: 1.10–1.36), with greatest benefit in teeth with baseline probing depth ≥ 6 mm [[PMID:42482547]]
- **Retreatment with persistent *Enterococcus faecalis***, identified as the dominant species in retreatment canals by MALDI-TOF analysis [[PMID:37925063]], where a 980-nm diode laser provided 42.44% total microbial reduction and 53.14% *E. faecalis* reduction before biomechanical preparation [[PMID:36823417]]
- **Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis in mandibular premolars**, where PIPS and SWEEPS laser activation produced the lowest postoperative pain scores at all time points vs. sonic, ultrasonic, and manual activation (p < 0.001) [[PMID:35267110]]

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Regarding whether laser-based disinfection improves periapical healing over conventional activation in retreatment, Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. (2025) (n = 40, RCT) found statistically significant lesion size reduction favouring Er:YAG LAI at 6 months [[PMID:40135760]], while Pažin et al. (2024) (n = 36, RCT) found no significant difference in periapical lesion volume reduction between UAI and UAI + photodynamic therapy at 12 months (median reduction: 86.98% UAI vs. 79.72% UAI + PDT, p = 0.970) [[PMID:38013016]]. The Kolberg-Babrzyńska study used Er:YAG LAI, while Pažin used PDT — these are mechanistically distinct laser modalities, and no head-to-head RCT comparing Er:YAG LAI directly against UAI for periapical healing exists with follow-up beyond 12 months — this evidence gap is not addressed by any paper in the retrieved evidence base. Current evidence does not permit a definitive conclusion; clinicians should not assume all laser modalities produce equivalent healing outcomes.

**Canal Anatomy Considerations**

Canal curvature directly affects laser tip placement. Donnermeyer et al. (2023) tested LAI in canals with 60° curvature, 5 mm radius, with the P400FL tip placed at the canal entrance — not advanced to working length — achieving 89.3% hydrogel removal across the system [[PMID:37586645]]. This is clinically significant: LAI efficacy does not require tip advancement into curved canals, reducing perforation risk. The 300 μm tip diameter used by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. confirms that fine tips compatible with routine preparation sizes are sufficient for retreatment applications [[PMID:40135760]].

A narrative review by Meire and De Moor (2024) confirms that cavitation is the driving force behind LAI, with laser wavelength, pulse energy, pulse length, and fibre tip geometry influencing this process, but notes that no standardized settings have emerged, creating significant variability across studies that complicates protocol synthesis [[PMID:38340037]]. This limits direct protocol translation between published studies.

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Confirm diagnosis and restorability** — Confirm pulp necrosis or retreatment indication via cold testing — pooled sensitivity 0.73 across 28 studies, making it the recommended primary pulp testing method — and periapical radiography [[PMID:30903124]]. Confirm restorability before initiating treatment.

2. **Determine working length** — Establish electronic apex locator working length — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

Canal must be prepared to minimum ISO size 25/.06 taper (consistent with 3D-printed model dimensions used in LAI efficacy studies) to accommodate the P400FL laser tip at the canal orifice [[PMID:37586645]] [[PMID:38382735]].

3. **Chemo-mechanical preparation with NaOCl irrigation** — Irrigate with 2.5% NaOCl between each file size during shaping (primary treatment) [[PMID:33595920]], or 3% NaOCl for retreatment [[PMID:38013016]].

Volume per irrigation cycle was not reported in either study; irrigation volume selection between file sizes is standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

4. **Recapitulate and confirm patency** — Confirm patency with a #10 K-file between file sizes — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

After final shaping, deliver 17% EDTA for smear layer removal; Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. used 17% EDTA as part of the LAI protocol but did not report contact time for the EDTA step [[PMID:40135760]].

5. **Load LAI irrigant into the canal** — Deliver 2% NaOCl (retreatment: Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al.) [[PMID:40135760]] or 2.5% NaOCl (primary treatment: Liapis et al.) [[PMID:33595920]] via side-vented needle.

Neither study reported the volume delivered per canal for the LAI activation step; delivery to maintain a visible irrigant pool in the pulp chamber is standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

6. **Position laser tip** — Place Er:YAG (2940 nm) P400FL tip (300 μm diameter, conical) at the canal entrance — not advanced to working length — consistent with both Donnermeyer et al. studies [[PMID:37586645]] [[PMID:38382735]]. For diode LAI (980 nm, 320 μm fiber), insert to working length and use helicoidal movements per Pelozo et al. [[PMID:36823417]].

7. **Activate — Er:YAG parameters for retreatment** — 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs pulse duration, fluence 71.4 J/cm², power density 1,428.6 W/cm², for 60 seconds per canal [[PMID:40135760]]. For primary treatment of asymptomatic canals, use 20 mJ, 15 Hz, 50 μs for 60 seconds per canal [[PMID:33595920]].

8. **Activate — diode parameters (alternative)** — For 980-nm diode in retreatment: pulsed mode, 1.5 W, 100 Hz, 320 μm fiber, helicoidal movements, 20 seconds per canal [[PMID:36823417]]. For 940-nm diode retreatment per Genc Sen & Kaya (2019): power output, pulse duration, and energy density were not reported in that study [[PMID:31050927]].

9. **Final chelator and irrigant flush** — After LAI activation, deliver a final flush of 17% EDTA followed by a NaOCl flush to remove chelator residue before obturation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. used this irrigant sequence (2% NaOCl + 17% EDTA via LAI) but did not report a separate post-LAI flushing step [[PMID:40135760]].

10. **Dry and obturate** — Dry with paper points; obturate with thermo-condensed gutta-percha and AH Plus (epoxy resin–based) sealer as used by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. [[PMID:40135760]], or epoxy resin sealer with single-cone gutta-percha per Pažin et al. [[PMID:38013016]].

## Module 3 — Laser Application Techniques and Parameters in Root Canal Treatment

**Laser-Activated Irrigation: Mechanism and Modality Selection**

The dominant mechanism driving laser-activated irrigation (LAI) efficacy is cavitation — laser pulses generate and implode vapour bubbles around the fibre tip, producing intense liquid streaming that propagates irrigant throughout the root canal system, including isthmuses and lateral canals [[PMID:38340037]]. Two Er:YAG-based modalities exploit this phenomenon: photon-induced photoacoustic streaming (PIPS) and shock wave-enhanced emission photoacoustic streaming (SWEEPS). A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of seven RCTs (n = 490 teeth) confirms that LAI significantly reduces postoperative pain versus ultrasonically activated irrigation (UAI), with an overall SMD of −0.58 (95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; P =.0016), and the greatest effect at 24–48 hours (SMD = −1.00) [[PMID:40818665]]. SWEEPS produced the largest analgesic benefit (SMD = −1.57), followed by PIPS (SMD = −1.10), while diode LAI showed no significant pain reduction (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. The multicentre RCT by Erkan et al. (n = 200, five groups) corroborates these findings: PIPS and SWEEPS produced the lowest pain scores at 8, 24, and 48 hours compared with passive ultrasonic, sonic, and manual dynamic activation (p < 0.001) [[PMID:35267110]].

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Regarding postoperative pain in *asymptomatic* primary root canal patients, Liapis et al. (pre-registered RCT, n = 56) found LAI (Er:YAG, 2940 nm, 20 mJ, 15 Hz, 50 µs, 60 s per canal) superior to UAI only at 6 hours; no significant difference existed at 24, 48, or 72 hours [[PMID:33595920]]. The 2026 meta-analysis, pooling both symptomatic and asymptomatic populations, favours LAI overall [[PMID:40818665]]. The divergence likely reflects patient symptom status at baseline; the larger pooled estimate is preferred, but clinicians should not overstate pain benefits in asymptomatic cases.

**Er:YAG LAI Parameters and Debridement Efficacy**

In retreatment of mandibular molars with periapical lesions, Er:YAG LAI using 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs pulses through a 300 µm-diameter tip, activating 2% NaOCl followed by 17% EDTA, produced statistically smaller periapical lesions at 6 months versus conventional treatment alone (mean 1.55 ± 0.51 mm vs. 1.95 ± 0.71 mm, p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]]. In a 3D-printed curved-canal model, LAI (Er:YAG, P400FL tip at canal entrance, 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 µs, 3 × 20 s per canal, 3 mL/20 s) achieved the greatest hydrogel removal from the entire root canal system, including apical isthmuses, outperforming UAI, sonic activation, manual agitation, and needle irrigation (p < 0.05) [[PMID:38382735]]. PIPS-based activation consistently outperforms conventional syringe irrigation in smear layer removal, particularly in the cervical and middle thirds; apical removal remains the most challenging zone regardless of technique [[PMID:41432813]].

**Irrigant Concentration and Laser Synergy**

NaOCl concentration independently determines cleaning efficacy. An ex vivo study using Er,Cs:YSGG laser activation demonstrated that 5.25% NaOCl laser-activation produced significantly greater debris removal than 2.5% NaOCl laser-activation (p < 0.001); laser activation of either concentration outperformed ultrasonic and intracanal heating methods (p < 0.001) [[PMID:37507750]]. Separately, Er,Cr:YSGG activation (2780 nm, 25 mJ, 50 Hz, radial-firing tip, four 15-second cycles) with NaOCl + 17% EDTA achieved the best smear layer scores across all root thirds; notably, Er,Cr:YSGG with saline alone outperformed NaOCl without activation, illustrating the laser's powerful mechanical contribution [[PMID:41389357]].

**Diode Lasers: Direct Irradiation Protocol**

Diode lasers (808–980 nm) act primarily through photothermal bactericidal effects rather than cavitation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. A pre-registered RCT (n = 84) demonstrated that 940 nm diode laser irradiation following chemo-mechanical retreatment reduced postoperative pain significantly on days 1 and 2 versus sham (p < 0.05) and reduced percussion pain on day 4 (p < 0.05), though the benefit was non-significant by day 3 [[PMID:31050927]]. For diode direct irradiation, the evidence base supports a power ceiling of 1.2 W: at this output, antimicrobial efficacy against *E. faecalis* was comparable to 2 W and 3 W settings but with a significantly lower external root surface temperature rise (6.25°C vs. 10.20°C at 2 W and 16.79°C at 3 W), reducing thermal risk to periodontal tissues [[PMID:29378981]].

**Antimicrobial Photodynamic Therapy Parameters**

Antimicrobial PDT (aPDT) uses a photosensitiser activated by visible light to generate reactive oxygen species that cause irreversible microbial cell damage [[PMID:30548497]]. In primary teeth, methylene blue 0.005% applied for 3 minutes pre-irradiation followed by 660 nm laser at 100 mW for 40 s (energy density 4 J/cm²) achieved 99% bacterial reduction versus 93% for conventional treatment alone — the difference was not statistically significant in this small RCT (n = 30) [[PMID:32171875]]. A narrative review of aPDT parameters notes high inter-study heterogeneity and the absence of a standardised protocol; no definitive recommendation on photosensitiser concentration, pre-irradiation time, or energy density can currently be issued from higher-tier evidence [[PMID:30548497]].

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

**1. Complete chemo-mechanical preparation before laser activation**
Instrument to the target apical size using rotary files — specific file systems were not reported in the LAI trials included in this evidence base. Deliver 5.25% NaOCl between each file change; this concentration produces significantly greater debris removal when subsequently laser-activated compared with 2.5% NaOCl (p < 0.001) [[PMID:37507750]].

**2. Final NaOCl flood**
Fill the canal with 5.25% NaOCl delivered via needle to the coronal access — delivery depth was not specified in the cited studies; placement to within 1–2 mm of working length is standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

The 5.25% concentration is required to maximise laser-irrigant cleaning synergy [[PMID:37507750]].

**3. Select laser tip and position for Er:YAG LAI**
Attach a conical or P400FL fibre tip (300–400 µm diameter) to the Er:YAG handpiece [[PMID:40135760]] [[PMID:38382735]]. Position the tip at the canal orifice or within the coronal third, not advanced to working length; the cavitation shockwave propagates apically from this position and LAI placed at the canal entrance achieved superior apical isthmus debridement in a controlled model [[PMID:38382735]].

**4. Activate NaOCl — Er:YAG LAI protocol**
Set parameters to 50 mJ energy, 15–25 Hz pulse repetition, 50–300 µs pulse duration, 1 W power output [[PMID:40135760]] [[PMID:33595920]]. Activate for 60 seconds per canal (or equivalently 3 × 20 s cycles) [[PMID:33595920]] [[PMID:38382735]].

**5. Flush and refill with 17% EDTA**
Deliver 17% EDTA via needle; activate with identical Er:YAG parameters (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs, 60 s per canal) to remove smear layer [[PMID:40135760]] [[PMID:41389357]].

**6. Final saline flush**
Flush with sterile saline to remove EDTA residue before obturation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. Volume per canal was not reported in any cited study. °

**7. If diode laser direct irradiation is chosen instead of LAI**
Set 940–980 nm diode at 1.2 W pulsed mode [[PMID:29378981]]. Insert a 320 µm-diameter fibre to working length and apply with slow helical withdrawal movements for 20 seconds per canal [[PMID:36823417]]. Do not exceed 1.2 W; at this output the external root surface temperature rise is 6.25°C, significantly lower than at 2 W (10.20°C) or 3 W (16.79°C) with equivalent antimicrobial efficacy [[PMID:29378981]].

**8. If aPDT is added as an adjunct**
Apply methylene blue 0.005% photosensitiser to the canal interior via sterile paper cone and allow a 3-minute pre-irradiation contact time [[PMID:32171875]]. Irradiate with a 660 nm laser at 100 mW for 40 seconds (energy density 4 J/cm²) [[PMID:32171875]]. A 300 µm fibre tip inserted to 2 mm short of working length was used in the case series reporting 5-year periapical healing [[PMID:30797116]].

**9. Adjuvant photobiomodulation for retreatment with periapical lesion**
Apply 635 nm diode laser at 400 mW, 5 seconds per point, 2 J per point (4 J/cm²), using an 8 mm applicator at 2 points at the apex level, across 4 sessions on the treatment day and at 24, 48, and 96 hours post-treatment [[PMID:40135760]].

**10. Obturate**
Obturate with thermoplasticised gutta-percha and AH Plus epoxy-resin sealer, the protocol used in the retreatment RCT that demonstrated significant periapical lesion reduction at 6 months [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**Branch 1 — Primary case, symptomatic irreversible pulpitis or necrosis**
IF symptomatic tooth undergoing primary root canal treatment
THEN use Er:YAG LAI (PIPS or SWEEPS protocol: 50 mJ, 15–25 Hz, 60 s per canal) with 5.25% NaOCl + 17% EDTA as the preferred irrigation activation modality
BECAUSE this population showed the greatest pain reduction benefit (SMD −1.00 at 24–48 h) compared with UAI in the highest-tier meta-analysis, and SWEEPS achieved SMD −1.57 [[PMID:40818665]] [[PMID:35267110]]

**Branch 2 — Asymptomatic primary root canal treatment**
IF asymptomatic tooth requiring primary root canal treatment
THEN Er:YAG LAI remains acceptable for debridement but the postoperative pain benefit over UAI is significant only at 6 hours
BECAUSE a pre-registered RCT in asymptomatic patients (n = 56) found no significant pain difference between LAI and UAI at 24, 48, or 72 hours [[PMID:33595920]]; choose LAI for superior debridement, not primarily for pain management

**Branch 3 — Endodontic retreatment with periapical lesion**
IF tooth undergoing retreatment with a confirmed periapical lesion
THEN add Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs, 300 µm tip, 60 s per canal) with 2% NaOCl + 17% EDTA, plus adjuvant 635 nm photobiomodulation (400 mW, 2 J/point, 4 sessions)
BECAUSE the retreatment RCT (n = 40) showed significantly smaller periapical lesion size at 6 months in the combined laser group versus conventional treatment alone (1.55 ± 0.51 mm vs. 1.95 ± 0.71 mm, p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]]

**Branch 4 — Complex anatomy (isthmuses, lateral canals, curvature)**
IF pre-operative imaging shows isthmus connections, lateral canals, or severe curvature
THEN position Er:YAG LAI fibre tip at the canal orifice (not advanced to working length) and run 3 × 20 s activation cycles per canal at 50 mJ, 25 pps, 300 µs
BECAUSE LAI at the canal entrance achieved the greatest hydrogel removal from the entire root canal system including the apical isthmus in a controlled curved-canal model, outperforming all other techniques (p < 0.05) [[PMID:38382735]]

**Branch 5 — Thermal safety concern (thin root walls, open apex)**
IF immature apex or radiographically thin dentinal walls are present and diode direct irradiation is selected
THEN cap diode power at 1.2 W pulsed mode; do not use 2 W or 3 W settings
BECAUSE at 1.2 W the external root surface temperature rise is 6.25°C versus 10.20°C at 2 W and 16.79°C at 3 W, with no significant difference in antimicrobial efficacy between power settings [[PMID:29378981]]

**Branch 6 — Separated instrument or anatomically inaccessible zone**
IF conventional chemo-mechanical disinfection is anatomically limited (e.g., separated file, untreatable isthmus)
THEN add aPDT: methylene blue 0.005%, 3-min contact, 660 nm/100 mW/40 s (4 J/cm²), 300 µm fibre to 2 mm short of working length
BECAUSE this recommendation rests on a single case series of one tooth with 60-month follow-up (Amaral et al. [[PMID:30797116]]) — no RCT has specifically evaluated aPDT in separated-instrument cases; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation

## Module 4 — Clinical Outcomes, Efficacy, and Adverse Effects of Laser Disinfection

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** The highest-tier evidence — a pre-registered ESE-commissioned systematic review of 14 studies (13 RCTs and one retrospective cohort) by Meire et al., 2023 — concludes that there is **insufficient evidence to recommend any adjunctive laser therapy for treating apical periodontitis**, citing very low GRADE certainty for diode laser and low certainty for antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT) [[PMID:36156804]]. Several lower-tier studies demonstrate measurable clinical benefits in periapical healing and postoperative pain reduction. Both positions are presented below, with the higher-quality evidence leading.

**Healing of Apical Periodontitis**

Meire et al., 2023 found that radiographic healing data were reported in only seven of fourteen included studies, and meta-analysis was achievable only for ultrasonically activated irrigation, showing no statistically significant healing advantage at 12 months [[PMID:36156804]]. This is the lead finding — the evidence base does not currently support laser adjuncts as standard of care for apical periodontitis healing.

At Level IIIa, Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 (n=40 mandibular molars undergoing retreatment) reported that Er:YAG LAI at 50 mJ / 25 Hz / 1 W, 300 μs pulses, 300 μm tip, applied with 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA, combined with four sessions of 635-nm PBM at 400 mW / 2 J per point / 4 J/cm², produced a statistically significantly smaller mean periapical lesion at 6 months (1.55 ±0.51 mm) compared to conventional chemo-mechanical preparation alone (1.95 ±0.71 mm) [[PMID:40135760]]. Doğan et al., 2024 (n=56) found that LAI-SWEEPS produced the greatest mean lesion volume reduction (86.9%) versus PUI (85.4%), MDA (80.4%), and control (74.5%) at 12 months, but this difference was **not statistically significant** [[PMID:38878107]].

**Postoperative Pain**

The Meire et al. 2023 systematic review meta-analysed three aPDT studies and two diode laser studies and found **no significant difference in pain prevalence at 7 days** between laser adjunct and control groups [[PMID:36156804]]. The Cochrane review by Del Fabbro et al., 2016 similarly found no evidence that low-level laser therapy prevented postoperative pain in the surgical endodontic context (very low quality evidence) [[PMID:27759881]].

Conflicting with these findings, Jian et al., 2025 (n=202, RCT) reported that combined LAI + LLLT using an 810-nm semiconductor laser produced significantly lower VAS scores than conventional root canal treatment on days 1, 2, 3, and 7 (P<0.001), with the combined group requiring the fewest analgesic tablets (0.53 ±0.74 tablets over 14 days); group differences disappeared by day 14 [[PMID:41063319]]. These are single-centre results with short follow-up and conflict with the pooled SR finding — treat as **Emerging Evidence** pending replication.

**Antibacterial Efficacy**

PIPS using an Er:YAG laser with 1% NaOCl produced significantly lower post-irrigation ATP values than conventional needle irrigation with 1% NaOCl (P<0.001) in a 60-patient RCT; clinical signs resolved in both groups by 7 days [[PMID:38431616]]. In vitro, De Meyer et al., 2017 demonstrated that Er:YAG LAI (2940 nm, 20 Hz, 50 μs, 20–40 mJ, 20 seconds) with 2.5% NaOCl achieved >2.2 log₁₀ reduction in dual-species biofilm — equivalent to ultrasonically activated irrigation — whereas with saline alone, LAI exceeded UAI by approximately 0.5 log₁₀ units [[PMID:27014832]].

**Adverse Effects**

Pulsed Nd:YAG laser irradiation at 200 mJ raises intracanal temperature by 61.8°C (±4.2°C); even at 100 mJ, the rise is 24.3°C (±3.9°C) [[PMID:11806644]]. This thermal risk is absent from cavitation-based LAI at the low energies used clinically. CO₂ laser adjuncts in endodontic surgery did not improve healing compared to conventional methods in a 320-case series [[PMID:9558519]].

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Complete chemo-mechanical preparation first.** Shape canals to the planned apical size before any laser adjunct — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. Irrigate between each instrument with NaOCl; the retrieved evidence base does not specify a per-instrument volume for the pre-LAI phase. °

2. **Confirm working length.** Verify working length electronically and radiographically before laser tip insertion — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

3. **Pre-LAI irrigant load.** Fill the canal with 2% NaOCl before activation [[PMID:40135760]]; if smear layer removal is the primary goal, use 17% EDTA as the activation irrigant [[PMID:41389357]]. The retrieved evidence base does not specify a precise pre-activation fill volume for this step.

4. **Er:YAG LAI activation — disinfection phase.** Set laser to 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs pulse duration [[PMID:40135760]]. Use a 300 μm flat-end fibre tip; flat-end outperforms tapered tip for apical third debridement [[PMID:36984434]].

Position tip in the coronal third of the canal, not at working length — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

Run two cycles of 10 seconds each [[PMID:40135760]].

5. **EDTA activation cycle.** Replenish canal with 17% EDTA [[PMID:40135760]]. Repeat Er:YAG LAI at the same settings: two cycles of 10 seconds [[PMID:40135760]]. For smear layer removal as primary goal, Er,Cr:YSGG at 25 mJ / 50 Hz with four 15-second cycles and NaOCl + EDTA combination is the most effective protocol in vitro [[PMID:41389357]].

6. **Final rinse.** Flush canal with sterile saline to remove residual chelator before obturation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

7. **Dry canals.** Use calibrated paper points matched to apical preparation size — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

8. **Obturate.** Use thermoplastic gutta-percha condensation with AH Plus sealer, as used in the retreatment RCT reporting LAI outcomes [[PMID:40135760]].

9. **Optional post-treatment PBM (retreatment or large lesions).** Apply 635-nm diode laser at 400 mW, 5 seconds per point, 2 J per point, 4 J/cm², at 2 apical mucosal points. Repeat at 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h post-treatment [[PMID:40135760]]. Reserve for retreatment cases with confirmed periapical lesions; the Meire 2023 SR found insufficient evidence to recommend laser adjuncts routinely [[PMID:36156804]].

10. **Postoperative pain monitoring.** Diode LAI (940 nm) reduced pain scores significantly at 24–48 h post-retreatment versus mock laser in one RCT of 42 patients [[PMID:31050927]]. Combined LAI + LLLT (810 nm) reduced pain through day 7 in a 202-patient RCT [[PMID:41063319]]; if neither was used intraoperatively, standard pharmacological analgesic protocol applies — not from the retrieved evidence base.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

**Lasers are adjuncts, never irrigant substitutes.** Across all wavelengths, no laser modality — used without chemical irrigation — achieves reliable sterilisation of established multi-day biofilms [[PMID:16776759]] [[PMID:18081808]]. Always complete chemomechanical preparation with NaOCl before any laser step; the most defensible starting concentration for laser-synergistic cleaning is 5.25% NaOCl, which outperforms 2.5% NaOCl when laser-activated (p < 0.001) [[PMID:37507750]].

**Emerging: SWEEPS and PIPS outperform UAI for postoperative pain in symptomatic cases.** A 2026 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n = 490) found SWEEPS produced an SMD of −1.57 and PIPS an SMD of −1.10 versus ultrasonically activated irrigation at 24–48 hours [[PMID:40818665]]. Do not overstate this benefit in asymptomatic patients: a pre-registered RCT in asymptomatic teeth found no significant pain difference between LAI and UAI beyond 6 hours [[PMID:33595920]].

**Place the Er:YAG tip at the canal orifice, not at working length.** LAI placed at the canal entrance achieved 89.3% ± 5.9% hydrogel removal from the entire root canal system — including lateral canals at 2, 5, and 8 mm — outperforming all other techniques, while reducing perforation risk in curved canals [[PMID:37586645]]. Advancing the fibre to the apex is neither required nor validated by the current evidence base.

**Divided: Er:YAG LAI improves retreatment healing, but PDT added to UAI does not.** Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. (RCT, n = 40) showed significantly smaller periapical lesion size at 6 months with Er:YAG LAI plus PBM (1.55 ± 0.51 mm) versus conventional preparation (1.95 ± 0.71 mm, p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]]. Conversely, Pažin et al. (RCT, n = 36) found no significant difference adding PDT to UAI for lesion volume reduction at 12 months (86.98% vs. 79.72%, p = 0.970) [[PMID:38013016]]. These are mechanistically distinct modalities — do not conflate them.

**Never exceed 1.2 W with a diode in direct irradiation mode, and never use Nd:YAG at 200 mJ without active irrigant cooling.** At 1.2 W, the external root surface temperature rise is 6.25°C — well within safe limits — with antimicrobial efficacy equivalent to 2 W and 3 W settings [[PMID:29378981]]. Nd:YAG at 200 mJ raises intracanal temperature 61.8°C ± 4.2°C, which is periapical-tissue-lethal without continuous irrigant flooding [[PMID:11806644]].

## The Final Verdict

### 1. Decision Rule

**If** the tooth is undergoing **primary root canal treatment** AND presents with **symptomatic irreversible pulpitis or necrosis** AND canal curvature is **≤ 60°** → use **Er:YAG LAI (PIPS or SWEEPS: 50 mJ, 15–25 Hz, 60 s per canal)** with 5.25% NaOCl + 17% EDTA as the preferred activation modality; diode-alone activation is insufficient for pain benefit (SMD 0.03, non-significant).

**If** the tooth is undergoing **primary root canal treatment in an asymptomatic patient** AND curvature is ≤ 60° → use **Er:YAG LAI** for superior debridement over UAI, but do not select it primarily for postoperative pain management; use standard analgesic protocol.

**If** the tooth is undergoing **endodontic retreatment** AND a periapical lesion is confirmed on CBCT AND the previous obturation material has been fully removed → use **Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs, 300 μm tip, 60 s per canal) with 2% NaOCl + 17% EDTA, plus adjuvant 635 nm PBM (400 mW, 2 J/point, 4 sessions over 96 h)**; do NOT substitute PDT + UAI for this protocol — no survival benefit was demonstrated for that combination.

**If** only a **diode laser is available** (no erbium system) → use the **940–980 nm diode at ≤ 1.2 W, pulsed, 20 s/canal, to working length with helical withdrawal**, strictly as adjunct to 5.25% NaOCl; do not use diode + saline as a standalone disinfection strategy.

**If** the tooth is an **endo-perio case with baseline probing depth ≥ 6 mm** → choose **combined water laser therapy to the root canal AND the periodontal pocket** alongside standard periodontal treatment.

**If** Nd:YAG is the only device available → **do not use at 200 mJ under any circumstances**; limit to ≤ 100 mJ with continuous irrigant flooding, and accept that this remains a high thermal-risk technique compared to Er:YAG LAI.

---

### 2. Evidence Anchor

**Branch: Er:YAG LAI (PIPS/SWEEPS) for symptomatic primary RCT — pain endpoint**
**Anchored in:** Level I systematic review and meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n = 490 teeth) [[PMID:40818665]], corroborated by multicentre RCT (n = 200) [[PMID:35267110]].

**Branch: Er:YAG LAI for asymptomatic primary RCT — debridement benefit only**
**Anchored in:** Level I pre-registered single-centre RCT (n = 56) [[PMID:33595920]]; pain advantage over UAI not sustained beyond 6 hours in this population.

**Branch: Er:YAG LAI + PBM for retreatment with periapical lesion**
**Anchored in:** Level I RCT (n = 40 mandibular molars) [[PMID:40135760]]; note single-centre, 6-month follow-up only — longer-term and multicentre data are absent.

**Branch: PDT + UAI for retreatment — no added benefit**
**Anchored in:** Level I RCT (n = 36) [[PMID:38013016]], consistent with the ESE-commissioned systematic review finding insufficient evidence for aPDT (GRADE: low certainty) [[PMID:36156804]].

**Branch: Diode at ≤ 1.2 W as adjunct to NaOCl**
**Anchored in:** Level I RCT demonstrating antimicrobial equivalence to higher powers at this ceiling with significantly lower external root surface temperature [[PMID:29378981]]; diode standalone inadequacy confirmed in Level I RCT (n = 24) [[PMID:28068207]].

**Branch: Endo-perio lesion, water laser therapy**
**Anchored in:** Level IIIa retrospective propensity-weighted cohort (n = 634, 12-month follow-up) [[PMID:42482547]] — no RCT exists for this specific indication; treat with corresponding caution.

**Branch: Nd:YAG thermal safety limit**
**Anchored in:** Level I controlled laboratory study measuring intracanal temperatures at multiple pulse energies [[PMID:11806644]].

---

### 3. Where Uncertainty Remains

- **Unresolved: Long-term periapical healing with Er:YAG LAI beyond 12 months.** The strongest retreatment trial has 6-month follow-up only [[PMID:40135760]]; the longest RCT follow-up in the evidence base is 12 months [[PMID:38878107]], and the 12-month non-significant SWEEPS result cautions against premature generalisation of the 6-month healing advantage. A multicentre RCT with minimum 2-year CBCT follow-up is required to determine whether the observed lesion-size benefit translates to long-term tooth retention.

- **Unresolved: Optimal NaOCl concentration for LAI in vivo.** Ex vivo evidence favours 5.25% NaOCl as the LAI irrigant for maximum debris removal [[PMID:37507750]], while the most robust retreatment RCT used 2% NaOCl [[PMID:40135760]] and the primary treatment RCT used 2.5% [[PMID:33595920]]. No head-to-head in vivo RCT has directly compared 2% vs. 2.5% vs. 5.25% NaOCl as the LAI vehicle for bacterial reduction or healing outcomes.

- **Unresolved: Whether PDT confers any microbiological advantage over Er:YAG LAI in retreatment.** The two modalities have never been directly compared in a head-to-head RCT using matched retreatment cases and a shared bacterial endpoint. Current evidence compares each against conventional irrigation separately; Pažin et al. found no PDT benefit [[PMID:38013016]] while Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. found Er:YAG LAI benefit [[PMID:40135760]], but patient populations, tooth types, and laser protocols differ completely, making indirect comparisons unreliable.

- **Unresolved: Whether the pain benefit of SWEEPS/PIPS persists in multi-rooted molars.** The vast majority of included RCTs recruited single-rooted mandibular premolars [[PMID:33595920]] [[PMID:35267110]] [[PMID:40135760]]; canal geometry and irrigation dynamics differ substantially in multi-rooted teeth. A prospective cohort or RCT of comparable design in mandibular and maxillary molars is required.

- **Unresolved: Whether the endo-perio laser benefit applies to Grade III endo-perio lesions.** The retrospective cohort supporting water laser therapy in endo-perio cases [[PMID:42482547]] did not stratify by lesion classification; it is not known whether the composite success RR of 1.22 applies equally to Grade I, II, and III presentations. A prospective RCT stratified by contemporary endo-perio classification would resolve this.

---

### 4. When NOT to Apply This Rule

- **Vertical root fracture confirmed on CBCT or direct inspection.** The decision rule assumes a restorable tooth with intact root structure. A confirmed vertical root fracture carries a poor prognosis regardless of disinfection protocol. → Extract or refer to an oral surgeon; do not commit laser resources to a tooth with a structural endpoint.

- **Active sinus tract accompanied by swelling, fever, trismus, or lymphadenopathy.** These features indicate spreading odontogenic infection that requires systemic antibiotic therapy and may require surgical drainage. → Refer for urgent medical and surgical management; laser disinfection of the canal is a secondary, deferred step and should not delay emergency care.

- **Immature open apex with thin dentinal walls where the target apical preparation size cannot be safely achieved.** The LAI evidence base requires a minimum ISO 25/.06 taper to accommodate the laser tip [[PMID:37586645]]; forced shaping of immature teeth risks perforation, and diode irradiation to working length in thin-walled roots risks thermal strip perforation even at 1.2 W. → Manage with apexification or regenerative endodontic procedures under specialist guidance; laser disinfection should not be the primary disinfection strategy until root maturation is confirmed.
