## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

The retrieved evidence base does not directly address root canal treatment in pregnancy — no included study enrolled pregnant patients or compared trimester-specific outcomes, anaesthetic teratogenicity, or foetal safety.

Not from the retrieved evidence base: mainstream obstetric-dental guidance (ADA, ACOG Committee Opinion 569, AAE, SDCEP) is that necessary endodontic treatment should not be deferred because untreated pulpal/periapical infection carries greater maternal-foetal risk than the procedure itself; the second trimester (weeks 14–20) is generally preferred for elective completion, urgent treatment is appropriate at any stage, 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine (FDA category B) is the agent of choice, articaine and prilocaine are considered acceptable, and bupivacaine, mepivacaine plain, and long-acting agents are generally avoided or used cautiously; epinephrine at standard endodontic doses is not contraindicated but total dose should be minimised, and intraligamentary/intraosseous techniques that increase systemic vascular uptake warrant caution. Radiographs should use lead shielding and thyroid collar; NSAIDs should be avoided particularly in the third trimester. °

Returning to what the retrieved literature does support for the clinical decision: when articaine or lidocaine is chosen, high-quality Level I evidence establishes that 4% articaine achieves more effective pulpal anaesthesia than 2% lidocaine in irreversible pulpitis and is associated with less painful injection and faster onset [[PMID:31491042]]. If the primary block fails and supplemental anaesthesia is needed, intraosseous articaine achieves substantially higher success than repeat IANB (81% vs 41%) but produces a greater transient heart-rate rise reflecting higher systemic epinephrine uptake — a trade-off worth weighing in a pregnant patient [[PMID:39503145]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

None of the retrieved trials enrolled pregnant patients, so all inferences about anaesthetic choice must be extrapolated from the general adult population with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis. The umbrella review by Nagendrababu et al. synthesised five high-quality systematic reviews (AMSTAR 8–11/11) and concluded that 4% articaine is more effective than 2% lidocaine for pulpal anaesthesia in irreversible pulpitis, with limited evidence of less painful injection and faster onset [[PMID:31491042]]. Rodrigues et al. (n=84) showed that preemptive dexamethasone or diclofenac potassium increases IANB success with 4% articaine — relevant because NSAIDs and systemic corticosteroids have pregnancy-specific restrictions (NSAIDs contraindicated after 30 weeks, avoided in first trimester) that limit their applicability [[PMID:38279778]]. Miroshnychenko et al. found bupivacaine superior to lidocaine with epinephrine for postoperative analgesia duration after extraction, but bupivacaine is generally avoided in pregnancy dental practice [[PMID:36608963]]. Shadmehr et al. reported that lidocaine with clonidine outperformed lidocaine with epinephrine for IANB success (59% vs 29%), but clonidine's placental transfer and hypotensive effects make it unsuitable in pregnancy [[PMID:27176631]]. For supplemental anaesthesia, Vatankhah et al. showed intraosseous QuickSleeper articaine achieved 81% success vs 41% for IANB, though the greater and faster heart-rate response reflects higher systemic vascular uptake — a consideration if minimising foetal epinephrine exposure is a priority [[PMID:39503145]]. Kothari et al. demonstrated intraligamentary NSAIDs (diclofenac, ketorolac) outperform lignocaine as supplemental agents, but again NSAIDs are pregnancy-restricted [[PMID:40705444]]. Gaudin et al. confirmed intraosseous computerised anaesthesia is safe and more successful (91% vs 69%) than IANB with 4% articaine, with a transient heart-rate rise as the only cardiovascular signal [[PMID:37209243]].

**Level II–V**

No retrieved studies at these tiers address pregnancy, trimester timing, or foetal outcomes of endodontic treatment.
