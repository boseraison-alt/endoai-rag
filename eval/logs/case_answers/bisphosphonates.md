## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Level II and Level IIIa evidence, in patients receiving bisphosphonates or other antiresorptives, **non-surgical root canal treatment is the preferred strategy over extraction** for compromised teeth: 60-month prospective data show survival (85%) and success (76%) rates equivalent to healthy controls, with no MRONJ events attributable to endodontic treatment [[PMID:32901962]], while a large cancer cohort (n=1,621) found extraction carried an adjusted OR of ~33 for MRONJ whereas root canal treatment did not increase risk [[PMID:19805682]]. Untreated periapical periodontitis is itself an independent risk factor for MRONJ (Level IIIa evidence, adjusted OR 14.1 in denosumab-treated cancer patients), so temporising endodontic disease is not a safe alternative — treat pulpal/periapical pathology promptly and definitively [[PMID:33997909]]. When extraction is unavoidable in a high-risk patient (IV zoledronate/denosumab for malignancy, long duration, corticosteroid co-therapy), refer to oral surgery for a protocolised approach (atraumatic technique, alveoplasty, primary closure, antibiotic prophylaxis) rather than performing it in general practice — Level IIIa institutional cohorts using such protocols report 0% new MRONJ even in elevated-risk patients [[PMID:39455783]]. Restore endodontically treated teeth at the tissue level when structural compromise exists to prevent late fracture and subsequent extraction [[PMID:32901962]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

The most directly relevant systematic review (Mota et al., 2026) synthesised preclinical evidence and concluded that untreated apical periodontitis in the context of antiresorptive therapy increases MRONJ risk, with 72.7% of included studies showing histological or clinical MRONJ in AP regions — this reframes endodontic disease itself as a driver of MRONJ, not merely a bystander [[PMID:41390055]]. Aminoshariae et al.'s systematic review on systemic disease and endodontic outcomes found that oral bisphosphonate use did not appear to be associated with impaired endodontic outcomes, though the evidence base was of moderate-to-high risk of bias [[PMID:28190585]]. Moreno-Rabié et al.'s systematic review of imaging changes identified periapical radiolucency, widened PDL space, thickened lamina dura, and sclerotic bone as early radiographic changes in antiresorptive-treated patients, supporting the use of baseline radiographic assessment before therapy [[PMID:32627123]]. The large HORIZON pivotal fracture trial (Grbic et al., n=7,714 postmenopausal women on IV zoledronate 5 mg annually) reported only one MRONJ-like event in each arm, confirming very low absolute risk in the osteoporosis population and supporting routine dental care without protocol alteration in this cohort [[PMID:18167382]]. A Najeeb et al. systematic review of animal studies suggested surface application of zoledronate/alendronate may reduce root resorption after avulsion, though intracanal etidronate accelerated resorption — a caution against off-label intracanal bisphosphonate use [[PMID:27960049]].

**Level II — Prospective Studies**

Zamparini et al.'s 60-month prospective study (n=109 root canal treatments in 65 BP patients vs 102 controls) is the strongest direct evidence: survival 85% and success 76% in the BP group, statistically indistinguishable from healthy controls (88%/73%), with no MRONJ events attributable to endodontic treatment [[PMID:32901962]]. Importantly, they advocated tissue-level restoration for structurally compromised teeth to prevent fracture and secondary extraction. Wang et al.'s 2025 prospective cohort (248 extractions in 101 antiresorptive-treated patients using a comprehensive protocol — preoperative antibiotics, minimally invasive technique, CGF filling, meticulous closure) reported 0% MRONJ at 90 days, demonstrating that when extraction is necessary, protocolised surgical management can achieve excellent outcomes [[PMID:41366443]]. Hsiao et al.'s earlier retrospective work (n=34 BP teeth vs 38 controls) similarly found comparable periradicular healing (73.5% vs 81.6%, ns), supporting root canal treatment as a safe alternative to extraction [[PMID:19840641]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort Studies**

The pivotal Vahtsevanos et al. cohort (n=1,621 cancer patients on IV BP, 29,006 doses) established the risk hierarchy: dental extraction carried an adjusted OR of 32.97 for MRONJ, ever-receiving zoledronate an OR of 28.09, whereas **root canal treatment did not increase MRONJ risk** — this remains the single most influential finding shaping current guidance to prefer endodontics over extraction [[PMID:19805682]]. Hasegawa et al. (n=136 extractions in 72 cancer patients on denosumab) found post-extraction DRONJ in 28.7% of teeth, with pre-existing inflammation (OR 243.8), corticosteroid therapy (OR 73.5), and periapical periodontitis (OR 14.1) as the dominant risk factors; critically, a short drug holiday offered no protection [[PMID:33997909]]. Kanno et al. (n=511 cancer patients) showed that periapical disease and bone sclerotic changes on pre-extraction panoramic imaging were significantly more common in patients who subsequently developed ARONJ — reinforcing that hopeless teeth should be identified and managed *before* antiresorptive initiation whenever possible [[PMID:37689127]]. Two UK institutional cohorts (Arya et al., n=124 extractions in 46 elevated-risk patients; Mehta et al., n=65 extractions in 28 patients with prior MRONJ) both reported 0% new MRONJ using a protocol of alveoplasty, primary closure, prophylactic antibiotics, and delayed BMA restart — evidence that even the highest-risk patients can undergo extraction safely with appropriate technique [[PMID:39455783]] [[PMID:39455775]]. Moreno-Rabié et al. and Gaêta-Araujo et al. independently identified endodontic-periodontal disease, absent or inadequate root canal fillings with periapical lesions, sclerotic bone patterns, and furcation involvement as radiographic predictors of MRONJ at extraction sites — implicating **inadequate endodontic treatment as a modifiable risk factor** [[PMID:35789184]] [[PMID:34006074]].

**Level IIIb — Case-Control Studies**

Goker Kamalı and Turkaydın (n=711 osteoporosis patients vs matched controls) found a positive association between number of teeth with apical periodontitis and inadequate root canal fillings with AP in the osteoporosis cohort, suggesting either enhanced radiographic detectability or genuinely amplified inflammatory response in low-BMD patients — either way, arguing for higher-quality endodontic treatment standards in this group [[PMID:39503647]]. Dereci et al. (n=37 teeth in patients on IV zoledronate) found a duration-dependent effect: patients treated >1 year had significantly worse endodontic healing than those treated <1 year, indicating cumulative exposure matters and prognosis should be adjusted accordingly [[PMID:26830918]].

**Level IV — Case Series**

Two case reports (Meshkin et al.; Rocha et al.) illustrate a critical diagnostic pitfall: MRONJ can mimic acute apical abscess or endodontic disease, and misdiagnosis leads to unnecessary root canal therapy or extraction that then triggers or worsens osteonecrosis [[PMID:38900463]] [[PMID:39394868]]. A careful medication history and inspection for exposed bone are essential before initiating any endodontic intervention in antiresorptive-treated patients. Coaguila-Llerena et al. reported an 8-year favourable follow-up of a patient on alendronate who suffered accidental sodium hypochlorite extrusion during endodontic treatment — reassuring but underscoring the importance of meticulous working length control and avoidance of periapical irrigant extrusion in this population [[PMID:34626612]].

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews and Guidelines**

Mauceri et al.'s 2025 scoping review and expert meta-synthesis (223 teeth) concluded that root canal *failure* — not root canal treatment itself — is the concerning event, and emphasised careful risk-benefit evaluation and technical excellence [[PMID:40849463]]. The ADA Council on Scientific Affairs position (Hellstein et al.) estimated ARONJ prevalence at approximately 0.10% in the osteoporosis population and concluded that antiresorptive benefit outweighs MRONJ risk, with sound oral hygiene and regular dental care as the optimal preventive strategy; discontinuing bisphosphonate therapy may not lower risk [[PMID:22041409]]. The 2026 AAE position statement on endodontic implications of MRONJ is directly relevant guidance and should be consulted [[PMID:41985837]]. A recent Chinese expert consensus on denosumab-treated patients reiterated the same principles: individualised assessment, minimally invasive surgery when extraction is unavoidable, antibiotic prophylaxis, and multidisciplinary coordination [[PMID:41919535]].

> *Not from the retrieved evidence base — practical operational guidance*: Current SDCEP, ESE position statement (2021), and AAOMS 2022 position paper stratify MRONJ risk by drug, indication, duration, and route: oral bisphosphonates for osteoporosis <5 years without corticosteroids are lower-risk; IV zoledronate or denosumab (120 mg monthly) for malignancy is high-risk. Standard practice for a high-risk patient presenting with a symptomatic tooth is: (1) attempt orthograde endodontic treatment with rubber dam, controlled working length, and avoidance of periapical instrumentation and irrigant extrusion; (2) decoronate and root-treat rather than extract when the coronal tooth structure is unrestorable but roots are sound (a "root submergence" or tissue-level approach); (3) refer to oral surgery for extraction when endodontics is not feasible, ideally to a unit using a protocol like those in the Arya/Mehta or Wang studies. Antibiotic prophylaxis for routine endodontic treatment is not standard practice per these bodies; it is standard for extraction in high-risk patients. Drug holiday decisions must be made by the prescribing oncologist/physician, not the dentist, and — as the Hasegawa data show — a short holiday does not reliably reduce risk.

Returning to what the retrieved evidence establishes: root canal treatment in bisphosphonate/antiresorptive patients yields survival and success rates equivalent to healthy controls at 5 years [[PMID:32901962]], does not increase MRONJ risk (unlike extraction) [[PMID:19805682]], and prompt endodontic management of pulpal/periapical disease is the safer alternative because periapical periodontitis itself is a strong risk factor for MRONJ [[PMID:33997909]].
