# bisphosphonate-extraction-vs-rct-treatment

> 68-year-old with osteoporosis on alendronate, needs a decision between extraction and root canal treatment on a lower molar with a large periapical lesion

intent: treatment  |  papers: 24  |  cost: $0.0956

---
**Assessment:** This case presents a high-stakes risk-benefit decision: a 68-year-old on oral alendronate with a mandibular molar carrying a large periapical lesion, where both extraction and periapical infection carry MRONJ risk, but the risks differ in character and magnitude.

**Recommendation:** Prioritize nonsurgical root canal treatment (NS-RCT) over extraction as the first-line approach. Retention of the tooth with elimination of the endodontic infection is the safer strategy in a patient on oral bisphosphonates. If NS-RCT fails at recall, surgical retreatment — not extraction — should be the next consideration, with apical surgery as a tooth-preserving alternative before extraction is entertained.

Rationale: Untreated apical periodontitis in the context of antiresorptive therapy is an independent risk factor for MRONJ development, with preclinical evidence showing MRONJ lesions arising in areas of untreated AP [[PMID:41390055]]. Extraction itself, particularly when osteotomy is required, carries significant ORN/MRONJ risk in this population [[PMID:38591808]]. A prospective 60-month study of BP patients undergoing root canal treatment demonstrated survival and success rates comparable to non-BP controls (85% survival, 76% success), confirming that NS-RCT is a safe and effective approach in this cohort [[PMID:32901962]]. Contemporary NS-RCT achieves pooled periapical healing rates of 78–87% even for retreatment cases [[PMID:38280514]], and large lesion size — while a negative prognostic factor — does not preclude healing [[PMID:38280514]] [[PMID:2084204]].

**Evidence:**

- Zamparini et al. (2021) in a 60-month prospective study (n=109 BP patients) found NS-RCT survival of 85% and success of 76% — statistically indistinguishable from non-BP controls — supporting NS-RCT as a safe tooth-preserving strategy in bisphosphonate users [[PMID:32901962]].

- Mota et al. (2026) systematic review found that in preclinical models, untreated AP in animals receiving bisphosphonates (most commonly zoledronic acid) led to MRONJ in 72.7% of studies, and regions with AP developed MRONJ more frequently than unaffected regions — underscoring the danger of leaving infection untreated [[PMID:41390055]].

- Rupe et al. (2024) prospective study (n=156) found ORN occurred in 2.6% of patients following pre-radiotherapy extraction, with osteotomy requirement as a strong independent risk factor (OR 21.9); by analogy, extractions requiring bone removal in BP patients carry analogous elevated risk [[PMID:38591808]].

- Sabeti et al. (2024) systematic review of NS-ReTx found that periapical healing rates of 78–87% are achievable with contemporary techniques; smaller preoperative lesion size and adequate root filling length were significant positive predictors [[PMID:38280514]].

- Brochado Martins et al. (2025) CBCT-assessed meta-analysis confirmed that periapical lesion size and number of visits are significant outcome predictors; CBCT-based assessment detects healing more accurately than periapical radiographs alone [[PMID:40122230]].

- Sjögren et al. (1990) foundational cohort (n=356, 8–10yr follow-up) established that necrotic pulps with periapical lesions achieve 86% healing after primary treatment, and only 62% after retreatment — setting realistic expectations for a large pre-existing lesion [[PMID:2084204]].

- Del Fabbro et al. (2016) Cochrane review found no clear evidence of superiority of surgical over nonsurgical retreatment at 1 year, and that MTA root-end filling outperforms GP smoothing at both 1 and 6 years (86% vs 55% success at 6 years) — relevant if apical surgery is eventually needed [[PMID:27759881]] [[PMID:26898567]].

- Dogan et al. (2024) RCT (n=56, large periapical lesions) showed all irrigation groups achieved significant lesion volume reduction at 12 months, with no enlargement detected — supporting optimism for lesion resolution after competent NS-RCT even in large lesions [[PMID:38878107]].

- Flynn et al. (2024) systematic review found that bone grafting with/without barrier membrane significantly improves healing after apical surgery (OR 2.18) — relevant if surgical retreatment becomes necessary [[PMID:38491954]].

**Key Considerations:**

- **Drug holiday:** Current evidence does not support that a drug holiday prior to dental procedures in oral BP patients meaningfully reduces MRONJ risk given the long skeletal half-life of alendronate; consult with the prescribing physician before any decision, but this should not delay treatment of active infection.

- **Oral vs. IV bisphosphonate distinction:** Oral alendronate (as in this patient) carries substantially lower MRONJ risk than IV bisphosphonates (e.g., zoledronic acid). The 60-month Zamparini et al. data [[PMID:32901962]] and the Mota et al. preclinical findings [[PMID:41390055]] — which predominantly used IV zoledronic acid — may overestimate risk in this specific patient, though caution remains warranted.

- **Duration of bisphosphonate use:** Risk increases with cumulative dose and duration (>3–4 years). Document this and include it in the patient consent discussion.

- **Restorability first:** Confirm the tooth is restorable before committing to RCT. A tooth requiring extraction post-RCT places the patient at exactly the risk you are trying to avoid.

- **Lesion size and follow-up:** The large periapical lesion reduces but does not eliminate the probability of healing [[PMID:2084204]] [[PMID:38280514]]. Use CBCT at baseline to volumetrically characterize the lesion and again at 12 months to assess healing objectively [[PMID:40122230]] [[PMID:40539414]].

- **Coronal seal:** Ensure a definitive coronal restoration is placed promptly after obturation. Coronal leakage is a well-established failure driver; an intraorifice barrier or adequate base under the final restoration is prudent [[PMID:31587317]].

- **If NS-RCT fails:** Modern apical surgery with MTA retrograde fill and consideration of bone graft/membrane is preferable to extraction in this patient [[PMID:27759881]] [[PMID:26898567]] [[PMID:38491954]]. Extraction should be a last resort, performed with minimally invasive technique and socket grafting under antibiotic cover if it becomes unavoidable.
