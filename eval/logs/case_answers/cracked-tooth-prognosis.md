## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Level I evidence, endodontically treated cracked teeth show a moderately high pooled tooth survival of approximately 84–91% at 1–5 years, but prognosis is stratified by crack extent: teeth with a single crack, cracks contained within crowns, pre-operative probing depths ≤3 mm, and non-terminal abutment status carry the best prognosis, whereas cracks extending subgingivally, deep periodontal probing (>3 mm), and terminal abutment position markedly increase extraction risk [[PMID:31797172]] [[PMID:38272437]]. Full-cuspal-coverage restoration is mandatory after RCT — cracked RCT-treated teeth without full crowns have an ~11-fold higher extraction risk than those with crowns [[PMID:38272437]]. Where a radicular crack extends into the canal orifice, placement of an orifice barrier apical to the crack under microscope magnification is supported by very-low-certainty in-vivo evidence and is a reasonable adjunct [[PMID:41651276]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**

No Cochrane review addresses cracked tooth prognosis after RCT stratified by crack extent. The available Cochrane reviews on single- versus multiple-visit RCT (Mergoni et al., 2022, n=5693) and surgical versus non-surgical retreatment (Del Fabbro et al., 2016) do not disaggregate outcomes by pre-operative crack morphology [[PMID:36512807]] [[PMID:27759881]]. This is a genuine gap at the highest evidence tier.

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

The most directly relevant synthesis is Leong et al. (2020), a systematic review and meta-analysis of endodontically treated cracked teeth reporting an overall 60-month tooth survival of 84.1% (95% CI 72.3–91.5%); four preoperative factors carried clinical (though not statistical) significance — single crack and cracks contained within crowns favoured retention, whereas periodontal probing >3 mm and terminal-abutment status increased extraction risk [[PMID:31797172]]. Zhang et al. (2024), a larger meta-analysis (27 studies), reported tooth survival for CT-RCT of 90.5–91.1% at 1–2 years and success 83.0–91.2% at 1–4 years, and demonstrated that RCT-treated cracked teeth restored without full-cuspal coverage had an 11.3-fold higher extraction risk than those with full crowns (p<0.001) [[PMID:38272437]]. Gavriil et al. (2025) synthesised 14 cohort studies on vital cracked teeth and confirmed consistently high 1-year tooth survival (96.1–100%) across restorative approaches, with onlays outperforming crowns for pulp outcomes in the pre-RCT phase [[PMID:40581327]]. Wylie et al. (2026), the first systematic review of orifice barriers, found two small single-arm cohorts reporting 90.6% success / 96.6% survival at 2–4 years and 100% at 1–3 years for cracked RFT with an orifice barrier placed apical to the crack, though certainty was very low [[PMID:41651276]]. Fiyaz Ghan et al. (2025), addressing the pre-RCT decision point, reported 85% 12-month pulp survival with external splinting for cracks with vital pulp, informing which teeth ultimately proceed to RCT [[PMID:40653156]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort Studies**

The four cohorts pooled by Leong et al. remain the primary data source; extent-related predictors (crack visibility only on one surface vs. two, absence of subgingival extension, and absence of a J-shaped periradicular lesion) consistently favoured survival, but none reached statistical significance in the pooled analysis because of limited sample size [[PMID:31797172]].

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Guidelines**

The Korean clinical imaging guideline (Kim et al., 2024) recommends periapical radiography first for suspected vertical root fracture, with small-FOV CBCT as an adjunct — relevant when trying to establish whether a crack has propagated into a true VRF, which shifts prognosis from "cracked tooth amenable to RCT" to "extraction" [[PMID:38976094]].

**Prognostic stratification by crack extent (synthesised from the above):**

- *Favourable (survival approaching 90–97% at 1–5 years):* incomplete crack confined to crown, single crack line, no subgingival extension, pre-operative probing ≤3 mm, vital pulp or reversible pulpitis at presentation, non-terminal abutment, full-cuspal-coverage restoration placed promptly after RCT [[PMID:31797172]] [[PMID:38272437]] [[PMID:40581327]].
- *Intermediate:* crack extending to pulpal floor but not into a root, mild localised probing defect, RCT with immediate cuspal-coverage protection [[PMID:38272437]].
- *Poor:* crack extending subgingivally or into the root, isolated periodontal probing >6 mm, J-shaped radiolucency, terminal abutment, or crack propagating into the canal system — approaching true vertical root fracture, for which extraction is usually indicated [[PMID:31797172]] [[PMID:38976094]].

*Not from the retrieved evidence base:* AAE position statements categorise cracks along a continuum (craze line → fractured cusp → cracked tooth → split tooth → vertical root fracture), and the practical clinical rule most endodontists apply — that a crack visible on both the mesial and distal marginal ridges, extending across the pulpal floor, or associated with an isolated deep narrow probing defect, predicts a poor RCT outcome — comes from that consensus framework rather than from a study in this evidence base. °

What the retrieved literature *does* establish is that once RCT is undertaken, full-cuspal-coverage restoration is the single most important modifiable determinant of survival [[PMID:38272437]], and that in the specific subset with a radicular crack, an orifice barrier apical to the crack under microscopy is a reasonable adjunct [[PMID:41651276]].
