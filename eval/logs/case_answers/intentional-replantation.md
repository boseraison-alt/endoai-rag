## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Level I systematic-review evidence, intentional replantation (IR) is a reasonable salvage option for teeth with persistent apical periodontitis when nonsurgical retreatment has failed and periapical microsurgery is not feasible (e.g., inaccessible root apex, proximity to vital anatomy, thick cortical plate, or lingually inclined mandibular molars), with pooled survival ~88% and a root-resorption rate ~11% [[PMID:25742795]]. IR should be framed as an alternative — not a first-line substitute — since no comparative trials exist against nonsurgical retreatment or apical surgery, and implant-supported single crowns show significantly higher survival (~97%) [[PMID:25742795]] [[PMID:35294790]]. Case selection favours single-rooted or conical-rooted teeth with atraumatic extractability, extra-oral time minimised (<15 min ideal), minimal handling of the periodontal ligament, and short-term flexible splinting consistent with avulsion protocols [[PMID:19290892]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

The most directly relevant synthesis is Plotino et al., a 2023 systematic review that set out to compare IR against nonsurgical root canal treatment/retreatment or apical surgery for teeth with apical periodontitis; critically, no comparative clinical trials were identified, and four longitudinal studies were excluded because they evaluated IR alone (Plotino et al.) [[PMID:35294790]]. The authors concluded that available evidence — largely single-arm observational data — supports IR as a viable alternative modality with high mid- to long-term survival and low complication rates, but that decision-making must remain case-by-case given the absence of comparative evidence [[PMID:35294790]]. Torabinejad et al. performed a meta-analysis directly comparing IR with implant-supported single crowns (ISCs), reporting a weighted mean IR survival of 88% (95% CI 81–94%) versus 97% (95% CI 96–98%) for ISCs (p<0.001), with root resorption in a mean 11% of IR cases; they also noted that orthodontic pre-extrusion prior to IR improved survival in a contemporary study [[PMID:25742795]]. Adjacent evidence from the avulsion literature is informative for the biological principles governing replantation: Hinckfuss and Messer's systematic review of splinting duration in avulsed teeth found no evidence contraindicating short-term flexible splinting (≤14 days), supporting the current traumatology-derived splinting approach that is typically transferred to IR [[PMID:19290892]]. Day et al.'s Cochrane review of avulsion interventions reinforces that all evidence on adjunctive measures (medicaments, hyperbaric oxygen, EMD) is very low-quality, so extrapolation of any specific adjunct to IR is not supported [[PMID:30720860]].

**Specialty Guidelines & Position Statements**

The ESE position statement on antibiotics in endodontics addresses systemic antibiotic use in traumatic and surgical scenarios and would inform peri-procedural antibiotic decisions during IR, though it does not specifically endorse a regimen for intentional replantation itself (Segura-Egea et al.) [[PMID:28436043]].

> **NOT FROM THE RETRIEVED EVIDENCE BASE — standard practice / guideline consensus:** Case selection considerations widely cited by AAE and ESE narrative guidance include: teeth with favourable root morphology (single-rooted, minimally divergent multi-rooted teeth that can be extracted without fracture), adequate crown/restorative substrate, absence of vertical root fracture, and a periodontally healthy socket. Technique fundamentals emphasised in the endodontic surgical literature include atraumatic luxation with forceps engaging the crown (not roots), minimising extra-alveolar time (typically <15 minutes), keeping the root moist in HBSS or sterile saline, avoiding curettage of the socket and root surface, performing extra-oral retrograde preparation with ultrasonics and placing a bioceramic root-end filling (MTA or a calcium-silicate cement) when a persistent apical lesion is the indication, and passive replantation with a short-term (7–14 day) flexible splint. Post-operative review at 3, 6, and 12 months is prudent to detect early replacement or inflammatory resorption. None of these procedural specifics are drawn from the retrieved evidence base above and should be verified against the AAE Guide to Clinical Endodontics and ESE quality guidelines.

Returning to what this evidence base supports: IR is a legitimate treatment option when apical microsurgery is not feasible, with the caveat that its ~88% survival is meaningfully lower than ISC and that no head-to-head comparison against nonsurgical retreatment or apical surgery exists [[PMID:25742795]] [[PMID:35294790]].
