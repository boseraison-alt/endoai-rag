# Vital Pulp Therapy in Adult Teeth: Carious Pulp Exposure
### A 20-Minute Endodontic Teaching Curriculum

---

## OVERVIEW

Carious pulp exposure in adult permanent teeth represents a critical clinical junction: the exposed vital pulp may be preserved or sacrificed depending on the precision of diagnosis, technique, and material selection. This curriculum equips clinicians to classify carious pulp exposures by their true inflammatory state, select among direct pulp capping (DPC), partial pulpotomy (PP), and full pulpotomy (FP) using intraoperative haemostasis as the operative gate, apply calcium silicate cements as the evidence-based default, and interpret long-term outcome data to identify and manage complications. The stakes are high: a correct decision preserves pulp vitality, maintains sensibility, and avoids unnecessary root canal treatment; an incorrect one risks painful failure or undetected progression to apical periodontitis. The curriculum integrates Level I systematic review and RCT evidence with procedural protocol and decision tools for direct chairside use.

---

## Module 1 — Vital Pulp Exposure: Classification and Pathophysiologic Basis

**Defining the Carious Pulp Exposure**

A carious pulp exposure occurs when the advancing caries front breaches the pulp-dentin barrier, placing vital pulp tissue in direct contact with the oral microbiome. Unlike mechanical exposures, carious exposures carry a zone of pre-existing pulpal inflammation extending beyond the visible wound margin — a fact that fundamentally drives procedure selection. The ESE position statement [[PMID:30664240]] emphasises that accurate diagnosis of the inflammatory state of the exposed pulp, not merely the fact of exposure, must govern clinical management.

**Classification: Pulpal Diagnosis Dictates Procedure**

Three clinical scenarios arise at a carious exposure, each demanding a different procedure:

- **Reversible pulpitis with exposure**: Pulp responsive to cold, no spontaneous pain, bleeding controllable at the exposure site. Eligible for DPC or PP.
- **Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) with exposure**: Lingering or spontaneous pain, exaggerated cold response, yet radicular pulp histologically sound. Eligible for CP or PP with deeper tissue removal.
- **Irreversible pulpitis with necrosis or apical periodontitis**: Spontaneous pain, sinus tract, or periapical radiolucency present. The ESE position statement identifies these findings as indicators that more invasive intervention beyond vital pulp therapy is required [[PMID:30664240]].

The decisive challenge is that clinical diagnosis correlates poorly with histological state [[PMID:30664240]]. Inflammatory mediator research demonstrates that IL-1β is significantly upregulated in the radicular pulpal blood of irreversibly inflamed teeth compared with reversibly inflamed teeth (p < 0.05), while TGFα and FGF-2 are downregulated in both reversible and irreversible pulpitis relative to healthy controls [[PMID:40568802]]. Symptomatic teeth show more pronounced coronal inflammatory mediator expression, with IL-1α and TGFα elevated in coronal blood of teeth with severe symptomatic irreversible pulpitis [[PMID:40568802]].

These biomarker gradients explain why intraoperative haemostasis assessment remains the practical clinical gate for proceeding with vital pulp therapy — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

**Pathophysiologic Basis for Tissue Removal Depth**

Singh et al., 2023 [[PMID:37460903]] operationalised partial pulpotomy by amputating exactly 2 mm of superficially inflamed coronal pulp tissue, achieving high success regardless of capping agent — supporting the biological principle that removing the inflamed zone relocates the capping interface to pulp tissue with lower inflammatory mediator burden. Pulp canal obliteration (PCO) represents a distinct post-treatment biological event: continued mineralisation within the root canal, occurring more frequently after complete pulpotomy than partial pulpotomy. Ramani and Sangwan, 2025 [[PMID:40898413]] demonstrated in a 5-year pre-registered RCT that PCO was significantly more prevalent in the CP group (p = 0.003, RR = 0.22, 95% CI 0.07–0.70), while PP preserved cold-test sensibility responses more reliably (p = 0.015, RR = 2.35, 95% CI 1.09–7.43).

**Inflammation, Bacterial Load, and Lavage**

The inflammatory microenvironment at the exposure site is characterised by elevated matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9), with high MMP-9/total protein ratios in pulpal fluid predicting early painful failures within 250 days post-treatment [[PMID:37890614]]. Ballal et al., 2024 [[PMID:37890614]] showed that lavage with 2.5% NaOCl before pulp capping raised 4-year estimated pulp survival from 7% (95% CI 1%–40%) to 55% (95% CI 30%–100%) compared with saline lavage, and reduced the proportion of painful failures from 62% to 33%. This establishes chemical debridement of the exposure site as a critical procedural step with high-tier RCT support [[PMID:37890614]].

**Prognostic Factors**

The strongest predictor of long-term success across all vital pulp therapy procedures is the quality of the coronal restoration, confirmed across 35 years of follow-up by Ricucci et al., 2023 [[PMID:36375647]] (p < 0.001 in all follow-up periods). Secondary caries was confirmed as a significant prognostic factor at 5 years in the Ramani and Sangwan RCT [[PMID:40898413]]. Exposure size influenced outcomes at 20- and 35-year follow-up [[PMID:36375647]].

**Hierarchy of Success Rates by Procedure**

The 2011 systematic review by Aguilar and Linsuwanont [[PMID:21496652]] pooled data showing DPC success declining to 72.9% beyond 3 years, while PP sustained 99.4% and full pulpotomy 99.3% over the same horizon. More recent RCT data confirm this pattern: at 12 months, PP achieved 91.5% versus DPC 81.3% in reversible pulpitis (p = 0.08) [[PMID:40617285]], and full pulpotomy matched RCT at 12 months (97.3% vs 98.6%, p > 0.05) in irreversible pulpitis cases [[PMID:39415163]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Confirm pulpal diagnosis pre-operatively.** Perform cold test and periapical radiography. Classify as reversible pulpitis, SIP, or irreversible with apical involvement using criteria from the ESE position statement [[PMID:30664240]]. Proceed with VPT only if no periapical radiolucency is present and the tooth is restorable [[PMID:30664240]].

2. **Administer local anaesthesia and apply rubber dam.** Complete rubber dam isolation is mandatory; Singh et al. specified dental dam isolation as a protocol requirement for all partial pulpotomy arms [[PMID:37460903]].

3. **Complete caries excavation under magnification.** Remove all carious dentin completely under magnification. Ricucci et al. performed complete caries removal under magnification in all 225 DPC cases in their retrospective cohort [[PMID:36375647]].

4. **Assess exposure site haemostasis with a sterile dry cotton pellet.** Apply a sterile dry cotton pellet for up to 5 minutes. The ESE position statement identifies haemostasis assessment as the key intraoperative indicator of radicular pulp health [[PMID:30664240]]. If bleeding does not resolve, escalate to partial pulpotomy or deeper tissue removal.

5. **Lavage the exposure site with 2.5% NaOCl.** Ballal et al. used 2.5% sodium hypochlorite solution as the irrigant applied directly to the pulp wound before capping [[PMID:37890614]]; those authors did not report a specific contact time or volume.

Apply a fresh sterile dry cotton pellet after lavage — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

6. **For partial pulpotomy: amputate exactly 2 mm of coronal pulp tissue.** Use a sterile diamond bur or sharp sterile spoon excavator to remove 2 mm of the superficially inflamed pulp stump, as specified by Singh et al. [[PMID:37460903]]. Reassess haemostasis.

If bleeding persists, deepen by 1–2 mm and reassess — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

7. **Apply calcium silicate-based capping material over the pulp wound.** Iwamoto et al. used ProRoot white MTA and calcium hydroxide (Dycal) as the capping materials in their RCT, placed after a 60-second sterile cotton pellet haemostasis step [[PMID:16764130]]. Singh et al. found no clinical or radiographic difference among Ca(OH)₂, MTA, Biodentine, or Emdogain at 12 months in partial pulpotomy [[PMID:37460903]].

8. **Place base material over the capping agent, then restore with resin composite.** Iwamoto et al. placed a light-cured compomer over the capping material, followed by 35% phosphoric acid gel etching for 15 seconds, rinsing, blot drying, bonding adhesive application, and resin composite restoration — all in the same visit [[PMID:16764130]]. Ricucci et al. identified varnish used as a base as associated with a higher proportion of failures at 5 years [[PMID:36375647]]; avoid varnish as a base material.

9. **Place final coronal restoration immediately in the same visit.** Ricucci et al. confirmed quality and presence of coronal restoration as the single most significant prognostic factor across all follow-up periods up to 35 years (p < 0.001) [[PMID:36375647]].

10. **Schedule clinical and radiographic review at 6 and 12 months.** Taha et al. used 6- and 12-month clinical and radiographic follow-up as their assessment schedule [[PMID:40617285]]. At 12-month recall, apply both cold test and EPT simultaneously; Sangwan et al. demonstrated that positive response to either test combined detects significantly more responses than each test alone (p < 0.001) [[PMID:39810326]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** pre-operative cold test elicits a brief response that resolves within normal limits, no spontaneous pain, and no periapical radiolucency, with a single pinpoint exposure achieving haemostasis within 5 minutes
**THEN** diagnose reversible pulpitis; DPC is appropriate — 12-month success 81.3%
**BECAUSE** Taha et al. demonstrated DPC achieves 81.3% success at 12 months in reversible pulpitis using calcium silicate cement [[PMID:40617285]]

**IF** reversible pulpitis diagnosis is confirmed but haemostasis is delayed beyond 5 minutes at the exposure level, or multiple exposures are present
**THEN** escalate to partial pulpotomy — remove exactly 2 mm of coronal pulp tissue and reassess; 12-month success 91.5%
**BECAUSE** PP outperformed DPC numerically at both 6 and 12 months (91.5% vs 81.3%), and intraoperative haemostasis is the key escalation criterion per ESE guidance [[PMID:40617285]] [[PMID:30664240]]

**IF** pre-operative diagnosis is SIP (lingering cold response, spontaneous pain history) with no periapical radiolucency
**THEN** perform complete or partial pulpotomy; prefer PP to reduce PCO risk and preserve sensibility responses
**BECAUSE** CP and PP achieved equivalent 5-year success of 83.05% in SIP (p = 0.494), but PCO was significantly higher in the CP group (p = 0.003) [[PMID:40898413]]

**IF** periapical radiolucency, sinus tract, or absent pulp response to all sensibility testing is identified pre-operatively
**THEN** exclude from all VPT procedures; the ESE position statement identifies these as indicators requiring more invasive intervention beyond vital pulp therapy [[PMID:30664240]]
**BECAUSE** all VPT outcome trials in this evidence base excluded teeth with periapical pathology as an entry criterion [[PMID:40898413]] [[PMID:40617285]] [[PMID:37460903]]

**IF** secondary caries is detected at any recall appointment
**THEN** treat as a critical failure risk event: excavate immediately, reassess pulpal status, and place definitive coronal restoration
**BECAUSE** secondary caries was the only statistically significant prognostic factor for 5-year VPT failure (p < 0.05) [[PMID:40898413]]

## Module 2 — Clinical Recognition of Exposed Vital Pulp: Diagnosis and Presentation

**The diagnostic encounter determines everything downstream.** Before a bur touches the pulp chamber, the clinician must establish pulpal status with sufficient precision to select the correct vital pulp therapy (VPT) procedure and predict its outcome. The outcome of VPT after carious pulp exposure is explicitly multifactorial and related to the procedure, biomaterial, and pre-operative pulpal diagnosis [[PMID:33222178]].

**Pulpal Diagnosis: The Classification That Drives Case Selection**

The ESE S3-level clinical practice guideline formalises pulpal classification as the gateway to VPT decision-making, distinguishing normal pulp, reversible pulpitis, and symptomatic/asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis as the pivotal categories governing management [[PMID:37772327]]. This is not merely semantic — the 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis by Coll et al. established that all VPT methods (indirect pulp treatment, direct pulp capping, partial pulpotomy, full pulpotomy) achieve equivalent 24-month success rates of 91–97% in teeth diagnosed with normal pulp or reversible pulpitis, whereas teeth with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) — defined as exhibiting spontaneous unprovoked pain, lingering thermal pain, or referred pain — carry a distinct prognostic profile requiring full pulpotomy rather than more conservative approaches [[PMID:40533920]].

**Clinical Indicators: Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria for Carious Pulp Exposure VPT**

Inclusion criteria (tooth is a VPT candidate):
- Positive, non-lingering response to cold test
- Pain provoked by stimulus only, resolving within ≤10 seconds
- No spontaneous, nocturnal, or referred pain (for DPC/partial pulpotomy); spontaneous/lingering pain redirects to full pulpotomy [[PMID:40533920]]
- Periapical radiograph shows intact lamina dura, no periapical pathosis
- Tooth is restorable with an adequate coronal seal achievable — quality of coronal restoration was the single most significant predictor of DPC success across all follow-up periods in Ricucci et al.'s 225-tooth, up-to-35-year retrospective cohort [[PMID:36375647]]
- Controlled haemostasis achievable within 6 minutes of pulp exposure [[PMID:41121563]]

Exclusion criteria (redirect to root canal treatment):
- Spontaneous pain persisting after caries removal with haemostasis failure beyond 6 minutes
- Radiographic evidence of internal/external resorption or apical periodontitis
- Periapical pathosis present alongside SIP — Coll et al. confirmed periapical involvement significantly reduces full pulpotomy success (P=0.04 at 5-year follow-up) [[PMID:40533920]]
- Tooth non-restorable
- Profuse, uncontrollable bleeding after pulp amputation

**Haemostasis as a Real-Time Diagnostic Tool**

The colour and character of pulpal bleeding at the moment of exposure is your most direct, intraoperative window into pulp health. Darker pulpal blood colour correlates with increasing inflammatory cell infiltration and is associated with pulpectomy-level disease in primary teeth [[PMID:28332168]]. Emerging biomarker data in permanent teeth shows IL-1β is significantly upregulated in radicular blood of teeth with irreversible versus reversible pulpitis, and that coronal inflammatory markers are elevated even in reversibly inflamed teeth, underscoring why haemostasis control at the exposure site — not just bleeding presence — is the operative diagnostic criterion [[PMID:40568802]]. The current evidence-based recommendation is that haemostasis within 6 minutes of sodium hypochlorite irrigation likely improves pulpotomy success; failure beyond this threshold is the intraoperative signal to escalate to full pulpotomy or root canal treatment [[PMID:40533920]] [[PMID:41121563]].

**When Caries Removal Reveals an Exposure: The 28% Statistic**

Clinicians must anticipate that complete (total) caries removal in deep lesions will produce a pulp exposure in approximately 28% of cases [[PMID:39442480]]. In the 2024 RCT by Taha et al. (n=124, caries ≥2/3 dentine depth, reversible pulpitis diagnosis), preoperative pain level ≥5/10 on a numeric rating scale was an independent prognostic factor — odds of failure increased significantly when pain was ≥5/10 (OR 0.2, 95% CI 0.04–0.8; P=0.024) [[PMID:39442480]] — making the preoperative pain score a mandatory recorded clinical parameter, not an informal observation.

**Emerging Debate: Selective vs. Total Caries Removal Before VPT**

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** The Cochrane review (Schwendicke et al., 2021, 27 RCTs, 4195 teeth) found lower failure rates with selective excavation versus complete removal in permanent teeth (OR 11.32, 95% CI 1.97–65.02; very low-certainty evidence), supporting tissue-sparing strategies [[PMID:34280957]]. Conversely, the 2024 Taha et al. RCT found pulp survival at 12 months was significantly higher after total caries removal plus immediate VPT (98.4%) than after selective caries removal (82.5%; P=0.003), with selective removal failing to reveal overt exposures in over one quarter of cases [[PMID:39442480]]. The Coll et al. 2025 systematic review resolves this contextually: selective caries removal is strongly recommended (high certainty) for deep caries in normal pulp/reversible pulpitis, but for teeth with SIP or extremely deep caries, complete caries removal to expose and assess the pulp is strongly recommended (moderate certainty) [[PMID:41121563]]. Current standard-of-care follows this stratified, diagnosis-driven approach.

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

**1. Complete preoperative assessment**
Record pain character, duration, and intensity on a 0–10 numeric rating scale. Obtain periapical radiograph. Confirm positive cold test response resolving in ≤10 seconds; confirm absence of spontaneous/nocturnal pain for DPC or partial pulpotomy; confirm no periapical pathosis. Preoperative pain ≥5/10 NRS is an independent prognostic risk factor for failure [[PMID:39442480]].

**2. Establish rubber dam isolation**
Place rubber dam before any caries removal — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

**3. Remove caries under magnification**
For normal pulp/reversible pulpitis: selective removal to firm/leathery dentine. For SIP or extremely deep caries: complete (non-selective) removal to hard dentine to expose and directly assess pulp status [[PMID:41121563]]. Magnification is recommended to enhance pulp visualization for more accurate status assessment [[PMID:41121563]].

**4. Confirm pulp exposure and inspect**
Note exposure size, location (central vs. peripheral), and bleeding character. Darker blood correlates with higher inflammatory burden [[PMID:28332168]]. Exposure size is a significant independent predictor of DPC success at 20- and 35-year follow-up [[PMID:36375647]].

**5. Achieve haemostasis with sodium hypochlorite**
Apply NaOCl to the exposure site on a sterile cotton pellet. NaOCl is strongly recommended over saline for DPC haemostasis (high certainty) [[PMID:41121563]]. The cited GRADE recommendations do not specify a single mandated NaOCl concentration across pooled studies. Apply for up to 6 minutes total; haemostasis within 6 minutes is the threshold associated with improved success [[PMID:40533920]] [[PMID:41121563]].

**6. Intraoperative diagnosis decision**
If bleeding controlled at ≤6 minutes: proceed with chosen VPT procedure per preoperative diagnosis. If bleeding uncontrolled at 6 minutes: escalate to full pulpotomy or root canal treatment [[PMID:40533920]].

**7. Apply calcium silicate-based capping material**
Place hydraulic calcium silicate cement (HCSC) directly to the pulp wound — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base for the placement act itself. °

HCSC shows significantly higher probability of clinical and radiographic success than calcium hydroxide (OR 2.68, 95% CI 1.70–4.22; I²=0%; moderate certainty, 5 RCTs, 552 teeth) [[PMID:40394883]]. For anterior or esthetic zone teeth, use a non-bismuth-oxide-containing HCSC — MTA with bismuth oxide caused tooth discolouration in 83% of cases versus 0% for bismuth-free Biodentine (P<0.001) [[PMID:40533920]].

**8. Place coronal base and definitive restoration**
Quality of coronal restoration was the single most significant predictor of DPC success across every follow-up period from 1 to 35 years (P<0.001) [[PMID:36375647]].

Restore definitively at the earliest opportunity — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base for the specific timing interval. °

**9. Schedule follow-up**
Clinical and radiographic recall is recommended at minimum at 6 months for partial pulpotomy success confirmation [[PMID:31515048]].

Specific annual recall intervals thereafter are standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

### 4b. Decision Tree

**Branch 1**
IF preoperative diagnosis is normal pulp or reversible pulpitis AND radiograph shows intact lamina dura AND pain resolves ≤10 seconds post-stimulus
THEN proceed with selective caries removal; if exposure occurs, perform DPC, partial pulpotomy, or full pulpotomy with HCSC
BECAUSE all VPT methods show equivalent 91–97% success at 24 months in this diagnostic category [[PMID:40533920]]

**Branch 2**
IF preoperative diagnosis is symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (spontaneous, nocturnal, or lingering pain) AND periapical status is normal radiographically
THEN perform complete (non-selective) caries removal to expose the pulp; if bleeding controlled ≤6 minutes, perform full pulpotomy with HCSC
BECAUSE pooled pulpotomy success in SIP is 90% and not significantly different from NP/RP (P=0.054); complete caries removal is strongly recommended (moderate certainty) for SIP [[PMID:40533920]] [[PMID:41121563]]

**Branch 3**
IF haemostasis is NOT achieved within 6 minutes of NaOCl application at the pulp exposure site
THEN escalate to root canal treatment; do not proceed with VPT
BECAUSE haemostasis within 6 minutes is the evidence-based threshold associated with improved pulpotomy success [[PMID:40533920]] [[PMID:41121563]]

**Branch 4**
IF SIP is diagnosed AND periapical pathosis IS present radiographically
THEN root canal treatment is indicated; do not perform VPT
BECAUSE periapical involvement significantly reduces full pulpotomy success at 5 years (P=0.04) [[PMID:40533920]]

**Branch 5**
IF VPT is planned on a tooth in an esthetic zone
THEN select a non-bismuth-oxide-containing HCSC (e.g., Biodentine)
BECAUSE MTA with bismuth oxide caused clinically detectable discolouration in 83% of teeth versus 0% for bismuth-free Biodentine (P<0.001) [[PMID:40533920]]

## Module 3 — Vital Pulp Therapy Techniques: Pulp Capping, Pulpotomy, and Partial Pulpotomy

**Technique Selection: Evidence Hierarchy for Carious Pulp Exposure**

When caries produces a frank pulp exposure in a mature adult tooth, three procedures compete: direct pulp capping (DPC), partial pulpotomy (PP), and full pulpotomy (FP). The 2025 GRADE-based systematic review and meta-analysis by Coll et al. establishes the most current pooled estimate: at 24 months, IPT, DPC, PP, and FP successes range 91–97% in teeth diagnosed with normal pulp or reversible pulpitis (NP/RP), with no statistically significant difference between them (P = 0.19) [[PMID:40533920]]. This is a clinically important finding — it means technique choice for NP/RP exposures can legitimately be guided by operator skill, exposure size, and restorability, not by a rigid hierarchy of procedures.

**Material Selection Drives Outcome**

Technique equivalence in NP/RP teeth dissolves when the capping material is suboptimal. The systematic review by Cushley et al. (2021, 36-month follow-up) quantifies this precisely: calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂) success rates decline from 74% at 6 months to 56% at 4–5 years, whereas MTA holds 91%, 86%, 84%, and 81% at the same intervals, and Biodentine achieves 96%, 86%, and 86% at 6 months, 1 year, and 2–3 years respectively [[PMID:33222178]]. Meta-analysis confirmed MTA superiority over Ca(OH)₂ at 1 year (OR 2.66, 95% CI 1.46–4.84, P = 0.001) and at 2–3 years (OR 2.21, 95% CI 1.42–3.44, P = 0.0004), with no difference between MTA and Biodentine [[PMID:33222178]]. A 2025 S3-level guideline meta-analysis (Herbst et al., with declared Septodont funding — score reduced accordingly) similarly found hydraulic calcium silicate cement (HCSC) superior to Ca(OH)₂ with OR 2.68 (95% CI 1.70–4.22, I² = 0%, moderate certainty) [[PMID:40394883]]. **The clinical default for any VPT on a carious exposure is therefore calcium silicate cement (MTA or Biodentine), not calcium hydroxide.**

**Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis: Expanding the Indication**

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Traditional teaching holds that spontaneous pain, lingering thermal response, or referred pain mandates root canal treatment. Emerging evidence challenges this. The 2025 Coll et al. SR reports PP/FP success of 90% in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) teeth versus 97% in NP/RP teeth — not significantly different (P = 0.054); five-year FP success in SIP was 78%, rising when periapical involvement was absent (P = 0.04) [[PMID:40533920]]. The 2022 meta-analysis by Ather et al. pooled an 86% success rate for pulpotomy in irreversible pulpitis (95% CI 0.76–0.92; I² = 81.9%), with Biodentine outperforming MTA, Ca(OH)₂, and CEM as a capping material [[PMID:36385132]]. Santos et al. (2021) found 1–5-year HCSC VPT success rates of 78–90% in mature posterior teeth with SIP, with two studies showing outcomes comparable to NSRCT [[PMID:34205149]]. Taha et al.'s RCT (n = 60 molars, 12-month follow-up) confirmed equivalent 93% success for full pulpotomy with Biodentine versus root canal treatment, with significantly lower day-1 pain scores (P = 0.037) and fewer patients requiring analgesics (P = 0.028) following pulpotomy [[PMID:37080387]].

Current standard-of-care — as acknowledged by ESE and AAE — still requires confirmed haemostasis before committing to VPT in SIP; absent haemostasis control within a defined time window should redirect to root canal treatment — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

**Hemostasis: The Critical Intraoperative Decision Point**

Coll et al. (2025) explicitly identifies haemostasis within 6 minutes as a factor likely to improve pulpotomy success [[PMID:40533920]]. Pulp lavage chemistry also matters: Ballal et al.'s RCT (n = 96) demonstrated 4-year estimated pulp survival of 55% (95% CI 30–100%) with 2.5% NaOCl lavage versus 7% (95% CI 1–40%) with physiological saline, and NaOCl additionally reduced painful failures from 62% to 33% [[PMID:37890614]]. Taha and Khazali confirmed the importance of material choice in PP: MTA achieved 85% success versus 43% for Ca(OH)₂ at 2 years in mature molars with carious exposure and irreversible pulpitis signs (P = 0.006), using 5% NaOCl for pre-capping disinfection [[PMID:28673494]].

**Coronal Seal: The Most Consistently Underestimated Variable**

Ricucci et al.'s retrospective cohort (n = 225 teeth, up to 35-year follow-up) reported DPC success of 95% at 5 years and 89% at 35 years — the dominant predictor of failure was coronal restoration quality (P < 0.001 at all time points), not capping material or exposure size [[PMID:36375647]]. This finding rests on a single-operator retrospective cohort of 225 teeth (Ricucci et al. 2023 [[PMID:36375647]]) — no RCT has yet isolated coronal seal as an independent variable in a controlled design; treat as a strong signal pending prospective confirmation.

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

**1. Establish diagnosis and obtain pre-operative radiograph**
Confirm NP/RP or SIP by cold test, percussion, palpation, and periapical radiograph. Periapical pathosis is a relative contraindication for VPT; absent periapical involvement significantly increases FP success in SIP teeth (P = 0.04) [[PMID:40533920]].

**2. Administer local anaesthesia and place rubber dam**
Rubber dam isolation is mandatory — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

Duncan et al. identify isolation of the operating field as a factor requiring further experimental investigation but note it as a clinical prerequisite for VPT [[PMID:36334098]].

**3. Complete caries excavation**
For NP/RP with deep caries, selective caries removal minimises pulp exposure [[PMID:40533920]]. For SIP or extremely deep caries, complete caries removal is recommended to expose the pulp and allow assessment [[PMID:40533920]].

**4. Irrigate the exposed pulp wound with 2.5% NaOCl**
Ballal et al.'s RCT applied 2.5% NaOCl (versus physiological saline as control) to the pulp wound before DPC with MTA; the NaOCl group achieved 55% estimated 4-year pulp survival versus 7% for saline [[PMID:37890614]]. Ballal et al. did not specify a fixed contact time in seconds — irrigate until active bleeding is assessed, within the 6-minute haemostasis window [[PMID:40533920]].

**5. Select technique based on exposure character and bleeding**
- Pinpoint exposure, bleeding controlled at surface within 6 minutes → DPC
- Larger exposure, surface contamination, or bleeding uncontrolled at surface → PP: amputate 2 mm of coronal pulp tissue [[PMID:28673494]]
- Bleeding uncontrolled at 2 mm depth → advance to FP: remove entire coronal pulp to canal orifices [[PMID:40533920]]

**6. Confirm haemostasis within 6 minutes**
If bleeding is not controlled within 6 minutes at the current level of tissue removal, escalate to the next removal level or redirect to NSRCT [[PMID:40533920]].

**7. Apply calcium silicate cement directly to the pulp wound**
Place MTA or Biodentine as the capping material. For anterior teeth where discolouration is a concern, select bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine: MTA causes discolouration in 83% of teeth versus 0% for bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine (P < 0.001) [[PMID:40533920]]. Taha and Khazali used White ProRoot MTA placed directly over the haemostatic pulp wound in the PP group [[PMID:28673494]].

**8. Place an intermediate base over the set calcium silicate cement**
Taha and Khazali placed resin-modified glass ionomer cement over MTA prior to permanent restoration in their PP protocol [[PMID:28673494]].

This step is otherwise standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base for DPC specifically. °

**9. Place the definitive coronal restoration**
Ballal et al.'s protocol specified immediate composite resin restoration placed in the same visit after MTA and base placement [[PMID:37890614]]. Coronal restoration quality is the single dominant predictor of long-term DPC failure (P < 0.001) [[PMID:36375647]].

**10. Schedule review at 6 and 12 months minimum**
Assess vitality with cold test and periapical radiograph. Cushley et al. used 6-month intervals as standard review points across included studies [[PMID:33222178]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the tooth has a carious pulp exposure, responds to cold within normal limits or with brief hypersensitivity only (no lingering, no spontaneous pain), and periapical radiograph shows no pathosis
**THEN** perform DPC with calcium silicate cement after haemostasis achieved with 2.5% NaOCl
**BECAUSE** 24-month success is 91–97% for NP/RP across all VPT techniques when HCSC is used; material is the dominant variable [[PMID:40533920]]

**IF** the exposure is large, the pulp bleeds heavily at the surface, or surface contamination has occurred
**THEN** perform PP — amputate 2 mm of coronal pulp tissue, re-assess bleeding after 2.5% NaOCl haemostasis
**BECAUSE** PP removes superficially inflamed tissue; MTA PP achieved 85% success at 2 years in mature molars versus 43% for Ca(OH)₂ (P = 0.006) [[PMID:28673494]]

**IF** the tooth presents with spontaneous or lingering thermal pain (SIP), periapical status is normal, and haemostasis is achieved within 6 minutes after full coronal pulp removal
**THEN** perform full pulpotomy with Biodentine or MTA and restore immediately
**BECAUSE** PP/FP success in SIP is 90% — not significantly different from NP/RP success of 97% (P = 0.054); haemostasis within 6 minutes is a positive prognostic factor [[PMID:40533920]]

**IF** haemostasis cannot be achieved within 6 minutes at any level of coronal pulp removal, or periapical pathosis is radiographically confirmed
**THEN** do not proceed with VPT; redirect to NSRCT
**BECAUSE** periapical pathosis in SIP significantly reduces FP success (P = 0.04); uncontrolled bleeding signals inflamed radicular tissue beyond VPT salvage [[PMID:40533920]]

**IF** the tooth is in the aesthetic zone and the patient is concerned about discolouration
**THEN** select bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine over grey or white MTA
**BECAUSE** MTA causes clinically significant tooth discolouration in 83% of cases versus 0% for bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine (P < 0.001) [[PMID:40533920]]

**IF** the coronal restoration cannot be placed to a high standard (inadequate isolation, insufficient tooth structure, or the patient cannot attend for timely definitive restoration)
**THEN** do not proceed with VPT regardless of pulpal diagnosis
**BECAUSE** restoration quality is the single dominant predictor of DPC failure across all follow-up periods to 35 years (P < 0.001) [[PMID:36375647]]

## Module 4 — Vital Pulp Therapy Outcomes, Success Rates, and Complication Management

**Success Rates by Procedure and Material**

For carious pulp exposures in mature permanent teeth, all VPT modalities achieve high short-term success when calcium silicate cements (CSCs) are used. The 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis by Coll et al. reports 24-month success rates of 91–97% across indirect pulp treatment, DPC, partial pulpotomy, and full pulpotomy in teeth diagnosed with normal pulp or reversible pulpitis — with no statistically significant difference between procedures (P=0.19) [[PMID:40533920]]. Material choice profoundly affects outcome. Cushley et al.'s systematic review and meta-analysis (2021) of DPC in cariously exposed permanent teeth pooled success rates for calcium hydroxide declining from 74% at 6 months to 56% at 4–5 years, versus MTA at 91% (6 months), 86% (1 year), and 81% (4–5 years), and Biodentine at 96% (6 months) and 86% at both 1 and 2–3 years; MTA outperformed calcium hydroxide at 1 year (OR 2.66, 95% CI 1.46–4.84, P=0.001) and 2–3 years (OR 2.21, 95% CI 1.42–3.44, P=0.0004), with no difference between MTA and Biodentine [[PMID:33222178]].

**Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis: Pulpotomy as Definitive Treatment**

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Historically, irreversible pulpitis mandated root canal treatment. Coll et al. (2025) report a pooled partial/full pulpotomy success rate of 90% in teeth with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) versus 97% in normal pulp/reversible pulpitis teeth (P=0.054, not significant), and a 5-year full pulpotomy success of 78% for SIP, with significantly better outcomes when periapical pathosis is absent (P=0.04) [[PMID:40533920]]. Ather et al.'s meta-analysis yields a pooled pulpotomy success of 86% (95% CI: 0.76–0.92) for irreversible pulpitis, with open-apex teeth significantly outperforming closed-apex (96% vs. 83%, P=0.02), and Biodentine yielding better outcomes than MTA, calcium hydroxide, and calcium-enriched mixture cement [[PMID:36385132]]. Zhu et al.'s RCT found full pulpotomy vs. RCT clinical success rates of 97.3% vs. 98.6% and radiographic success of 93.3% vs. 94.6% (P>0.05) at 12 months in mature molars with irreversible pulpitis, with pulpotomy producing greater postoperative pain reduction at day 1 and significantly shorter treatment time and lower cost [[PMID:39415163]]. Taha et al.'s RCT reports comparable success at 12 months (93% each), with significantly lower day-1 pain (P=0.037) and analgesic requirement (P=0.028) after pulpotomy versus RCT [[PMID:37080387]]. **Current standard-of-care per the ESE and AAE still recognises RCT as the established treatment for irreversible pulpitis; pulpotomy should be framed as an evidence-supported alternative pending longer-term RCT data.**

**Partial vs. Full Pulpotomy for Carious Exposure**

Louzada et al.'s systematic review of two RCTs (n=156 teeth, both with irreversible pulpitis) finds no significant clinical or radiographic difference between partial and full pulpotomy at 12 months (pooled unadjusted success: full pulpotomy 90%, partial pulpotomy 83%); one study reported significantly less postoperative pain at 1 week with full pulpotomy, the other did not [[PMID:39264795]].

**Critical Prognostic Factors**

The most influential intraoperative modifier is hemostasis: Coll et al. (2025) report that hemostasis within 6 minutes likely improves pulpotomy success [[PMID:40533920]]. Coronal restoration quality is equally critical — Ricucci et al.'s retrospective cohort of 225 teeth followed up to 35 years identifies restoration quality as the strongest predictor of DPC success at every follow-up interval (P<0.001), with success rates of 100% at 1 year, 95% at 5 and 10 years, 86% at 20 years, and 89% at 35 years [[PMID:36375647]]. Duncan et al. confirm that inflammatory pulpal status, capping material, caries depth, biomarker levels, and restoration integrity significantly affect outcome, whereas age, sex, tooth type, root maturity, and the quantity of intraoperative pulpal haemorrhage per se do not [[PMID:36334098]].

**Pulp Lavage**

Ballal et al.'s RCT (n=96) demonstrates that pulp lavage with 2.5% NaOCl before DPC yielded estimated 4-year pulp survival of 55% (95% CI: 30–100%) versus 7% (95% CI: 1–40%) with physiological saline, and reduced the proportion of painful failures from 62% to 33% in the saline group compared to 33% in the NaOCl group [[PMID:37890614]].

**Tooth Discoloration**

MTA discolors teeth significantly more (83%) than bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine (0%), a finding of particular importance for anterior teeth [[PMID:40533920]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

**Applicable procedure: Carious pulp exposure — partial or full pulpotomy with calcium silicate cement**

1. **Confirm diagnosis pre-operatively.** Verify symptomatic or reversible pulpitis via clinical and radiographic assessment. Exclude periapical pathosis associated with necrosis; SIP with periapical involvement may be treated but carries reduced success (78% at 5 years) [[PMID:40533920]]. Donnermeyer et al. note that no single sensibility test reliably confirms true pulpal status — use a combination of thermal testing, percussion, and symptom history [[PMID:35536159]].

2. **Achieve rubber-dam isolation.** Rubber-dam isolation is standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. °

Fraser & Webster note that their cohort deviated from this standard with acceptable outcomes, but explicitly describe it as deviation from recommended practice [[PMID:38714875]].

3. **Remove all carious dentine completely.** For teeth with SIP or extremely deep caries, complete caries removal is recommended to expose the pulp [[PMID:40533920]].

4. **Irrigate the pulp wound with 2.5% NaOCl.** Ballal et al.'s RCT specifies 2.5% NaOCl lavage applied to the pulp exposure site before hemostasis assessment; exposure time was not explicitly reported in the abstract [[PMID:37890614]]. Wazurkar et al. describe bleeding control using 2.5% NaOCl-soaked cotton applied to the exposure site [[PMID:38975485]].

5. **Achieve hemostasis within 6 minutes.** Apply a sterile cotton pellet moistened with 2.5% NaOCl to the pulp wound with digital pressure; this technique is described by Wazurkar et al. for partial pulpotomy [[PMID:38975485]]. Coll et al. (2025) report that hemostasis within 6 minutes likely improves pulpotomy success [[PMID:40533920]].

6. **Perform partial or full pulpotomy under magnification.** Remove inflamed coronal pulp tissue to 2–3 mm depth for partial pulpotomy [[PMID:38975485]]; extend to the canal orifice for full pulpotomy when SIP is confirmed or hemostasis cannot be achieved at the partial level within 6 minutes [[PMID:40533920]]. Wisniewski & Hogue note that examination of pulp tissue condition using magnification is critical to success [[PMID:41115020]].

7. **Apply calcium silicate cement capping material.** Place MTA or bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine directly onto the pulp wound; Wazurkar et al. describe MTA placement over a 2–3 mm pulpotomy wound following hemostasis in a partial pulpotomy case [[PMID:38975485]]. For anterior or visible teeth, prefer bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine over conventional MTA given 0% vs. 83% discoloration rates respectively [[PMID:40533920]].

8. **Place intermediate base and immediate permanent coronal restoration.** Wazurkar et al. describe placement of resin-modified glass ionomer cement over set MTA followed by composite restoration in the same visit [[PMID:38975485]]. Coronal seal quality is the strongest single long-term predictor of DPC success (P<0.001) [[PMID:36375647]].

9. **Review clinically and radiographically at 6 months, 12 months, then annually.** Success criteria: asymptomatic tooth, positive cold or EPT response, absence of radiographic periapical change, confirmed by Cushley et al. [[PMID:33222178]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** tooth presents with carious pulp exposure, provoked pain resolving within seconds, no spontaneous unprovoked pain, and no periapical pathosis (reversible pulpitis / normal pulp)
**THEN** perform partial pulpotomy or DPC with calcium silicate cement; target hemostasis within 6 minutes
**BECAUSE** all VPT modalities achieve 91–97% success at 24 months in NP/RP with CSC; procedure choice does not significantly affect outcome (P=0.19) [[PMID:40533920]]

**IF** tooth presents with carious pulp exposure, spontaneous/unprovoked pain or lingering thermal pain >10 seconds (SIP), and hemostasis is achieved within 6 minutes at partial pulpotomy depth
**THEN** full pulpotomy with bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine or MTA is appropriate as definitive treatment
**BECAUSE** full pulpotomy yields 90% success at 24 months in SIP teeth; hemostasis within 6 minutes is a positive prognostic marker [[PMID:40533920]] [[PMID:39264795]]

**IF** tooth presents with SIP plus radiographic periapical pathosis (apical periodontitis)
**THEN** treat with caution; discuss RCT as the standard alternative, as VPT 5-year success for SIP drops to 78% overall, with periapical pathosis being a significant negative prognostic factor (P=0.04)
**BECAUSE** teeth without periapical involvement show significantly increased success compared to those with it [[PMID:40533920]]

**IF** hemostasis is not achieved within 6 minutes at any pulpotomy level
**THEN** do not place capping material — resect further pulp tissue or convert to root canal treatment
**BECAUSE** uncontrolled bleeding at 6 minutes is associated with inflammatory tissue beyond the resection plane and is linked to poorer outcomes [[PMID:40533920]] [[PMID:36334098]]

**IF** anterior tooth with carious exposure requires pulpotomy or capping and aesthetics are critical
**THEN** select bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine as the capping material rather than conventional MTA
**BECAUSE** MTA causes 83% discoloration versus 0% with bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine (P<0.001) [[PMID:40533920]]

**IF** VPT fails clinically (onset of spontaneous pain, confirmed necrosis on sensibility testing, or periapical radiolucency on recall radiograph)
**THEN** convert to non-surgical RCT — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base (AAE guidelines). °

The retrieved evidence base supports VPT as comparable to RCT at 12–24 months in appropriately selected cases, meaning the root canal anatomy in a failed VPT tooth remains intact and conventional RCT is a fully viable rescue option [[PMID:39415163]] [[PMID:34854987]].

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

**1. Pulpal diagnosis, not exposure size, determines procedure selection.**
Classify every carious exposure as reversible pulpitis, SIP, or irreversible with apical involvement before touching the pulp. All VPT procedures achieve equivalent 91–97% success at 24 months in normal pulp/reversible pulpitis with calcium silicate cement — meaning the correct diagnosis opens the full menu of techniques [[PMID:40533920]]. Teeth with periapical pathosis are categorically excluded from VPT [[PMID:30664240]].

**2. Lavage with 2.5% NaOCl and achieve haemostasis within 6 minutes — these are non-negotiable steps.**
Pulp lavage with 2.5% NaOCl before capping raises 4-year estimated pulp survival from 7% (saline) to 55% [[PMID:37890614]]. Haemostasis within 6 minutes is the intraoperative gate: failure to achieve it at the current level of tissue removal mandates escalation — first to partial pulpotomy (amputate exactly 2 mm [[PMID:37460903]]), then to full pulpotomy, then to root canal treatment [[PMID:40533920]] [[PMID:41121563]].

**3. Calcium silicate cement is the only acceptable first-line capping material; select bismuth-oxide-free formulations in the aesthetic zone.**
Calcium hydroxide success falls to 56% at 4–5 years versus 81% for MTA — replace it as your default [[PMID:33222178]]. MTA causes clinically detectable discolouration in 83% of teeth versus 0% for bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine (P<0.001) [[PMID:40533920]]; in anterior teeth, bismuth-free Biodentine is the required choice. Never use varnish as a base over the capping material [[PMID:36375647]].

**4. Coronal seal quality is the single strongest long-term predictor of VPT success — do not delay the definitive restoration.**
Across 225 teeth followed for up to 35 years, restoration quality was the dominant predictor of DPC failure at every follow-up interval (P<0.001) [[PMID:36375647]]. Place the definitive resin composite in the same visit as the capping material. At every recall, verify the seal and excavate secondary caries immediately — the only statistically significant 5-year failure predictor in the Ramani & Sangwan RCT (P<0.05) [[PMID:40898413]].

**5. Emerging: Pulpotomy in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis is a legitimate evidence-supported alternative to root canal treatment in appropriately selected, haemostasis-confirmed cases.**
Pooled PP/FP success in SIP is 90% at 24 months — not significantly different from 97% in NP/RP (P=0.054) [[PMID:40533920]]; two independent RCTs report equivalent 12-month success versus root canal treatment (93% vs 93% [[PMID:37080387]]; 97.3% vs 98.6% [[PMID:39415163]]). However, 5-year FP data in SIP stand at 78%, longest RCT follow-up for this indication remains 24 months in most studies, and ESE/AAE still list root canal treatment as the established standard for irreversible pulpitis — frame pulpotomy in SIP as an evidence-supported alternative, not established standard-of-care, until longer-term prospective data mature.

## The Final Verdict

### 1. Decision Rule

**IF** carious pulp exposure is present AND preoperative diagnosis is normal pulp or reversible pulpitis (brief cold response ≤10 s, no spontaneous pain) AND periapical radiograph shows no pathosis AND haemostasis is achieved at exposure level within 6 minutes with 2.5% NaOCl → **perform DPC with HCSC** (single pinpoint exposure, immediate haemostasis) **OR partial pulpotomy removing exactly 2 mm of coronal pulp** (larger exposure, haemostasis delayed >5 min at surface, or multiple exposures); place bismuth-oxide-free HCSC; restore immediately with resin composite.

**IF** preoperative diagnosis is SIP (spontaneous or lingering thermal pain >10 s, no periapical pathosis radiographically) AND haemostasis is achieved within 6 minutes after complete coronal pulp removal → **perform full pulpotomy with bismuth-oxide-free Biodentine or MTA**; restore immediately; consider partial pulpotomy if haemostasis is achieved at 2 mm depth.

**IF** haemostasis is NOT achieved within 6 minutes at any level of coronal tissue removal → **stop VPT; convert to non-surgical root canal treatment in the same visit or at earliest appointment**.

**IF** periapical radiolucency, sinus tract, or absent sensibility to all testing is present preoperatively → **do not attempt VPT; root canal treatment is indicated**.

### 2. Evidence Anchor

**DPC and partial pulpotomy for NP/RP carious exposure:**
**Anchored in:** Level I systematic review with GRADE analysis, 2025 (Coll et al., pooled n across 40+ RCTs) [[PMID:40533920]], corroborated by Level I SR (Cushley et al., 2021, n=552) [[PMID:33222178]] and Level I RCT (Taha et al., 2025, n=140, 12 months) [[PMID:40617285]].

**2.5% NaOCl lavage as mandatory pre-capping step:**
**Anchored in:** Level I RCT (Ballal et al., 2024, n=96, 4-year follow-up) [[PMID:37890614]].

**Haemostasis within 6 minutes as escalation criterion:**
**Anchored in:** Level I SR/meta-analysis (Coll et al., 2025) [[PMID:40533920]] and Level V GRADE guideline (Coll et al., 2025) [[PMID:41121563]].

**Full pulpotomy for SIP without periapical pathosis:**
**Anchored in:** Level I SR with GRADE (Coll et al., 2025, pooled success 90%, P=0.054 vs NP/RP) [[PMID:40533920]], corroborated by two Level I RCTs (Taha et al., 2023, n=60; Zhu et al., 2024, n=146, both 12-month follow-up) [[PMID:37080387]] [[PMID:39415163]] and Level I meta-analysis (Ather et al., 2022, pooled 86%) [[PMID:36385132]].

**VPT exclusion for periapical pathosis:**
**Anchored in:** Level I SR (Coll et al., 2025; periapical involvement significantly reduces FP success, P=0.04) [[PMID:40533920]] and Level V ESE position statement [[PMID:30664240]].

**Coronal seal as dominant long-term predictor:**
**Anchored in:** Level IIIa retrospective cohort (Ricucci et al., 2023, n=225, 35-year follow-up, P<0.001) [[PMID:36375647]] — no RCT has yet isolated coronal seal as an independent variable in a controlled design; treat as a strong signal pending prospective confirmation.

### 3. Where Uncertainty Remains

- **Sub-question: What is the optimum NaOCl concentration for pulp lavage in VPT?** Ballal et al. used 2.5% [[PMID:37890614]], Taha & Khazali used 5% [[PMID:28673494]], and pooled GRADE guidance declines to mandate a single concentration [[PMID:41121563]]. No head-to-head RCT has directly compared 2.5% vs 5% NaOCl for pulp wound lavage on a pulp survival endpoint; a three-arm RCT (saline control, 2.5% NaOCl, 5% NaOCl) with ≥2-year follow-up would resolve this.

- **Sub-question: What is the true long-term (≥5-year) success of full pulpotomy in SIP in mature adult teeth?** The longest follow-up RCT data for FP in SIP extends to only 24 months in most trials; the 5-year Ramani & Sangwan data [[PMID:40898413]] includes both PP and CP arms in a mixed diagnosis population. A pre-registered RCT with ≥5-year primary endpoint specifically for FP in SIP, stratified by periapical status, is required.

- **Sub-question: Does preoperative pain score (≥5/10 NRS) independently predict VPT failure when HCSC is used?** The Taha et al. 2024 prospective study identified ≥5/10 NRS as an independent failure predictor (OR 0.2, P=0.024) [[PMID:39442480]] in a single-centre cohort of 124 teeth; no multicentre RCT has confirmed this finding with HCSC as the capping material. A multicentre prospective cohort with pain score as a pre-specified covariate would resolve this.

- **Sub-question: Does the coronal seal material (resin composite vs RMGIC vs ceramic) independently modify long-term VPT survival?** All current evidence identifies "coronal restoration quality" as predictive [[PMID:36375647]] but no RCT has directly compared restoration types as the study variable; a factorial RCT crossing capping material with restoration type over ≥5 years is required.

- **Sub-question: Is CBCT superior to periapical radiograph for detecting early VPT failure at recall?** No included trial used CBCT as the primary imaging outcome; periapical radiography remains the only validated imaging modality in the VPT evidence base [[PMID:40617285]]. A prospective cohort comparing periapical radiograph versus CBCT for recall sensitivity at 12 and 24 months would define the role of CBCT in VPT monitoring.

### 4. When NOT to Apply This Rule

- **Confirmed or suspected vertical root fracture** (e.g., wedge-shaped periapical lesion, isolated periodontal probing defect, percussion sensitivity localised to one surface): do not attempt VPT or root canal treatment without first excluding fracture by transillumination, direct visualisation under magnification, or CBCT. → Refer to endodontist or oral surgeon for fracture assessment; extraction is likely the definitive treatment.

- **Active sinus tract, fluctuant swelling, or systemic signs of spreading infection** (pyrexia, cervical lymphadenopathy, trismus): the pulp is either necrotic or the periapical pathology is beyond VPT salvage regardless of preoperative sensibility testing. → Establish drainage, prescribe antibiotics if indicated, and plan definitive root canal treatment or extraction; do not apply the VPT decision rule.

- **Patient with a documented history of abandoned recall, inability to achieve rubber dam isolation, or inability to place an immediate definitive coronal restoration** (coronal seal quality is the dominant long-term predictor, P<0.001 [[PMID:36375647]]): absence of a reliable seal converts a biologically sound VPT case into a predictable failure. → Defer VPT; perform interim caries management and reassess when definitive restoration and reliable recall are both achievable, or refer to a centre able to provide both in a single visit.
