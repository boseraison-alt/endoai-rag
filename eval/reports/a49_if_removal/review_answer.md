## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Cochrane-level evidence, single-visit and multiple-visit root canal treatment produce equivalent long-term outcomes for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis, with no difference in radiographic healing or tooth retention at ≥12 months [[PMID:36512807]]. Either protocol is defensible; select on case-specific grounds (ability to dry the canal, absence of acute swelling, patient tolerance, operator time), recognising that single-visit treatment is associated with a modestly higher rate of pain within the first postoperative week (moderate-certainty evidence) [[PMID:36512807]]. For necrotic teeth specifically, adding a triple antibiotic paste inter-appointment dressing does not reduce postoperative pain versus a single-visit approach [[PMID:32047601]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**

The 2022 Cochrane review by Mergoni et al. (47 RCTs, 5,805 participants, 5,693 teeth) is the highest-tier evidence and directly addresses this question [[PMID:36512807]]. For the primary outcomes, there was no difference in tooth extraction (RR 0.46, 95% CI 0.09–2.50; very low certainty) or radiological failure at ≥1 year (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81–1.07; moderate certainty) between single- and multiple-visit protocols [[PMID:36512807]]. The one signal favouring multiple visits was a higher proportion of patients reporting pain within the first week after single-visit treatment (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.14–2.09; moderate certainty), with subgroup analysis showing this effect was concentrated in vital teeth (RR 2.16) rather than necrotic teeth, and with mechanical instrumentation [[PMID:36512807]]. Swelling/flare-up, analgesic use, and sinus tract persistence did not differ [[PMID:36512807]]. The critical qualifier is that the Cochrane review pools vital and necrotic teeth; the necrotic-with-apical-periodontitis subgroup was not separately analysed for the primary healing outcome, so the equivalence conclusion is generalised rather than subgroup-specific.

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

The Cochrane review dominates this tier [[PMID:36512807]]. Among individual RCTs specific to necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis, AbdurRahman et al. randomised 44 patients to single-visit versus multiple-visit treatment with triple antibiotic paste dressing and found no difference in postoperative pain at 24, 48, 72 hours, or 1 week — arguing against the pain-suppressing rationale for medicated multi-visit protocols in this indication [[PMID:32047601]]. Riaz et al. (n=60, single-rooted teeth with necrotic pulps) similarly reported no difference in post-obturation pain between protocols (mean VAS 2.23 vs 2.38) [[PMID:30317336]]. Shubham et al. (n=160) found visit number itself was not an independent predictor of postoperative pain — pulp status and preoperative pain were the influencing factors [[PMID:34078331]]. The older Figini/Lodi Cochrane iteration reached the same overall conclusion of equivalent radiographic outcomes [[PMID:18718362]]. Both the Cochrane review and the individual RCTs are limited by heterogeneity in intracanal medicaments (Ca(OH)₂ vs triple antibiotic paste vs none) and inconsistent risk of bias.

**Level II — Prospective Studies**

Shubham et al. prospectively confirmed that for necrotic teeth, single- vs multi-visit protocols produced comparable postoperative pain, with baseline pulp status and preoperative pain being the stronger predictors [[PMID:34078331]]. A recent US endodontist survey (Tsai & Kim, 2026) reported that 74.9% routinely complete necrotic cases in a single visit and 65.6% perceive no difference in postoperative complications — reflecting how the Cochrane conclusion has translated into specialist practice [[PMID:42214667]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort Studies**

Kirci & Karataslioglu compared single-visit (no medicament), multi-visit with Ca(OH)₂, and multi-visit with chlorhexidine gel in 62 mandibular molars with periapical lesions, using PAI scores and fractal analysis at 6 and 12 months; all three groups showed significant and equivalent periapical healing with no intergroup differences [[PMID:41460772]]. Wong et al. (n=538 teeth across two centres) reported no significant difference in post-obturation pain at 1 day (24.7% vs 33.5%) or 7 days (4.0% vs 5.3%) between single- and multi-visit protocols; teeth with apical periodontitis actually had a lower incidence of post-obturation pain [[PMID:26272704]]. In contrast, Albashaireh & Alnegrish (n=291) found significantly higher post-obturation pain in the multi-visit group (38% vs 27%) and specifically noted a greater pain incidence when treating non-vital pulps [[PMID:9594474]], and Jabeen & Khurshiduzzaman (n=120 asymptomatic non-vital single-rooted teeth) reported significantly more pain in single-visit cases [[PMID:24858151]]. These cohort-level pain findings conflict, and the higher-certainty Cochrane pooled estimate (slight excess pain within one week for single-visit, driven by vital teeth) should take precedence.

**Level IV — Case Series**

Asgary & Ahmadyar reported successful resolution of inflammatory external root resorption with periapical lesions in maxillary incisors treated by one-visit RCT, with full radiographic healing at 14 months — illustrating that single-visit treatment can succeed even in complicated necrotic presentations when disinfection and coronal seal are adequate [[PMID:23130060]]. This is anecdotal and hypothesis-generating only.

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews**

The Bryce et al. summary of the 2022 Cochrane review reiterates its conclusion: no difference in success or healing between protocols, with the caveat of increased 1-week postoperative pain after single-visit treatment [[PMID:37188920]]. Mohammadi et al. articulates the traditional rationale for reserving multi-visit treatment (with intracanal medication) for necrotic/infected canals — a position no longer supported by the current pooled evidence [[PMID:17069072]].
