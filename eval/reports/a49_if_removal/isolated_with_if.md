## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Cochrane-level evidence, single-visit and multiple-visit root canal treatment produce equivalent radiographic healing and tooth survival for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis, so either approach is defensible and the choice can be made on case-specific factors (canal dryness, ability to obturate to standard, patient tolerance, appointment logistics) [[PMID:36512807]]. Clinicians should counsel patients that single-visit treatment carries a modestly higher risk of pain within the first week without any long-term outcome penalty [[PMID:36512807]]. For teeth where the canal cannot be dried, or where there is diffuse swelling / acute exacerbation, multiple visits with an interappointment calcium hydroxide dressing remain the more prudent choice.

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**

The 2022 Cochrane review by Mergoni et al. (47 RCTs, 5,805 participants, 5,693 teeth) is the definitive synthesis on this question and directly addresses necrotic teeth as a subgroup [[PMID:36512807]]. For the primary outcomes, there was no difference between single-visit and multiple-visit protocols in tooth extraction (RR 0.46, 95% CI 0.09–2.50; very low certainty) or radiographic failure at ≥12 months (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81–1.07; moderate certainty) [[PMID:36512807]]. The one signal favouring multiple visits was a higher proportion of patients reporting pain within one week in single-visit groups (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.14–2.09; moderate certainty), but subgroup analysis showed this effect was driven by vital teeth (RR 2.16) rather than necrotic teeth, and there were no differences in swelling, flare-up, analgesic use, or sinus tract persistence [[PMID:36512807]].

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

The Schwendicke and Göstemeyer meta-analysis (29 trials, 4,341 patients) is broadly concordant with the Cochrane review: no significant difference in long-term complications (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.75–1.35) or pain (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.76–1.30), but a signal for higher flare-up risk after single-visit treatment (RR 2.13, 95% CI 1.16–3.89), leading the authors to suggest multiple visits may be preferable for teeth with periapical lesions [[PMID:28148534]]. Trial-sequential analysis, however, showed firm evidence had not been reached for any outcome. In contrast, Almeida et al.'s meta-analysis of 17 RCTs in non-vital teeth found single-visit therapy produced 21% less post-obturation pain (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66–0.94), directly opposing the Cochrane finding [[PMID:28349914]]. Among primary RCTs, Wong et al. (n=220, mean follow-up 29 months) reported equivalent success rates (88.9% vs 87.4%) and no significant pain difference for necrotic and vital teeth combined [[PMID:26687126]], while their larger multicentre trial (n=567) similarly found no difference in one-day or seven-day post-obturation pain [[PMID:26272704]]. AbdurRahman et al.'s RCT in necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis (n=44) found triple antibiotic paste dressing did not reduce postoperative pain compared with single-visit treatment, undermining a common rationale for the multi-visit approach in this specific indication [[PMID:32047601]].

**Level II — Prospective Studies**

The Wong et al. prospective cohorts are the same trials cited above and are absorbed into the Cochrane pooled estimates [[PMID:26687126]] [[PMID:26272704]]. A recent US survey of 462 endodontists is descriptive rather than comparative, but shows that 74.9% routinely complete necrotic cases single-visit and 92.4% for irreversible pulpitis, with single-visit rates declining sharply when canals cannot be dried (7.4%) or diffuse swelling is present (20.1%) — reflecting the clinical carve-outs contemporary practitioners already apply [[PMID:42214667]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort Studies**

Kirci and Karataslioglu retrospectively analysed 62 mandibular molars and found no difference in periapical healing (PAI scores) or fractal dimension changes at 6 and 12 months between single-visit and multi-visit groups (with either calcium hydroxide or chlorhexidine dressing) [[PMID:41460772]]. Albashaireh and Alnegrish's earlier prospective cohort (n=291) reported the opposite of the modern signal — higher 24-hour post-obturation pain in the multiple-visit group (38% vs 27%), particularly in non-vital teeth — reminding that primary study results in this literature are directionally inconsistent and the Cochrane pooled estimate is the more reliable summary [[PMID:9594474]].

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews**

Bryce et al.'s critical appraisal of the Cochrane review reaches the same clinical bottom line: single-visit RCT is no less effective than multi-visit at 12 months, with the caveat of increased one-week post-operative pain [[PMID:37188920]].
