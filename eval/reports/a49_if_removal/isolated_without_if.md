## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Cochrane-level evidence, single-visit and multiple-visit root canal treatment produce equivalent radiographic healing and long-term outcomes for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis, so either protocol is defensible and the choice can be driven by case-specific factors (canal dryness, ability to control infection, patient tolerance for a longer appointment) [[PMID:36512807]]. Clinicians should counsel patients that single-visit treatment carries a modestly higher risk of pain within the first week and possibly flare-up, while offering shorter overall chairside time [[PMID:36512807]] [[PMID:28148534]]. When canals cannot be adequately dried, acute apical abscess with diffuse swelling is present, or heavy infection persists after chemomechanical preparation, a multiple-visit approach with an interappointment calcium hydroxide dressing is the more prudent choice.

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**

The 2022 Cochrane review by Mergoni et al. (47 RCTs, 5,805 participants, 5,693 teeth) is the highest-tier evidence and directly addresses this question [[PMID:36512807]]. It found no difference between single- and multiple-visit protocols in radiological failure (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81–1.07; 13 studies, 1,505 teeth; moderate certainty), tooth extraction, swelling/flare-up, analgesic use, or sinus tract persistence. However, moderate-certainty evidence showed a higher proportion of patients reporting pain within one week after single-visit treatment (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.14–2.09), with subgroup analysis showing this signal was most pronounced in vital teeth (RR 2.16) and with mechanical instrumentation (RR 1.80). Importantly, the review's outcomes for necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis specifically were not separated as a distinct subgroup, but the aggregate finding of equivalent radiographic healing applies to the mixed necrotic/vital population studied.

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

Schwendicke and Göstemeyer's meta-analysis (29 trials, 4,341 patients) with trial-sequential analysis reached a similar conclusion regarding equivalence for long-term complications (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.75–1.35) and short-term pain (RR 0.99), but flagged a significantly higher risk of flare-up after single-visit treatment (RR 2.13, 95% CI 1.16–3.89, though rated very weak evidence) — leading the authors to suggest multiple-visit treatment "might be preferred for certain teeth (eg, those with periapical lesions)" [[PMID:28148534]]. This differs modestly from the Cochrane conclusion, which found no flare-up signal. Wong et al.'s RCT (n=220, mean follow-up 29.4 months) reported success rates of 88.9% (single-visit) vs 87.4% (multiple-visit), p=0.729, with single-visit averaging 31 minutes less chairside time [[PMID:26687126]]. Their companion two-centre RCT (n=567) found no difference in post-obturation pain at 1 day (24.7% vs 33.5%) or 7 days (4.0% vs 5.3%) [[PMID:26272704]]. AbdurRahman et al. (n=44) specifically studied necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis and found no difference in postoperative pain between single-visit and multiple-visit with triple antibiotic paste [[PMID:32047601]]. Almeida et al.'s meta-analysis of nonvital teeth actually found *less* post-obturation pain with single-visit (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66–0.94), contradicting the Cochrane pain-within-one-week finding [[PMID:28349914]] — this discrepancy likely reflects different inclusion criteria and timeframes.

**Level II — Prospective Studies**

The prospective arm largely overlaps with the RCT evidence above. A recent US survey by Tsai and Kim (n=462 endodontists) documented that 74.9% routinely complete necrotic teeth in a single visit, with multiple-visit strongly preferred when canals cannot be dried (92.6%) or diffuse swelling is present (79.9%), and single-visit completion declining as lesion size increases (60.4% at ≥5 mm; 37.9% at ≥15 mm) [[PMID:42214667]]. This describes practice patterns rather than establishing efficacy but reflects contemporary clinical judgment about when the two approaches are not interchangeable.

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort Studies**

Kirci and Karataslioglu's 12-month retrospective analysis of 62 mandibular molars compared multiple-visit with Ca(OH)₂, multiple-visit with chlorhexidine gel, and single-visit without medicament, using PAI scores and fractal dimension analysis of periapical bone [[PMID:41460772]]. All three groups showed significant reduction in PAI and improvement in fractal dimension at 6 and 12 months with no intergroup differences, supporting equivalent healing regardless of protocol or intracanal medicament. Albashaireh and Alnegrish's earlier prospective series (n=291) reported the opposite pain finding — significantly *higher* postobturation pain in the multiple-visit group (38% vs 27%) within 24 hours [[PMID:9594474]] — again showing that pain outcomes are inconsistent across studies and depend heavily on the timing of assessment.

**Level IIIb — Case-Control Studies**

No additional case-control evidence changes the picture; the Kirci study is duplicated in this tier.

**Level IV — Case Series**

Asgary and Ahmadyar reported successful one-visit RCT of three maxillary incisors with periradicular lesions and inflammatory external root resorption, with complete radiographic healing at 14 months [[PMID:23130060]] — supporting feasibility but underpowered for any comparative conclusion.

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews**

Bryce et al.'s critical summary of the 2022 Cochrane review reinforces its conclusions and highlights the one-week pain signal as the only clinically meaningful difference between protocols [[PMID:37188920]].
