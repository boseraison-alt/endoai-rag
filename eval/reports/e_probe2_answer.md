## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Level I evidence, perform **partial (Cvek) pulpotomy** using a hydraulic calcium silicate cement (MTA or Biodentine), followed immediately by a well-sealed definitive restoration. For a complicated crown fracture in a mature premolar, this vital pulp therapy approach is preferred over direct pulp capping, with partial pulpotomy success reported as 82.9–100% versus 19.5% for direct pulp capping in traumatised permanent teeth [[PMID:35348216]]. Pulpotomy is significantly more successful than DPC for traumatic pulp exposures (P ≤ 0.001), root maturity does not affect pulpotomy success, and haemostasis within 6 minutes likely improves outcomes [[PMID:40533920]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

The strongest evidence for managing complicated crown fractures comes from vital pulp therapy (VPT) systematic reviews. Matoug-Elwerfelli et al. reviewed 1,081 traumatised permanent teeth and reported partial pulpotomy success of 82.9–100%, complete pulpotomy 79.4–85.7%, and direct pulp capping only 19.5% — a striking gradient favouring pulpotomy over capping in trauma cases [[PMID:35348216]]. Donnelly et al., in a systematic review of pulpotomy for complicated crown fractures, found partial and complete pulpotomy success ranging 75–96%, and in one included study partial pulpotomy substantially outperformed pulp capping (90.9% vs 67%); they concluded partial pulpotomy should be the treatment of choice for both mature and immature teeth with complicated crown fractures [[PMID:35076954]]. Tzanetakis et al.'s meta-analysis found no significant difference between MTA and calcium hydroxide, or between bioceramic and CaOH₂, for pulp survival after VPT for complicated crown fracture, though quality of evidence was very low to moderate [[PMID:35090933]]. Coll et al.'s 2025 systematic review confirms that partial/full pulpotomy is significantly more successful than DPC for traumatic pulp exposures (P ≤ 0.001), that root maturity does not affect pulpotomy success, and that Biodentine causes significantly less discolouration than MTA — a relevant consideration if the fractured premolar is visible [[PMID:40533920]]. Abuelniel et al.'s RCT confirmed comparable clinical/radiographic outcomes for MTA and Biodentine in traumatised immature teeth, with significantly more discolouration in the MTA arm [[PMID:32096595]].

**Classic / Foundational**

Cvek's 1978 seminal work established partial pulpotomy with calcium hydroxide as a technique for complicated crown fractures in permanent incisors, forming the biological basis for the modern procedure [[PMID:283188]].

**Level II — Prospective Studies**

Tan et al. showed pulpotomy with a tricalcium silicate cement in mature permanent teeth achieved 90.2% pulp survival, with appropriate coronal restoration a key predictor of long-term success [[PMID:31902641]]. Wang et al.'s cohort of 603 uncomplicated crown fractures identified concurrent luxation as a major risk factor for pulp necrosis, and most necrosis occurred within 3 months — reinforcing the need for follow-up regardless of treatment chosen [[PMID:24571370]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort**

Bissinger et al., in 616 crown fractures, found no superiority of MTA over calcium hydroxide as pulp capping agent and reported 85.5% of complications appeared within 2 years [[PMID:32705398]]. Wang et al. (n=375 complicated crown fractures) found partial and coronal pulpotomy had equivalent success (~90%), and both were dramatically superior to direct pulp capping (odds ratio 8.2 for DPC failure); the time interval between injury and treatment did not significantly influence pulpotomy outcome, whereas root maturity did [[PMID:28235162]]. Shahmohammadi et al. reported cervical pulpotomy 90.4% and partial pulpotomy 85.2% success in immature crown-fractured teeth, versus only 47.2% for restoration alone [[PMID:34478786]]. Hu et al. showed comparable long-term (5-year) success for WMTA (94.4%) and iRoot BP Plus (97.7%) as partial pulpotomy agents, with universal discolouration in the MTA group and none in the bioceramic group [[PMID:36409268]].

**Level IIIb — Case-Control**

Cheng et al., in 307 crown-fractured teeth with a 23-month median follow-up, reported complicated crown fractures treated with pulpotomy had 93.6% success, higher than uncomplicated crown fractures overall (85.4%); pulpotomy significantly outperformed indirect pulp capping (hazard ratio 13.5 for IPC failure) [[PMID:37916540]].

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews**

Duncan et al. review pulpotomy prognostic factors and conclude that patient age, gender, tooth type, root development, and intraoperative haemorrhage do not significantly affect outcome, whereas capping material (calcium silicate > calcium hydroxide) and final restoration quality do [[PMID:36334098]]. Duncan's broader review emphasises that with modern hydraulic calcium silicate cements, VPT has become the preferred approach for cariously and traumatically exposed pulps in mature teeth [[PMID:35080024]].
