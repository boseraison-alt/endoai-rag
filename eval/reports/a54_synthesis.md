## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on Level I evidence, MTA and newer bioceramic root-end filling materials (EndoSequence RRM, BioMA) produce equivalent clinical and radiographic success following apicoectomy, with no significant difference in healed/failed outcomes by von Arx criteria (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.94–1.07) [[PMID:40637350]]. Either material is a defensible choice; bioceramics are positioned as a promising alternative to MTA with superior sealing and biocompatibility versus older materials such as amalgam or IRM [[PMID:40464307]]. Use ultrasonic root-end preparation under microscope magnification regardless of material chosen — this technique is associated with a significantly higher pooled success rate (94.4%) than resin-based surgery approaches [[PMID:29681480]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**

The 2016 Cochrane review synthesised 20 RCTs on retreatment of periapical lesions and remains the highest-tier evidence available [[PMID:27759881]]. It reported better healing when root-ends were filled with MTA compared with smoothing of orthograde gutta-percha after apicoectomy (RR 1.60, 95% CI 1.14–2.24; one RCT, 46 participants), but categorised the evidence as low quality [[PMID:27759881]]. The review did not identify high-quality RCTs directly comparing MTA to newer bioceramic putties, leaving that comparison to be addressed by more recent Level I evidence.

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**

Two 2025 systematic reviews directly address the MTA-versus-bioceramic question in root-end filling. One pooled 5 studies (3 RCTs, 2 retrospective cohorts) comparing new bioceramics (predominantly EndoSequence RRM, one BioMA) against MTA using von Arx criteria and found no significant difference in success (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.94–1.07) or failure (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.51–1.71); GRADE certainty was moderate, and bioceramics were characterised as non-inferior with improved handling [[PMID:40637350]]. A separate review of 19 clinical studies of bioceramics in retrograde apicectomy concluded that MTA, bioceramic putty, and premixed hydraulic silicate cements demonstrate superior sealing and biocompatibility versus amalgam/IRM, positioning bioceramics as a promising standard but calling for longer-term controlled data [[PMID:40464307]]. A meta-analysis of endodontic microsurgery outcomes reported 94.4% pooled success for modern endodontic microsurgery using MTA, SuperEBA, IRM, or calcium silicate cements — significantly higher than resin-based root-end surgery (82.2%) [[PMID:29681480]]. An earlier systematic review found MTA superior to amalgam and gutta-percha alone but similar to IRM as root-end filling [[PMID:20614042]]. A review of physicochemical evidence comparing Biodentine and MTA concluded there is insufficient evidence for clinical superiority of either as a root-end filling material [[PMID:33340080]].

**Classic / Foundational (San Antonio Guide)**

A foundational review established that management of the resected root end — root outline, canal anatomy, smear layer, and material selection — collectively determines periradicular healing [[PMID:8300259]]. A canine study demonstrated that root-end demineralisation (removal of the smear layer) accelerated cementogenesis and dentoalveolar healing [[PMID:8245756]]. These are context for material selection but predate contemporary bioceramic formulations.

**Level II — Prospective Studies**

Longitudinal outcome data for MTA as root-end filling come from a series of prospective cohorts. At 1 year, MTA-filled teeth healed significantly more often (91.3%) than Retroplast-adhesive-composite (79.5%) across 353 consecutive cases [[PMID:20630283]]. At 5 years the overall rate of healed cases was 75.9%, and ProRoot MTA outperformed SuperEBA (86.4% vs 67.3% healed) as an independent predictor [[PMID:22515881]]. At 10 years, 81.5% of 119 MTA-treated teeth remained healed, with most losses due to longitudinal root fracture rather than endodontic failure [[PMID:30827766]]. For bioceramic root repair material (BCRRM putty), a 1-year prospective cohort of 170 teeth reported 94.1% success, comparable to historical MTA cohorts [[PMID:32267138]]. A micro-CT comparison of MTA Angelus versus MTA Flow found comparable void and gap volumes after ultrasonic or Er:YAG laser root-end cavity preparation [[PMID:40616059]]. A prospective RCT reported comparable healing between Dia-Root BioMTA and glass ionomer cement over 12 months in 40 patients [[PMID:41379411]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort**

The Buffalo cohort of 101 endodontic microsurgery cases (mostly MTA/bioceramic root-end fillings) reported 88% radiographic success on periapical films and 86% on CBCT, with major procedural error the only predictor of failure — material choice within the calcium silicate family did not independently affect outcome [[PMID:32975855]]. A separate cohort of 197 teeth treated with calcium-enriched mixture (CEM) as root-end filling achieved 94.9% success at a mean 45 months, with systemic disease the only significant predictor of failure [[PMID:41731450]].

**Level IIIb / Level III (legacy retrospective)**

Legacy prospective data (n=122) showed no significant difference between MTA and IRM as root-end filling materials at 12 and 24 months (92% vs 87%) [[PMID:12887380]]. This is consistent with the Level I pooled finding of equivalence within the calcium silicate / zinc oxide-eugenol reinforced family.

**In Vitro / Ex Vivo — Bench Studies (not clinical evidence)**

Bench work supports the sealing and biomineralisation rationale for calcium silicate materials but does not establish clinical outcome differences. A micro-CT comparison of MTA Angelus, Biodentine, and Neo MTA Plus found no significant differences in marginal gaps or internal voids after root-end filling [[PMID:34787245]]. A systematic review of marginal adaptation studies found MTA generally comparable to other root-end materials, with no in vivo studies identified and substantial methodological heterogeneity [[PMID:29450937]]. A prognostic review identified MTA as a root-end filling material as one of several factors associated with better periapical surgery outcomes, alongside anterior/premolar location, small lesion size, and absence of periodontal involvement [[PMID:26449431]].

What the retrieved literature does establish is that outcomes between MTA and newer bioceramics are statistically equivalent [[PMID:40637350]], and technique (microscope, ultrasonics, adequate preparation) is a dominant driver of success [[PMID:29681480]] [[PMID:32975855]].

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews**

Reviews consistently identify MTA as the historical reference material and bioceramic putties as viable alternatives, with the primary differentiation being handling properties rather than clinical outcome [[PMID:33340080]] [[PMID:31189792]].
