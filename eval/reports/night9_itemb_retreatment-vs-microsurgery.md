## CLINICAL RECOMMENDATION

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Based on Level I evidence, no treatment option has demonstrated clear superiority: apical surgery tends to show better short-to-medium-term periapical healing, while nonsurgical retreatment (NSRT) tends to show better long-term tooth survival [[PMID:35762859]] [[PMID:27759881]]. The decision should therefore be case-specific: choose NSRT when the existing root filling is inadequate, the coronal restoration must be replaced, or the canal system is accessible for redisinfection; choose endodontic microsurgery (EMS) when the root filling and coronal restoration are technically satisfactory, when a post/crown makes disassembly risky, or when a previous well-executed retreatment has failed [FDSRCS — Periradicular Surgery (2020; current; UK)] [AAE — Treatment Standards White Paper (with 2019 Executive Summary) (2018; current; US)]. With contemporary microsurgical technique and MTA/bioceramic root-end fillings, EMS achieves 1-year success ~91% and long-term (2–13 yr) pooled success ~78–91% [[PMID:32899437]] [[PMID:22515881]], while modern NSRT achieves ~80% periapical success and ~95% 4-year tooth survival [[PMID:21366626]] [[PMID:21366627]].

## EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Cochrane Reviews**
The Del Fabbro Cochrane review (2016) directly addressed surgical versus non-surgical retreatment and found no clear superiority of either approach for periapical healing at 1-year (RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.97–1.35; 2 RCTs, 126 participants) or at 4- and 10-year follow-up, though the evidence was rated very low quality [[PMID:27759881]]. More postoperative pain in the first week was reported after surgery (RR 3.34, 95% CI 2.05–5.43). The review concluded that available evidence does not provide reliable guidelines for choosing between the two modalities.

**Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews**
Bucchi et al. systematically reviewed five comparative studies (2 RCTs, 2 non-randomised trials, 1 retrospective cohort; 529 teeth, follow-up 6 months to 8.7 years) and reported that apical surgery appeared to give better periapical healing and fewer subsequent interventions, whereas tooth survival was higher in the nonsurgical retreatment group; meta-analysis was not possible due to heterogeneity and no treatment showed clear superiority [[PMID:35762859]]. This is the most focused synthesis directly addressing the PICO question and its bottom line — no clear winner, differential outcomes by endpoint — mirrors the Cochrane conclusion. The updated systematic review by Burns et al. estimated pooled primary RCT success at 92.6% (loose) and 82.0% (strict) [[PMID:35334111]], and the CBCT-based review by Brochado Martins et al. found similar pooled healing rates for treatment and retreatment (~84–87% loose; 36–45% strict), with number of visits, irrigant type, and operator experience as significant predictors [[PMID:40122230]].

**Classic / Foundational**
Sjögren et al.'s landmark 8–10-year study established that only 62% of previously root-filled teeth with periapical lesions healed after nonsurgical retreatment, versus 86% for primary treatment of necrotic teeth with lesions [[PMID:2084204]]. Moiseiwitsch and Trope emphasised that historical apical surgery success without proper canal debridement was only 50–60%, supporting thorough orthograde treatment before considering surgery [[PMID:9768425]]. These foundational data underpin the traditional principle that NSRT should generally be the first-line approach when technically feasible.

**Level II — Prospective Studies**
Ng et al.'s large prospective cohort of 1314 retreated roots reported 80% periapical healing after nonsurgical retreatment (comparable to 83% for primary treatment) and 95% 4-year tooth survival [[PMID:21366626]] [[PMID:21366627]]. For apical microsurgery, von Arx et al. followed 191 teeth prospectively and reported healing dropping from 84% at 1 year to 76% at 5 years, with MTA root-end fillings significantly outperforming SuperEBA [[PMID:22515881]]. de Chevigny et al. (Toronto Study, phases 3–4) reported 82% healing after orthograde retreatment at 4–6 years, with radiolucency, perforation, and root filling quality as key predictors [[PMID:18215667]].

**Level IIIa — Retrospective Cohort**
Long-term retrospective data support both modalities. López-Valverde et al. observed 5–37-year survival of 68% at 37 years for primary treatment, with periapical lesion presence a key negative predictor [[PMID:36933044]]. Azim et al.'s Buffalo study of 101 EMS cases reported 88% CBCT-verified success and 93% survival, with major procedural error the only significant negative predictor [[PMID:32975855]]. Pinto et al.'s meta-analysis of long-term EMS outcomes reported pooled success of 91.3% in RCTs and 78.4% in prospective cohorts across 2–13 year follow-up [[PMID:32899437]]. Olivieri et al. reported 80.6% success (strict) and 93% (loose) for NSRT at 2–3 years, with molars and large lesions (>5 mm) showing lower success [[PMID:37315780]]. Artaza et al. reported 89% (loose) and 67% (rigid) success for initial NSRT of teeth with large lesions, and 79%/47% for retreatment [[PMID:39151889]].

**Level IIIb — Case-Control**
Turan Gökduman et al.'s 5-year retrospective study of 408 nonsurgically retreated teeth reported 73.5% survival and 79% success among surviving teeth, with coronal restoration quality and initial obturation quality the principal predictors [[PMID:41062676]]. Baltieri et al. reported 92.5% success (loose) at ~30 months for NSRT of teeth with persistent apical periodontitis using foraminal enlargement and 2% chlorhexidine [[PMID:39313095]].

**Level IV — Case Series**
Case reports of guided endodontic microsurgery with minimally invasive access and biocompatible root-end materials have shown complete healing at 4-year follow-up, suggesting that contemporary techniques continue to broaden the surgical envelope for complex apicoectomy [[PMID:39162044]].

**Specialty Guidelines & Position Statements**
The FDSRCS guidelines for periradicular surgery (2020) frame apical surgery as indicated when orthograde retreatment is not feasible, has failed, or when disassembly of a satisfactory coronal restoration/post would compromise the tooth [FDSRCS — Periradicular Surgery (2020; current; UK)]. The AAE Treatment Standards (2018/2019) similarly position surgery as complementary rather than competitive with NSRT, with case selection driven by the cause of persistent disease and restorative considerations [AAE — Treatment Standards White Paper (with 2019 Executive Summary) (2018; current; US)]. The ESE S3-level clinical practice guideline (2023) provides evidence-graded recommendations across the endodontic decision pathway and stresses the importance of case-specific analysis and aseptic technique [[PMID:37772327]].

**Level V — Expert Opinion / Reviews**
The Chinese expert consensus on apical microsurgery (Wang et al., 2025) reports success rates exceeding 90% for contemporary microsurgery and specifies indications where orthograde retreatment is inappropriate or has failed [[PMID:39743567]]. Kim and Kratchman's foundational review of modern microsurgery describes how magnification, ultrasonic retropreparation, and biocompatible root-end fillings have transformed apical surgery from a salvage procedure to a predictable option [[PMID:16793466]].
