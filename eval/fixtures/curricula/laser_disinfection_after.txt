# Use of Lasers in Root Canal Disinfection
### A 20-Minute Endodontic Teaching Curriculum

---

## OVERVIEW

Laser-assisted root canal disinfection has moved from experimental curiosity to a clinically accessible adjunct, yet its evidence base remains sharply tiered: photoacoustic streaming with pulsed erbium systems demonstrably reduces postoperative pain [[PMID:40818665]], while periapical healing superiority over conventional chemo-mechanical preparation has not been confirmed at any GRADE level above low certainty [[PMID:36156804]]. This curriculum distils four interconnected modules — laser physics, biofilm removal efficacy, protocol application, and clinical outcomes — into operationally actionable guidance. By the end, clinicians will be able to select the appropriate laser modality and parameters for a given case, interpret the hierarchy of supporting evidence critically, and apply or withhold laser adjuncts based on the outcome being targeted rather than equipment availability alone.

---

## Module 1 — Laser Physics and Mechanisms in Endodontics

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** The 2026 PROSPERO-registered umbrella review by Duarte et al. (CRD42025599352), synthesising 9 systematic reviews across Er:YAG, Er,Cr:YSGG, Nd:YAG, and diode systems, concluded that evidence on laser-activated irrigation (LAI) "remains limited and heterogeneous" and that LAI "should currently be regarded as an adjunct with unproven clinical benefit" [[PMID:41833582]]. Conversely, individual systematic reviews and meta-analyses report statistically significant antimicrobial effects for specific wavelengths and parameters. Both positions are presented below, with the higher-tier umbrella review serving as the lead conclusion.

**Laser Wavelengths and Tissue Interactions**

Every dental laser used in endodontics is defined by its wavelength, which dictates chromophore absorption and therefore tissue interaction. Four systems dominate the literature:

- **Er:YAG (2940 nm):** Highest absorption by water and hydroxyapatite; generates photoacoustic streaming and cavitation within irrigant columns [[PMID:40136729]]
- **Er,Cr:YSGG (2780 nm):** Also strongly absorbed by water and hydroxyapatite; produces photoacoustic streaming, cavitation-like fluid movement, and localised photothermal effects within the root canal system [[PMID:42306383]]
- **Nd:YAG (1064 nm):** Primarily photothermal; penetrates dentinal tubules and denatures microbial proteins and disrupts cell membranes [[PMID:40565094]]
- **Diode lasers (808–980 nm):** Absorbed by pigmented tissues and haemoglobin; predominantly photothermal with photobiomodulation capability at lower powers [[PMID:41833582]]

**Mechanisms of Antimicrobial Action**

Three distinct physical mechanisms underlie laser-mediated canal disinfection:

1. **Photothermal effects** — Nd:YAG and diode wavelengths generate localised heat sufficient to denature bacterial proteins and disrupt lipopolysaccharide-containing cell membranes [[PMID:40565094]]
2. **Photoacoustic streaming (PIPS/SWEEPS)** — Pulsed erbium lasers create rapidly expanding and imploding vapour bubbles; the resultant cavitation drives intense liquid dynamics throughout the canal, forcing irrigant into lateral canals and dentinal tubules [[PMID:38340037]]
3. **Photochemical / reactive-oxygen-species (ROS) generation** — In antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT), a photosensitiser absorbs laser energy and generates ROS that oxidatively damage bacterial membranes [[PMID:31809911]] [[PMID:40485280]]

**Erbium Laser Efficacy — Quantified Evidence**

A systematic review and meta-analysis (Ghahari et al., 2024; n = 565 samples) demonstrated that erbium lasers produced a standardised mean difference of −0.551 (95% CI: −0.656 to −0.445; I² = 23.89%; p = 0.031) for biofilm reduction in root canals [[PMID:38662505]]. Peak efficacy was recorded at 2940 nm, 75–100 mJ energy, 100–150 µs pulse duration, and < 50 Hz pulse frequency [[PMID:38662505]]. These parameters are not yet standardised across clinical protocols — a limitation the umbrella review explicitly flags [[PMID:41833582]].

**Nd:YAG Penetration and Limitations**

The systematic review by Fiegler-Rudol et al. (2025) confirmed that Nd:YAG lasers achieve significant reductions in total microbial load via photothermal denaturation and dentinal tubule penetration, but complete sterilisation was not consistently achieved in any included experimental study [[PMID:40565094]]. This recommendation rests on experimental and preclinical studies only — no large-scale RCT has evaluated Nd:YAG as a standalone disinfection adjunct with validated clinical endpoints; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**Laser Ablation with Indocyanine Green (ICG)**

An RCT by Leonardo et al. (2023; n = 60) showed that laser ablation therapy with ICG produced statistically significant additional microbial reduction beyond chemomechanical preparation alone (p < .05), regardless of whether 2.5% NaOCl or saline was used as the primary irrigant [[PMID:37355163]]. An in vitro study (Dourado et al., 2025) further demonstrated that ICG-mediated laser ablation reduced colony-forming units to levels comparable to 2.5% NaOCl and induced the highest oxidative stress markers among tested photodynamic agents [[PMID:41465429]].

**Clinical Cavitation Mechanics (LAI)**

Meire and De Moor (2024) explain that cavitation is the primary driving force of LAI: pulsed laser energy creates vapour bubbles that grow and implode near the fibre tip, generating secondary phenomena — including acoustic streaming and microjet formation — that distribute irrigant throughout canal irregularities inaccessible to files [[PMID:38340037]]. Wavelength, pulse energy, pulse duration, and fibre-tip geometry all modulate cavitation intensity, yet no consensus parameter set exists [[PMID:38340037]] [[PMID:41833582]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Complete chemomechanical preparation first.** Shape to a minimum apical size of 25/.04 before any laser activation; laser adjuncts do not replace mechanical preparation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base.

2. **Select irrigant concentration.** Use 2% NaOCl for LAI-assisted retreatment [[PMID:40135760]] or 2.5% NaOCl for primary treatment [[PMID:37355163]]. Duarte et al. did not report a consensus irrigant concentration across included reviews [[PMID:41833582]].

3. **For Er:YAG LAI: set parameters to 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs pulse duration; use a 300 µm fibre tip.** These are the parameters used in the Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. RCT (n = 40), yielding a fluence of 71.4 J/cm² and power density of 1,428.6 W/cm² [[PMID:40135760]].

4. **Deliver Er:YAG LAI with 2% NaOCl, followed by 17% EDTA activation.** Both irrigants were activated sequentially in the Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. protocol; EDTA contact time was not separately specified in that study [[PMID:40135760]].

5. **For aPDT with ICG: apply ICG photosensitiser then activate with infrared diode laser.** Leonardo et al. did not report the ICG concentration or diode power output — those parameters must be sourced from the device manufacturer's validated protocol [[PMID:37355163]].

6. **For aPDT with methylene blue: use 0.005% methylene blue solution; irradiate at 660 nm, 100 mW, for 90 s (total energy 9.0 J); insert a 300 µm light diffusor 2 mm short of working length** [[PMID:30797116]]. This recommendation rests on a single case report (Amaral et al., 2019 [[PMID:30797116]]) with 60-month follow-up on one tooth — no RCT has evaluated these specific aPDT parameters; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

7. **Apply photobiomodulation (PBM) post-operatively if a periapical lesion is present.** Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. used 635 nm, 400 mW, 5 s per point, 2 J dose per point, 4 J/cm², 8 mm applicator diameter, at 2 apical points over 4 sessions (day 0, 24 h, 48 h, 96 h) [[PMID:40135760]].

8. **Obturate with thermoplasticised gutta-percha and AH Plus epoxy resin sealer.** This was the obturation method used in the validating RCT [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the tooth is undergoing primary root canal treatment with intact canal anatomy and no periapical lesion
**THEN** Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 300 µm tip) with 2.5% NaOCl is an acceptable adjunct after shaping
**BECAUSE** LAI may improve irrigant distribution in inaccessible areas, though the umbrella review confirms clinical benefit remains unproven [[PMID:41833582]]

**IF** the tooth requires endodontic retreatment with a chronic periapical lesion
**THEN** use Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 µs, 300 µm tip) with 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA, followed by post-operative 635 nm PBM over 4 sessions
**BECAUSE** this combined protocol produced significantly smaller periapical lesion size at 6 months (mean 1.55 ± 0.51 mm vs. 1.95 ± 0.71 mm in control, p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]]

**IF** residual bacteria persist after chemomechanical preparation and conventional irrigation in a single-rooted tooth
**THEN** add LA therapy with ICG using an infrared diode laser as a further adjunct
**BECAUSE** ICG-mediated LA produced significant additional CFU reduction beyond preparation and 2.5% NaOCl irrigation alone (p < .05) [[PMID:37355163]]

**IF** the canal anatomy is severely calcified or the fibre tip cannot be positioned safely near working length
**THEN** do not proceed with LAI; revert to conventional needle irrigation with ultrasonic activation
**BECAUSE** tip position directly governs cavitation efficacy and thermal safety; Meire and De Moor reported that fibre tip geometry is a primary determinant of cavitation outcome but did not report safe parameters for obstructed canals [[PMID:38340037]]

**IF** the clinician lacks a calibrated laser unit with verified output parameters
**THEN** do not use laser adjuncts; uncalibrated use risks thermal damage without confirmed antimicrobial benefit
**BECAUSE** current evidence requires specific energy/frequency settings and no standardised protocol exists [[PMID:41833582]]

## Module 2 — Microbial Biofilm Removal and Disinfection Efficacy

**Why biofilm makes standard irrigation insufficient.** Endodontic biofilm is a structured, multispecies community anchored to root canal walls and embedded within dentinal tubules, lateral canals, isthmuses, and apical deltas — anatomical refuges that a conventional side-vented needle cannot reliably reach. Laser-activated irrigation (LAI) addresses this by harnessing cavitation: pulsed laser energy generates rapidly expanding and imploding vapour bubbles around the fibre tip, producing intense fluid dynamics that drive irrigant throughout the underlying root canal [[PMID:38340037]]. This photoacoustic streaming mechanism is the physical basis for LAI's superior penetration into complex anatomy compared with passive needle delivery [[PMID:38340037]].

**LAI vs. other activation techniques — disinfection efficacy.** In vitro evidence consistently favours LAI for biofilm and smear layer removal. Donnermeyer et al., 2024 demonstrated in a 3D-printed model with isthmuses (2.5 × 0.4 × 0.2 mm) and lateral canals (0.2 mm diameter) that Er:YAG LAI — using a P400FL tip at canal entrance, 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 μs, continuous irrigation with 3 mL per 20 s for 3 × 20 s per canal — achieved the greatest overall hydrogel removal from the entire root canal system compared with needle irrigation, manual agitation, EndoActivator, EDDY sonic, and passive ultrasonic irrigation [[PMID:38382735]]. Abaza et al., 2025 found that Er,Cr:YSGG LAI (25 mJ, 50 Hz, radial-firing tip; four 15-second activation cycles) achieved the lowest smear layer scores across all root thirds when combined with NaOCl + EDTA, outperforming both diode LAI and UAI; notably, Er,Cr:YSGG with saline alone outperformed NaOCl without activation, indicating that mechanical cavitation can dominate chemical action [[PMID:41389357]]. A systematic review on the GentleWave system found that Er:YAG lasers produced comparable debris removal to GentleWave, while GentleWave achieved higher LPS/LTA reductions than UAI alone, underscoring that advanced activation outperforms passive delivery [[PMID:41514415]].

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Regarding whether LAI and other adjunct therapies translate into *improved periapical healing* at 12 months, two high-tier sources conflict. Meire et al., 2023 (ESE-commissioned systematic review, GRADE assessment, 13 RCTs + 1 cohort) concluded there is insufficient evidence to recommend any adjunctive laser therapy for healing of apical periodontitis — GRADE certainty was very low for diode laser and low for aPDT [[PMID:36156804]]. In contrast, Doğan et al., 2024 (RCT, n=56, 12-month CBCT volumetry) found that LAI-SWEEPS produced 86.9% periapical lesion volume reduction vs. 74.5% in the no-activation control, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05) [[PMID:38878107]]. The Meire et al. systematic review carries higher GRADE certainty and represents the current state of the evidence: it found insufficient evidence to recommend any adjunctive laser therapy for AP healing, while trends from individual RCTs remain biologically plausible [[PMID:36156804]].

**Antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT).** aPDT uses a photosensitiser activated by visible light to generate reactive oxygen species lethal to biofilm organisms. Das et al., 2025 (meta-analysis, 11 RCTs, GRADE assessment) reported that aPDT as an adjunct to chemo-mechanical irrigation significantly reduced postoperative pain at 24 h (SMD: −1.21) and 72 h (SMD: −0.63), and narrative findings across six studies suggested microbial load reduction, though heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis on that outcome [[PMID:41388259]]. Coelho et al., 2019 specifically used methylene blue at 1.56 μM/mL with an optical fibre applied for 180 seconds (P = 100 mW, E = 18 J) delivered to working length, with canals instrumented under 2.5% NaOCl irrigation, and found significantly lower postoperative pain VAS scores at 24 h and 72 h compared with controls [[PMID:31301436]]. Meire et al., 2023 found low-certainty GRADE evidence for aPDT and no statistically significant difference in 12-month radiographic healing versus controls [[PMID:36156804]], so aPDT offers adjunctive pain-reduction and antimicrobial benefit but cannot yet be said to independently improve periapical healing outcomes.

**Laser type matters.** Sabeti et al., 2026 (meta-analysis, 7 RCTs, n=490 teeth) showed LAI overall significantly reduced postoperative pain versus UAI (SMD: −0.58; 95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; p = 0.0016); subgroup analysis identified pulsed Er:YAG modalities as superior (PIPS: SMD −1.10; SWEEPS: SMD −1.57 at 24–48 h), while diode LAI showed no significant effect compared with UAI (SMD: 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. On the basis of this meta-analysis, diode LAI cannot be considered equivalent to Er:YAG for pain reduction relative to UAI [[PMID:40818665]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Complete chemo-mechanical shaping first.** Shape to a minimum apical preparation of #25/.06 taper before any activation step; the LAI model studies used 25/.06 preparations to allow tip insertion and irrigant flow [[PMID:38382735]].

2. **Primary irrigation with NaOCl throughout shaping.** Deliver via side-vented needle throughout instrumentation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. Coelho et al. used 2.5% NaOCl during canal instrumentation in their RCT [[PMID:31301436]]; the choice between 2.5% and 5.25% for necrotic cases is standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base.

3. **Er:YAG LAI activation of NaOCl — first cycle.** Fill canal with NaOCl. Insert P400FL fibre (400 μm diameter) at canal entrance — not to working length. Activate at 50 mJ, 25 pps, 300 μs pulse duration for 3 × 20 seconds per canal [[PMID:38382735]]. Repeat the sequence for each canal individually.

4. **EDTA flush — smear layer removal.** Irrigate with 17% EDTA; activate with Er:YAG LAI at 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs pulse duration (fluence 71.4 J/cm², power density 1,428.6 W/cm², 300 μm tip diameter) for one activation sequence per canal [[PMID:40135760]].

5. **Final NaOCl activation cycle.** Refill canal with NaOCl and perform one additional LAI sequence (3 × 20 s at 50 mJ, 25 pps, 300 μs) to flush EDTA and mobilised debris [[PMID:38382735]].

6. **aPDT adjunct (for necrotic or retreatment cases).** Flood canal with methylene blue photosensitiser at 1.56 μM/mL. Insert optical fibre to working length. Irradiate for 180 seconds at 100 mW output (total energy = 18 J) [[PMID:31301436]]. Alternatively, Toluidine Blue at 0.1 mg/mL (FotoSan® carrier) activated at 630 nm, 3 W/cm² has been reported in case-level evidence [[PMID:41789272]] — no RCT-level head-to-head comparison of these two photosensitisers was identified in the retrieved evidence base.

7. **Dry and obturate.** Paper-point dry to working length; obturate with thermoplastic gutta-percha and epoxy resin–based sealer. Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. used thermocondensed GP with AH Plus sealer following their Er:YAG LAI + 17% EDTA protocol [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**Branch 1 — Standard primary RCT, vital pulp, no periapical lesion:**
IF vital pulp, no periapical pathosis, routine canal anatomy
THEN complete chemo-mechanical preparation with standard needle irrigation; LAI adjunct is optional
BECAUSE Meire et al. 2023 found insufficient evidence (GRADE: very low to low certainty) that any laser adjunct improves 12-month healing over standard care [[PMID:36156804]]

**Branch 2 — Necrotic pulp with periapical lesion, single-rooted tooth:**
IF necrotic pulp with periapical index ≥3, single straight canal
THEN add Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 pps, 3 × 20 s per canal) and consider aPDT adjunct (methylene blue 1.56 μM/mL, 180 s at 100 mW)
BECAUSE LAI-SWEEPS produced 86.9% lesion volume reduction vs. 74.5% in controls at 12 months (not statistically significant); aPDT reduces postoperative pain at 24–72 h (SMD −1.21) [[PMID:38878107]] [[PMID:41388259]]

**Branch 3 — Retreatment with complex anatomy (isthmuses, lateral canals confirmed on CBCT):**
IF retreatment case with multi-rooted tooth and complex anatomy
THEN Er:YAG LAI is the preferred activation modality — superior to needle, sonic, and UAI in isthmus and overall system cleaning
BECAUSE Donnermeyer et al. found LAI achieved greatest hydrogel removal from entire root canal system including apical isthmuses; GentleWave and LAI both outperform passive delivery [[PMID:38382735]] [[PMID:41514415]]

**Branch 4 — Only diode laser available, no Er:YAG:**
IF only diode laser (810–980 nm) is available
THEN diode LAI may reduce postoperative pain versus needle irrigation at 6–48 h but should not be assumed to provide Er:YAG-equivalent photoacoustic streaming or antimicrobial activation
BECAUSE Sabeti et al. meta-analysis: diode LAI SMD vs. UAI = 0.03 (non-significant); McGillivray & Dutta found diode superior to needle for 6–48 h pain reduction [[PMID:40818665]] [[PMID:38484867]]

**Branch 5 — Immature apex / regenerative endodontic procedure:**
IF immature permanent tooth with necrotic pulp requiring REP
THEN use reduced-concentration NaOCl (1.5%); aPDT is an acceptable adjunct; avoid high-energy Er:YAG LAI near open apex
BECAUSE Er:YAG laser-assisted irrigation in REP context carries a reported risk of irrigant extrusion through the open apex [[PMID:40287048]]

## Module 3 — Clinical Laser Techniques and Protocol Application

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Two parallel questions govern laser use in root canal disinfection: (1) does laser adjunct therapy improve periapical healing, and (2) does it reduce postoperative pain? The highest-tier evidence answers these questions differently.

**Healing Outcomes.** The PROSPERO-registered ESE systematic review by Meire et al. (2023) [[PMID:36156804]] screened five databases and included 14 clinical studies evaluating adjunct therapies in apical periodontitis. GRADE assessment yielded *very low* certainty of evidence for diode laser canal irradiation and *low* certainty for aPDT. The authors concluded there is insufficient evidence to recommend any adjunctive laser therapy for the treatment of apical periodontitis [[PMID:36156804]]. A pre-registered RCT by Doğan et al. (2024) [[PMID:38878107]] corroborated this: in 56 single-rooted teeth with large periapical lesions, CBCT-measured lesion volume reduction at 12 months was 86.9% for SWEEPS-LAI versus 74.5% for controls — numerically greater but not statistically significant (p > 0.05) [[PMID:38878107]].

**Pain Outcomes.** Here, higher-tier evidence favours laser. A 2026 meta-analysis by Sabeti et al. [[PMID:40818665]] pooling 7 RCTs (n = 490 teeth) found laser-activated irrigation (LAI) reduced postoperative pain versus ultrasonically activated irrigation (SMD = −0.58; 95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; p = 0.0016), with peak effect at 24–48 hours (SMD = −1.00) [[PMID:40818665]]. Pulsed Er:YAG modalities showed the strongest benefit: PIPS SMD = −1.10; SWEEPS SMD = −1.57; diode LAI showed no significant effect (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. A separate 2025 PROSPERO-registered systematic review by Das et al. [[PMID:41388259]] confirmed aPDT reduces postoperative pain at 24 h (SMD = −1.21) and 72 h (SMD = −0.63) [[PMID:41388259]]. An RCT by Jian et al. (2025) [[PMID:41063319]] (n = 202) demonstrated that the combination of 810 nm LAI plus transcutaneous LLLT produced the lowest VAS scores and fewest analgesic tablets consumed (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets over 14 days), outperforming either modality alone [[PMID:41063319]].

**Smear Layer and Irrigant Activation.** An in vitro study by Abaza et al. (2025) [[PMID:41389357]] (n = 160 single-rooted premolars) compared Er,Cr:YSGG laser (25 mJ, 50 Hz, radial-firing tip, four 15-second activation cycles) against diode laser (pulsed 50%, 1.5 W), PUI, and conventional syringe-needle irrigation. The combination of Er,Cr:YSGG activation with NaOCl + EDTA achieved the lowest smear layer scores across all canal thirds; Er,Cr:YSGG with saline alone outperformed NaOCl alone, highlighting the dominant mechanical effect [[PMID:41389357]]. A model study by Donnermeyer et al. (2024) [[PMID:38382735]] using a 3D-printed canal system with 60° curvature, radius 5 mm, and isthmus structures (2.5 × 0.4 × 0.2 mm) found Er:YAG LAI (P400FL tip at canal entrance; 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 µs; 3 × 20 s per canal; 3 ml/20 s) achieved significantly greater hydrogel removal from the entire root canal system than all other tested techniques (p < 0.05) [[PMID:38382735]].

**Photobiomodulation for Vital Pulp Therapy.** A prospective cohort by Olszewska et al. (2024) [[PMID:37849444]] (n = 86 immature permanent teeth, ages 8–13) applied 635 nm diode laser PBM (100 mW, 200 mW/cm², 4 J total energy) immediately and 24 h after VPT with Biodentine. PBM significantly reduced cold sensitivity at 6 h, 24 h, 7 days, and 30 days compared to controls (p < 0.001), with no difference at 90 days [[PMID:37849444]].

**PDT in Retreatment.** A single-site RCT by Pažin et al. (2024) [[PMID:38013016]] (n = 36, periapical lesions > 5 mm) found that adding PDT after UAI final irrigation (3% NaOCl sequence) did not improve periapical lesion volume reduction at 1 year compared to UAI alone (86.98% vs. 79.72% reduction, p = 0.970) [[PMID:38013016]]. However, a retrospective analysis by Conejero et al. (2021) [[PMID:33731503]] (n = 214 teeth) showed RCT + PDT cases required calcium hydroxide intracanal dressing significantly less often than RCT alone in both primary (16.4% vs. 72.4%, p < 0.01) and retreatment (17% vs. 82.7%, p < 0.01) scenarios [[PMID:33731503]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

**1. Pre-operative CBCT assessment**
Obtain CBCT with small FOV (≤ 8 × 8 cm) to quantify periapical lesion volume and assess canal curvature. Document baseline volume in mm³ using ITK-SNAP or equivalent segmentation software for 12-month comparison [[PMID:38878107]].

**2. Chemo-mechanical preparation to patency**
Shape to a minimum apical preparation that accommodates the P400FL radial-firing tip (400 µm diameter) — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. Do not commence laser activation before shaping is complete.

**3. Initial irrigant delivery**
Abaza et al. (2025) [[PMID:41389357]] tested NaOCl as the primary irrigant throughout canal preparation; the retrieved evidence base does not specify a delivery needle depth for the pre-laser irrigation phase — standard practice (side-vented needle, short of working length) applies here.

**4. Er:YAG LAI activation — full-sequence protocol (default)**
Insert P400FL radial-firing tip (400 µm diameter) at the canal entrance (SWEEPS mode) or 1 mm short of working length (PIPS mode). Activate Er:YAG at 50 mJ, 25 Hz (1 W), 300 µs pulse duration; run 3 cycles × 20 seconds per canal with continuous 3 ml/20 s irrigant replenishment [[PMID:38382735]]. SWEEPS and PIPS yield the strongest pain reduction (SMD −1.57 and −1.10 respectively vs. UAI) [[PMID:40818665]].

**Alternative — Diode LAI (when Er:YAG unavailable):**
Use 810 nm or 980 nm diode at 1.5 W pulsed (50% duty cycle); activate for four 15-second cycles per canal [[PMID:41389357]]. Diode LAI showed no significant postoperative pain reduction versus UAI in meta-analysis (SMD = 0.03); choose diode only when Er:YAG equipment is unavailable [[PMID:40818665]].

**5. EDTA chelation step**
Deliver 17% EDTA [[PMID:41389357]] and activate with the same Er:YAG parameters for one 20-second cycle [[PMID:38382735]]. NaOCl + EDTA combination yielded the lowest smear layer scores across all canal thirds in comparative in vitro testing [[PMID:41389357]].

**6. Final NaOCl flush**
Deliver a final volume of NaOCl and activate for one additional 20-second LAI cycle to clear EDTA residue — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base.

**7. Dry the canal**
Use paper points to working length to confirm no residual moisture before obturation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base.

**8. Obturation**
Obturate with a calcium silicate-based bioceramic sealer — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. The retrieved evidence base does not report obturation material outcomes as a function of LAI use.

**9. Post-operative LLLT (pain management adjunct)**
Apply 810 nm diode transcutaneously to buccal and lingual/palatal mucosa at the apex level: 400 mW, 5 seconds per point, 2 J per point [[PMID:41063319]]. Repeat at 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h post-treatment. The combined LAI + LLLT protocol produced 0.53 ± 0.74 analgesic tablets used over 14 days versus significantly more in controls [[PMID:41063319]].

**10. 12-month CBCT review**
Re-scan with identical small FOV; quantify lesion volume change volumetrically. In single-rooted teeth, expect lesion reduction regardless of LAI use, as no statistically significant difference between SWEEPS-LAI (86.9%) and control (74.5%) was detected at 12 months [[PMID:38878107]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**Branch 1 — Standard LAI Application**
IF tooth has apical periodontitis with PAI ≥ 3, intact single or multi-canal root, and Er:YAG laser is available
THEN apply SWEEPS or PIPS protocol (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 300 µs, 3 × 20 s) as primary activation
BECAUSE SWEEPS and PIPS yield the greatest postoperative pain reduction versus UAI (SMD −1.57 and −1.10 respectively) [[PMID:40818665]]

**Branch 2 — Adjunct Withheld**
IF the sole clinical goal is periapical healing improvement in a single-rooted tooth and laser equipment is unavailable
THEN proceed with conventional chemo-mechanical preparation plus UAI without laser adjunct
BECAUSE no statistically significant difference in 12-month CBCT lesion healing was detected between SWEEPS-LAI, PUI, and controls in single-rooted teeth [[PMID:38878107]], and GRADE certainty for healing benefit remains very low for diode laser and low for aPDT [[PMID:36156804]]

**Branch 3 — Retreatment with Complex Anatomy**
IF endodontic retreatment is required with multi-rooted tooth and isthmus structures confirmed on CBCT
THEN add Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 25 pps, 300 µs, 3 × 20 s) to the retreatment protocol
BECAUSE LAI outperformed all other activation techniques in isthmus hydrogel removal in a complex curved canal model, and the primary study authors note outcomes may differ
