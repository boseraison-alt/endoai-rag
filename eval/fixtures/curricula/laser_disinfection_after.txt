# Use of Lasers in Root Canal Disinfection
### A 20-Minute Endodontic Teaching Curriculum

---

## OVERVIEW

Laser-assisted disinfection has moved from experimental curiosity to a clinically debated adjunct in contemporary endodontics, yet the gap between laboratory promise and clinical proof remains wide. This curriculum equips the clinician to select appropriate laser systems and wavelengths for specific clinical scenarios, apply evidence-based protocols with correct parameters, critically appraise claims of superiority over conventional chemomechanical preparation, and counsel patients realistically about what laser adjuncts can — and cannot — deliver. Four modules progress from biophysical principles through indication selection, step-by-step technique, and outcomes evidence, culminating in an operational decision rule for chairside use. The highest-tier evidence currently positions laser therapy as an adjunct for postoperative pain reduction [[PMID:40818665]] and, selectively, for biofilm disruption in complex anatomy [[PMID:38662505]] — not as a replacement for NaOCl-based irrigation [[PMID:41833582]].

---

## Module 1 — Laser Physics and Bactericidal Mechanisms in Endodontics

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** The 2026 PROSPERO-registered umbrella review by Duarte et al. synthesising 9 systematic reviews concluded that laser-assisted irrigation "should currently be regarded as an adjunct with unproven clinical benefit," citing heterogeneity in wavelengths, designs, and outcome measures [[PMID:41833582]]. Conversely, the systematic review and meta-analysis by Ghahari et al. (n = 565 samples) demonstrated statistically significant biofilm reduction by erbium lasers in root canals (SMD −0.551; 95% CI −0.656 to −0.445; I² = 23.89%; p = 0.031) [[PMID:38662505]], and the Er:YAG-focused systematic review by Fiegler-Rudol et al. found that all 15 included studies reported improved disinfection over conventional or ultrasonic methods [[PMID:40136729]]. The higher-quality, AMSTAR 2-assessed 2026 umbrella review favours the cautious interpretation: potential laboratory benefit exists, but clinical translation remains unconfirmed [[PMID:41833582]].

**Laser Systems Used in Endodontics**

Four principal laser types appear in the evidence base, each defined by its wavelength and tissue-interaction mechanism:

- **Er:YAG (2940 nm):** Peak water absorption; generates photoacoustic streaming and cavitation; highest efficiency for biofilm disruption at 75–100 mJ energy, 100–150 μs pulse duration, and <50 Hz pulse rate [[PMID:38662505]]
- **Er,Cr:YSGG (2780 nm):** Absorbed by both water and hydroxyapatite; produces photoacoustic streaming, cavitation-like fluid movement, and localised photothermal effects within the canal [[PMID:42306383]]
- **Nd:YAG (1064 nm):** Near-infrared; acts by direct photothermal effect without photosensitiser; at 200 mJ/pulse, 20 pulses per second for 20 s, reduces *E. coli* from 8.67 × 10⁶ to 4.39 × 10⁴ CFU/mL and *S. aureus* from 1.44 × 10⁶ to 3.8 × 10⁴ CFU/mL, but causes a temperature rise of 61.8 ± 4.2 °C — a clinically significant thermal risk [[PMID:11806644]]
- **Diode lasers (810–980 nm):** Absorbed by pigmented/vascular tissue; at 1.2 W (980 nm), comparable bactericidal effect on *E. faecalis* to 2 W and 3 W settings, with the lowest external root surface temperature rise (6.25 °C vs. 10.20 °C at 2 W and 16.79 °C at 3 W) [[PMID:29378981]]

**Bactericidal Mechanisms**

Two distinct mechanisms operate depending on laser class:

*Photothermal effect* — Direct delivery of infrared energy denatures bacterial proteins and disrupts cell membranes. This is the primary mechanism for Nd:YAG and diode systems; temperature rises sufficient for sterilisation risk thermal damage to periodontium and root dentin [[PMID:11806644]].

*Photoacoustic streaming and cavitation* — Erbium-wavelength lasers vaporise irrigant water, generating expanding and imploding vapour bubbles. Meire & De Moor describe cavitation as "the driving force behind LAI," with growing and imploding vapour bubbles causing intense liquid dynamics throughout the root canal [[PMID:38340037]]. This physically disrupts biofilm and propels irrigant into dentinal tubules and lateral canals [[PMID:40136729]]. Protocols include photon-induced photoacoustic streaming (PIPS) and shock wave-enhanced emission photoacoustic streaming (SWEEPS) [[PMID:41505924]].

*Antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT)* — Low-power laser light activates a photosensitiser (typically methylene blue 0.005% or toluidine blue 0.1 mg/mL), generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) that oxidise bacterial cell walls without thermal tissue damage [[PMID:38618073]]. Siddiqui et al., reviewing 17 studies using diode wavelengths 625–805 nm, fiber diameter 200 μm, and power 40 mW–5 W, found that 12 of 17 studies reported effective *E. faecalis* elimination, but 4 found conventional irrigation superior — efficacy "remains questionable" [[PMID:24192536]].

**Dentin Safety Considerations**

Laser-activated irrigation (LAI) demonstrably alters dentin microstructure. Rathod et al. found that 980 nm diode LAI caused FTIR-confirmed structural degradation of collagen (amide bands), phosphate, and carbonate, along with thermal-stress-induced cracking; Er,Cr:YSGG (2940 nm, 25 mJ, 10 Hz, 60 μs, 200 μm tip, 21 mm insertion length) produced comparatively lesser structural damage than diode LAI [[PMID:41519256]]. This finding rests on an ex vivo study of 42 premolars (n = 14/group) — no clinical RCT has evaluated long-term structural consequences of LAI on root dentin in vivo; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**Conventional Irrigation Remains Standard of Care**

Across this curriculum, NaOCl is cited at four concentrations (2%, 2.5%, 3%, and 5.25%), each tied to a distinct study context: 5.25% NaOCl is the primary irrigant concentration in the Module 1 foundational protocol (Mashalkar et al. [[PMID:24326811]]; Kottur et al. [[PMID:39955574]]), 3% NaOCl is the concentration used by Pažin et al. (2024) in their retreatment UAI/conventional arm [[PMID:38013016]], 2% NaOCl is the concentration used by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. (2025) during Er:YAG LAI activation [[PMID:40135760]], and 2.5% NaOCl is the concentration used by Swetha et al. (2024) for pulpotomy haemostasis [[PMID:38157279]]; the evidence base does not establish which concentration is preferred for laser-adjunct protocols generally, and clinicians should match the concentration to the specific study protocol being replicated.

Mashalkar et al. (n = 60 teeth) found that only 3 of 30 samples showed bacterial growth after 5.25% NaOCl + hydrogen peroxide irrigation, versus 8 of 30 after 980 nm diode laser alone — conventional chemomechanical preparation outperformed laser monotherapy [[PMID:24326811]]. The 2026 umbrella review positions lasers as adjuncts to, not replacements for, NaOCl-based irrigation [[PMID:41833582]].

The 5.25% NaOCl concentration cited here (Mashalkar et al. [[PMID:24326811]]) and in the Module 1 Clinical Application protocol [[PMID:39955574]] reflects the primary disinfection standard used in those studies, which is distinct from the 2% NaOCl used by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. (2025) specifically during Er:YAG LAI activation [[PMID:40135760]], the 3% NaOCl used by Pažin et al. (2024) in their retreatment RCT [[PMID:38013016]], and the 2.5% NaOCl used by Swetha et al. (2024) for pulpotomy haemostasis [[PMID:38157279]]; each concentration should be understood as study-specific rather than universally interchangeable.

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Establish access and working length**
   Determine working length radiographically and electronically — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. Prepare canal to a size that allows laser tip insertion; the retrieved evidence base does not specify a minimum apical preparation size for laser tip access as a generalised rule.

2. **Primary chemomechanical irrigation with 5.25% NaOCl**
   Irrigate with 5.25% NaOCl after each consecutive instrument throughout the preparation sequence [[PMID:39955574]]. This step is the disinfection foundation and must not be replaced by laser alone [[PMID:24326811]] [[PMID:41833582]].

3. **Smear layer removal**
   Irrigate with 17% EDTA as a final flush before laser activation [[PMID:29378981]].

4. **Select laser system per indication**
   - *Cavitation/LAI goal:* Er:YAG 2940 nm at 75–100 mJ, <50 Hz, 100–150 μs pulse duration [[PMID:38662505]]
   - *aPDT goal:* Diode 660 nm, 100 mW, fiber tip 300 μm [[PMID:30797116]]; or 630 nm LED at 3 W/cm² for two 30-second periods [[PMID:35996480]]
   - *Diode LAI:* 980 nm at 1.2 W (lowest effective power with least external temperature rise: 6.25 °C) [[PMID:29378981]]

5. **For aPDT: apply photosensitiser and observe contact time**
   Introduce methylene blue 0.005% via sterile paper cone for 3 min pre-irradiation [[PMID:30797116]]; alternatively, toluidine blue 0.1 mg/mL for two 30-second irradiation periods [[PMID:35996480]].

6. **Laser irradiation**
   - *aPDT (diode 660 nm):* Deliver 100 mW for 40 s (energy density 4 J/cm²) via 300 μm fiber inserted 2 mm short of working length [[PMID:30797116]]
   - *Nd:YAG (if selected):* 100 mJ or 200 mJ at 20 pps for 20 s; note that 200 mJ produces a temperature rise of 61.8 ± 4.2 °C and 100 mJ produces 24.3 ± 3.9 °C — both represent significant thermal load [[PMID:11806644]]
   - *Er,Cr:YSGG LAI:* 2940 nm, 25 mJ, 10 Hz, 60 μs, 200 μm tip, 21 mm insertion [[PMID:41519256]]

7. **Post-laser irrigant flush**
   Flush canal with 5.25% NaOCl after laser activation to remove debris mobilised by cavitation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base.

8. **Dry and obturate**
   Dry canals with paper points and obturate using the clinician's chosen technique — the retrieved evidence base does not report a laser-specific obturation protocol.

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the operator's primary goal is biofilm disruption and improved irrigant penetration into the apical third and lateral canals
**THEN** select Er:YAG (2940 nm) at 75–100 mJ, <50 Hz, 100–150 μs (PIPS or SWEEPS protocol)
**BECAUSE** erbium wavelengths produce cavitation-driven photoacoustic streaming with the strongest evidence for biofilm reduction in the root canal (SMD −0.551; 95% CI −0.656 to −0.445) [[PMID:38662505]] [[PMID:40136729]]

**IF** conventional chemomechanical preparation and NaOCl irrigation have been completed but residual infection is suspected (e.g., persistent sinus tract, necrotic tooth with periapical lesion)
**THEN** add aPDT as adjunct: methylene blue 0.005% for 3 min contact + 660 nm diode at 100 mW for 40 s
**BECAUSE** aPDT generates ROS-mediated killing without thermal risk, and combining aPDT with antimicrobial irrigants may provide a synergistic effect [[PMID:31809911]]

**IF** diode laser (980 nm) is the only system available
**THEN** set power to 1.2 W rather than 2 W or 3 W
**BECAUSE** 1.2 W produces comparable *E. faecalis* reduction with a significantly lower external root surface temperature rise (6.25 °C vs. 16.79 °C at 3 W) [[PMID:29378981]]

**IF** the case involves a separated instrument blocking apical access or anatomical complexity preventing full chemo-mechanical debridement
**THEN** aPDT adjunct (660 nm, 100 mW, 90 s total irradiation time delivering 9.0 J, fiber 300 μm inserted 2 mm short of working length) may be added to canals where instrumentation is limited
**BECAUSE** this recommendation rests on a single case report with 60-month follow-up (Amaral et al. [[PMID:30797116]]) — no RCT or prospective cohort has evaluated aPDT specifically for separated instrument cases; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**IF** high-powered laser is proposed as the sole disinfection method, replacing NaOCl-based irrigation
**THEN** revert to NaOCl-based chemomechanical preparation as the primary protocol; laser serves as adjunct only
**BECAUSE** conventional 5.25% NaOCl produced bacterial growth in only 3/30 samples versus 8/30 for 980 nm diode laser alone [[PMID:24326811]], and the 2026 umbrella review classifies laser-activated irrigation as having "unproven clinical benefit" as a standalone treatment [[PMID:41833582]]

## Module 2 — Clinical Assessment and Indication Selection for Laser Disinfection

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Whether laser-assisted disinfection produces meaningfully superior periapical healing compared to conventional irrigation is unresolved at the highest evidence tiers. Doğan et al., 2024 (n=56, 12-month CBCT follow-up) found that LAI-SWEEPS achieved 86.9% lesion volume reduction versus 74.5% in controls — yet this difference was not statistically significant (p>0.05) [[PMID:38878107]]. Conversely, Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 found a statistically significant reduction in periapical lesion size at 6 months when Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs, 300 μm tip) was combined with photobiomodulation at 635 nm compared to chemo-mechanical treatment alone [[PMID:40135760]]. Larger, adequately powered RCTs are required before laser disinfection can displace conventional irrigation as standard of care for periapical healing outcomes.

**Selecting the Right Patient: Inclusion Criteria**

Laser adjuncts are best indicated when chemo-mechanical preparation alone is likely to be insufficient. Prioritise laser disinfection in:

- Teeth with large periapical lesions (PAI score ≥3) confirmed on CBCT [[PMID:38878107]]
- Multi-rooted teeth with curved or complex canal anatomy, where Doğan et al., 2024 specifically acknowledge conventional irrigation is most limited [[PMID:38878107]]
- Endodontic retreatment cases with persistent infection — Conejero et al., 2021 (retrospective, n=214) showed adjunctive photodynamic therapy (PDT) reduced calcium hydroxide interappointment dressing need from 82.7% to 17% in retreatment (p<0.01); note this rests on a retrospective study of 214 teeth with no matched RCT confirmation — treat as provisional pending prospective validation [[PMID:33731503]]
- Combined endo-perio lesions, particularly with probing depth ≥6 mm, where Li et al., 2026 (n=634, retrospective) demonstrated composite success risk ratio of 1.22 (95% CI: 1.10–1.36) favouring adjunctive water laser [[PMID:42482547]]
- Patients with high preoperative pain scores, where semiconductor laser significantly reduces postoperative VAS at day 1 (p<0.001) [[PMID:41063319]]
- Anatomically complex cases (dens invaginatus, open apex) where supplementary disinfection is biologically justified [[PMID:40206775]]

**Contraindications and Exclusion Criteria**

Do not select laser disinfection as the primary intervention when:

- The tooth is non-restorable or has vertical root fracture (systemic treatment planning supersedes adjunct selection)
- Single-rooted teeth with small periapical lesions: Doğan et al., 2024 explicitly found no statistically significant healing benefit of LAI-SWEEPS over manual agitation in single-canal teeth at 12 months [[PMID:38878107]]
- Laser equipment or trained operator is unavailable — chemo-mechanical irrigation remains standard of care per AAE guidelines
- Patient has a documented photosensitiser allergy (relevant only when antimicrobial PDT is planned)
- Er:YAG laser retreatment for filling material removal: Fischer et al., 2024 (systematic review) found Er:YAG SWEEPS did not significantly reduce residual filling material volume (p>0.05) — PUI or XP-Endo Finisher should be preferred in retreatment [[PMID:39489325]]

**Diagnostic Workup Before Laser Selection**

Accurate pre-treatment diagnosis determines both whether laser is indicated and which modality to select. Pulpal and periapical diagnosis must follow established AAE classification. CBCT is the imaging standard for volumetric lesion measurement when laser-activated irrigation is planned for large periapical lesions [[PMID:38878107]]. Microbial profiling using MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry can identify dominant species (E. faecalis predominated in retreatment cases; Lactobacillus spp. in abscess cases in Ahlat et al., 2023, n=150) [[PMID:37925063]], though this remains a research tool rather than a clinical prerequisite.

Preoperative pain level is an independent predictor of postoperative pain: Jian et al., 2025 identified preoperative pain score, periapical lesion size, and laser protocol as the three main factors affecting postoperative VAS across 197 patients [[PMID:41063319]]. Patients with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis and large periapical lesions therefore represent the highest-benefit subgroup for laser adjunction. Antimicrobial PDT (aPDT) as adjunct to chemo-mechanical irrigation reduces postoperative pain (SMD −1.21 at 24 h; SMD −0.63 at 72 h) and microbial load, though only 3 of 11 included studies had low risk of bias [[PMID:41388259]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Establish diagnosis and indication** — Confirm PAI score ≥3 on CBCT or periapical radiograph; classify pulpal status per AAE criteria. Record preoperative VAS pain score. [[PMID:38878107]]

2. **Obtain working length** — Electronic apex locator confirmed by periapical radiograph. Doğan et al., 2024 did not report a specific apex locator; use institutional standard. [[PMID:38878107]]

3. **Canal shaping** — Shape to a minimum apical size adequate for laser tip insertion (manufacturer guidance for 300 μm tip requires patent canal to working length). Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 used standard chemo-mechanical preparation prior to Er:YAG LAI. [[PMID:40135760]]

4. **Primary chemo-mechanical irrigation** — Irrigate with 2% NaOCl (Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 protocol) throughout instrumentation; final flush volume not specified in that study. For pulpotomy cases, Swetha et al., 2024 used 2.5% NaOCl for haemostasis in the conventional arm. [[PMID:40135760]] [[PMID:38157279]]

These concentrations reflect different study contexts: 2% NaOCl is the irrigation concentration used by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. (2025) specifically during Er:YAG LAI activation in retreatment cases [[PMID:40135760]], and 2.5% NaOCl is the concentration used by Swetha et al. (2024) for pulp haemostasis in the conventional pulpotomy arm [[PMID:38157279]]; neither concentration is interchangeable with the 5.25% NaOCl used as the primary irrigant in the Module 1 and Module 1 Clinical Application protocols [[PMID:39955574]] [[PMID:24326811]].

5. **Er:YAG LAI activation** — Insert 300 μm tip to working length; parameters: 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs pulse; fluence 71.4 J/cm²; power density 1,428.6 W/cm². Activate 2% NaOCl, then repeat with 17% EDTA. [[PMID:40135760]]

6. **Final rinse and dry** — Paper points to working length; canal must be patent and dry before obturation or medicament placement.

7. **Diode LAI alternative (810 nm)** — If Er:YAG unavailable: 810 nm semiconductor diode laser for LAI reduces postoperative VAS versus controls (p<0.001 at day 1); Jian et al., 2025 did not report energy or tip parameters beyond wavelength. [[PMID:41063319]]

8. **Photobiomodulation (PBM) adjunct** — Apply 635 nm diode, 400 mW, 2 J per point, 4 J/cm², 8 mm applicator, 5 s per point, 2 apical points; repeat at 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h post-treatment. [[PMID:40135760]]

9. **Obturation** — Thermo-condensed gutta-percha with AH Plus sealer (epoxy resin-based), as used by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025. [[PMID:40135760]]

10. **Postoperative follow-up** — Clinical and radiographic review at 3, 6, 9 months; CBCT volumetric reassessment at 12 months for large lesions. [[PMID:38878107]]

### 4b. Decision Tree

**Branch 1 — Large periapical lesion, single-rooted tooth**
IF PAI ≥3 on a single-rooted tooth with straight canal anatomy
THEN use standard chemo-mechanical irrigation; laser adjunct is optional
BECAUSE Doğan et al., 2024 found no statistically significant healing benefit of LAI-SWEEPS vs. manual agitation in single-canal teeth at 12 months [[PMID:38878107]]

**Branch 2 — Large periapical lesion, multi-rooted or complex anatomy**
IF PAI ≥3 in a multi-rooted tooth or complex anatomy (curved canals, dens invaginatus)
THEN add Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 300 μm tip) with 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA plus postoperative PBM at 635 nm
BECAUSE Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 demonstrated statistically significant periapical lesion reduction at 6 months with this combined protocol [[PMID:40135760]]

**Branch 3 — Retreatment case with persistent infection**
IF endodontic retreatment with confirmed periapical pathosis and previous calcium hydroxide failure
THEN add aPDT (phenothiazinium photosensitiser, diode laser activation) to chemo-mechanical retreatment; avoid Er:YAG SWEEPS for filling removal
BECAUSE Conejero et al., 2021 showed 17% vs. 82.7% calcium hydroxide need with vs. without PDT (p<0.01) [[PMID:33731503]]; Fischer et al., 2024 showed Er:YAG SWEEPS did not significantly reduce residual filling material (p>0.05) [[PMID:39489325]]

**Branch 4 — High preoperative pain, symptomatic irreversible pulpitis**
IF preoperative VAS >5 and diagnosis of symptomatic irreversible pulpitis
THEN combine LAI and LLLT (combined protocol, 810 nm semiconductor laser) to minimise postoperative pain
BECAUSE Jian et al., 2025 (n=197) showed combined LAI+LLLT produced the lowest VAS scores and fewest analgesic tablets (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets) across all laser subgroups [[PMID:41063319]]

**Branch 5 — Endo-perio lesion with probing depth ≥6 mm**
IF combined endo-perio lesion confirmed, probing depth ≥6 mm
THEN add adjunctive water laser to conventional RCT and scaling/root planing
BECAUSE Li et al., 2026 (n=634) showed composite success risk ratio 1.22 (95% CI 1.10–1.36, p=0.003), with greatest benefit at baseline PD ≥6 mm [[PMID:42482547]]

**Branch 6 — Pulpotomy with haemostasis difficulty**
IF symptomatic irreversible pulpitis requiring pulpotomy and haemostasis unachieved with 2.5% NaOCl within 5 min
THEN switch to diode laser (GaAlAs 980 nm or KTP laser) for haemostasis and cavity disinfection
BECAUSE Swetha et al., 2024 (n=86) found LCP (980 nm) achieved 93% success at 18 months vs. 88.4% for conventional; Ayhan et al., 2025 (n=65) found KTP laser gave 90.5% vs. 86.4% success — neither difference was statistically significant [[PMID:38157279]] [[PMID:41121058]]

## Module 3 — Laser Disinfection Techniques and Treatment Protocols

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** The central clinical question — does adjunctive laser therapy improve outcomes in root canal treatment beyond conventional chemomechanical preparation — is answered differently depending on the outcome measured.

For **periapical healing**, the highest-tier available evidence is the ESE-commissioned systematic review by Meire et al., 2023, which found insufficient evidence to recommend any adjunctive therapy, including diode laser canal irradiation, aPDT, and ultrasonically activated irrigation, for the treatment of apical periodontitis (GRADE: very low to low certainty) [[PMID:36156804]]. This conclusion is reinforced by the RCT by Doğan et al., 2024 (n=56, 12-month CBCT follow-up), which showed LAI-SWEEPS produced 86.9% lesion volume reduction versus 74.5% for conventional irrigation — a numerically meaningful difference that nonetheless failed to reach statistical significance (p>0.05) [[PMID:38878107]]. Similarly, Pažin et al., 2024 found no significant difference in periapical lesion healing between UAI alone and UAI+PDT at 12 months (median reduction 86.98% vs. 79.72%; p=0.970) in a 36-patient single-visit retreatment RCT [[PMID:38013016]].

For **postoperative pain**, higher-tier evidence shifts in favor of laser adjuncts. The 2026 meta-analysis by Sabeti et al. (7 RCTs, n=490 teeth) demonstrated LAI significantly reduced postoperative pain compared to UAI overall (SMD = −0.58; 95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; p=0.0016), with the greatest effect at 24–48 hours (SMD = −1.00) [[PMID:40818665]]. Among laser types, pulsed Er:YAG modalities were clearly superior: SWEEPS produced SMD = −1.57 and PIPS produced SMD = −1.10, while diode LAI showed no significant pain benefit (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. These findings are corroborated by the meta-analysis by Luo et al., 2024 (22 studies, n=892), which reported significant pain reductions for LLLT (SMD = −0.86 at 24 h), diode laser (SMD = −0.27 at 48 h), and PDT (SMD = −1.12 at 24 h; pain incidence RR = 0.47) [[PMID:39287434]]. Das et al., 2025 (PROSPERO-registered SR, 11 RCTs) confirmed aPDT significantly reduced VAS pain at 24 h (SMD = −1.21) and 72 h (SMD = −0.63) [[PMID:41388259]].

**Current standard-of-care remains conventional chemomechanical preparation as the primary treatment.** Laser adjuncts targeting pain reduction — particularly pulsed Er:YAG via SWEEPS/PIPS — represent the best-evidenced supplementary role at present. Claims regarding improved healing should be treated as provisional pending adequately powered RCTs.

**Technique-Specific Considerations**

*Laser-activated irrigation (LAI):* Donnermeyer et al., 2024 (in vitro, n=20 model canals) demonstrated that Er:YAG LAI using a P400FL fiber tip at the canal entrance, set to 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 μs pulse duration, with 3×20 s activation per canal and 3 mL irrigant per 20-second cycle, produced significantly greater hydrogel removal from the entire root canal system including isthmus structures compared to ultrasonic, sonic, and manual agitation (p<0.05) [[PMID:38382735]]. Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 applied Er:YAG LAI with 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA (50 mJ; 25 Hz; 1 W; 300 μs; 300 μm tip; fluence 71.4 J/cm²) in retreatment and showed a statistically significant reduction in periapical lesion size at 6 months compared to controls (mean 1.55 mm vs. 1.95 mm; p<0.05), with this group also receiving adjunct 635-nm PBM [[PMID:40135760]].

*aPDT:* Trials included in the SR by Araújo et al., 2024 used phenothiazinium salts as photosensitizers; that review did not report specific intracanal exposure times [[PMID:37195228]]. PDT generates reactive oxygen species through light activation of a photosensitizer, causing microbial cell inactivation — standard practice description, not drawn from a single retrieved paper.

*PDT in retreatment:* Conejero et al., 2021 (retrospective, n=214) found RCT+PDT reduced calcium hydroxide requirement in retreatment from 82.7% to 17.0% of cases (p<0.01) and reduced the proportion requiring >2 visits from 64.9% to 49.1% (p=0.05) [[PMID:33731503]]. This recommendation rests on a retrospective cohort of 214 teeth (Conejero et al. 2021 [[PMID:33731503]]) — no prospective RCT has evaluated PDT's effect on visit number or calcium hydroxide need in retreatment specifically; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

**1. Complete chemomechanical preparation before any laser adjunct**
Laser adjuncts are supplements to, not replacements for, conventional instrumentation and irrigation — standard practice, not from the retrieved evidence base. Meire et al., 2023 defines adjunct therapy explicitly as procedures beyond conventional chemomechanical preparation [[PMID:36156804]].

**2. Primary irrigation with NaOCl throughout instrumentation**
Irrigate canals with 3% NaOCl throughout shaping (concentration used by Pažin et al., 2024) [[PMID:38013016]]. Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 used 2% NaOCl specifically for the LAI activation step [[PMID:40135760]]; use 2% when following that study's Er:YAG protocol.

The 3% NaOCl concentration is specific to the UAI/conventional irrigation arm of the Pažin et al. (2024) single-visit retreatment RCT [[PMID:38013016]] and is not equivalent to the 2% NaOCl used by Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. (2025) during Er:YAG LAI activation [[PMID:40135760]] or the 5.25% NaOCl used as the foundational irrigant in Module 1 [[PMID:39955574]]; clinicians should apply the concentration that matches the specific study protocol being followed.

**3. Final chelation step with 17% EDTA**
Apply 17% EDTA prior to final NaOCl irrigation and LAI activation, as used in Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025; that study did not report a specific EDTA contact time [[PMID:40135760]].

**4. Er:YAG LAI activation (preferred modality for pain and debridement)**
Position P400FL fiber tip at the canal entrance — not apically. Activate at 50 mJ energy, 25 Hz frequency, 1 W power, 300 μs pulse duration, 300 μm tip diameter [[PMID:38382735]] [[PMID:40135760]]. Run 3 cycles of 20 seconds per canal with continuous irrigant flow of 3 mL per 20-second cycle [[PMID:38382735]]. SWEEPS (SMD −1.57) and PIPS (SMD −1.10) modalities show the strongest postoperative pain reduction; diode LAI shows no significant benefit (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]].

**5. aPDT application (particularly indicated in retreatment)**
Apply phenothiazinium salt photosensitizer intracanally and activate with the appropriate light source [[PMID:37195228]]. Araújo et al., 2024 did not report specific intracanal exposure times for the included trials [[PMID:37195228]]. aPDT reduced postoperative VAS at 24 h (SMD −1.21) and 72 h (SMD −0.63) [[PMID:41388259]].

**6. LLLT — extraoral application for postoperative pain management**
Apply a 635-nm diode laser at 400 mW, 5 seconds per point, 2 J per point, 4 J/cm², at 2 application points at the apex level, over 4 sessions: on the treatment day and at 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h post-treatment [[PMID:40135760]]. In the 810-nm semiconductor laser RCT by Jian et al., 2025, LLLT was applied to buccal and palatal/lingual mucosa; that study did not report W/cm² or J dosing parameters [[PMID:41063319]].

**7. Combined LAI + LLLT for maximum pain control**
Combining LAI with LLLT produced the lowest postoperative VAS scores across all time points and lowest analgesic consumption (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets over 14 days) compared to either modality alone or control [[PMID:41063319]].

**8. Obturation**
Obturate with thermoplasticized gutta-percha and AH Plus epoxy resin-based sealer, as used in Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025 [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the tooth has apical periodontitis and is undergoing primary root canal treatment in a single-rooted canal
**THEN** complete conventional chemomechanical preparation; add laser adjuncts for postoperative pain management, but do not expect statistically significant improvement in periapical healing
**BECAUSE** Doğan et al., 2024 (n=56, 12-month CBCT RCT) showed no statistically significant healing advantage for LAI-SWEEPS versus conventional irrigation in single-rooted teeth (p>0.05) [[PMID:38878107]], and Meire et al., 2023 found insufficient evidence for any adjunct to improve AP healing [[PMID:36156804]]

**IF** the patient has significant preoperative pain or a large periapical lesion (PAI ≥3)
**THEN** use the combined LAI (Er:YAG SWEEPS/PIPS) + LLLT protocol
**BECAUSE** Jian et al., 2025 identified periapical lesion size and preoperative pain as the main predictors of postoperative pain, and the combined group used the fewest analgesics (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets over 14 days) [[PMID:41063319]]

**IF** the tooth is undergoing nonsurgical retreatment with an established periapical lesion
**THEN** consider adjunctive aPDT to reduce calcium hydroxide dependence and number of visits
**BECAUSE** Conejero et al., 2021 showed PDT reduced calcium hydroxide requirement in retreatment from 82.7% to 17.0% (p<0.01) [[PMID:33731503]] — retrospective cohort evidence only; treat as provisional

**IF** the tooth is multirooted with complex anatomy (isthmuses, lateral canals)
**THEN** prioritize Er:YAG LAI over sonic or ultrasonic activation
**BECAUSE** Donnermeyer et al., 2024 showed LAI superior to all other techniques including UAI for removal from isthmus structures (p<0.05) [[PMID:38382735]], and Doğan et al., 2024 noted their null healing finding may not extend to complex multirooted systems [[PMID:38878107]]

**IF** only a diode laser (810–980 nm) is available
**THEN** use it for extraoral LLLT rather than intracanal LAI
**BECAUSE** Sabeti et al., 2026 confirmed diode LAI produced no significant pain reduction (SMD = 0.03), while LLLT with diode devices shows consistent pain reduction benefit (SMD = −0.86 at 24 h) [[PMID:40818665]] [[PMID:39287434]]

## Module 4 — Clinical Outcomes, Efficacy, and Adverse Events

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Two parallel questions dominate outcomes research on laser-assisted root canal disinfection: (1) does laser adjunct therapy improve *healing of apical periodontitis* compared with conventional chemo-mechanical preparation, and (2) does it reduce *postoperative pain*? Higher-tier evidence answers these questions differently.

**Healing of Apical Periodontitis**

The highest-tier evidence addressing laser adjuncts for AP healing is the pre-registered ESE systematic review by Meire et al. (2023), which identified 14 studies (13 RCTs, 1 retrospective cohort) meeting ≥1-year follow-up and ≥10 patients per arm [[PMID:36156804]]. GRADE assessment returned *very low* certainty for diode laser and *low* certainty for aPDT, ozone, and UAI; meta-analysis on UAI (2 studies) showed no statistically significant difference in radiographic healing at 12 months. The authors concluded there is *insufficient evidence to recommend any adjunctive therapy for the treatment of AP* [[PMID:36156804]]. This finding is directly corroborated by Doğan et al. (2024), an RCT in 56 single-rooted mandibular teeth with PAI ≥3, which measured volumetric CBCT healing at 12 months: LAI-SWEEPS achieved 86.9% lesion volume reduction versus 74.5% for manual agitation, but the difference was *not statistically significant* (p > 0.05) [[PMID:38878107]]. Both sources converge: laser-activated irrigation does not statistically outperform conventional methods for periapical healing in single-canal teeth at 12 months, though a numerically greater reduction is consistently observed.

**Postoperative Pain**

Here the evidence favors laser adjuncts more clearly. The 2026 meta-analysis by Sabeti et al. pooled 7 RCTs (n = 490 teeth) comparing LAI directly to UAI: LAI reduced postoperative pain (SMD = −0.58; 95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; p = .0016), with the greatest effect at 24–48 hours (SMD = −1.00) [[PMID:40818665]]. Crucially, pulsed Er:YAG modalities drove this effect (PIPS: SMD = −1.10; SWEEPS: SMD = −1.57), while diode LAI showed no significant benefit (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. The multimodal RCT by Jian et al. (2025, n = 202) confirmed that combining 810-nm semiconductor LAI with transcutaneous LLLT produced the lowest VAS scores across 14 days and the fewest analgesic tablets consumed (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets), compared with LLLT alone or LAI alone [[PMID:41063319]]. Similarly, Luo et al. (2024, n = 892) demonstrated significant pain-score reductions for LLLT (SMD = −0.86 at 24 h), diode laser (SMD = −0.27 at 48 h), and aPDT (SMD = −1.12 at 24 h), with aPDT also reducing pain *incidence* (RR = 0.47; 95% CI: 0.31–0.72) [[PMID:39287434]]. Das et al. (2025) corroborated this for aPDT specifically, reporting standardized mean differences of −1.21 at 24 h and −0.63 at 72 h for VAS pain scores in their meta-analysis of 5 RCTs [[PMID:41388259]].

**Emerging Debate — LLLT for Periapical Healing vs. Pain**

A tension exists between the Cochrane review by Del Fabbro et al. (2016), which found no evidence that LLLT prevented postoperative pain in periapical *surgical* cases [[PMID:27759881]], and the non-surgical RCT and meta-analysis evidence summarised in Modules 3 and 4 showing significant pain reduction with LLLT (SMD = −0.86 at 24 h) and combined LAI+LLLT (0.53 ± 0.74 analgesic tablets) [[PMID:39287434]] [[PMID:41063319]]; this tension is not resolved by the current evidence base because the Cochrane review addresses periapical surgery patients while the favourable studies address non-surgical root canal treatment, and a head-to-head RCT powered for both populations would be required to determine whether the discrepancy reflects a true procedural difference or differences in study design and follow-up.

The Cochrane review by Del Fabbro et al. (2016) found *no evidence* that LLLT prevented postoperative pain in periapical surgery (very low quality evidence) [[PMID:27759881]], a finding that conflicts with the RCT-level pain evidence summarized above for *non-surgical* treatment. Clinicians should note that the current standard of care does not endorse laser adjuncts as mandatory; the ESE position, as reflected by Meire et al. (2023), treats all adjunct therapies as investigational for AP healing [[PMID:36156804]].

**Pulpotomy Outcomes with Laser Hemostasis**

In vital pulp therapy, laser hemostasis produces comparable or marginally superior results to conventional methods. Swetha et al. (2024) found 18-month success rates of 93% (diode laser 980-nm, GaAlAs) versus 88.4% (2.5% NaOCl hemostasis), with no statistically significant difference; however, laser pulpotomy produced less postoperative pain at 48 hours (mean CCP pain 1.7 ± 1.4, p < 0.001) and thicker dentine bridges on CBCT (median IQR: 0.89, 1.06 vs. CCP, p = 0.0479) [[PMID:38157279]]. Ayhan et al. (2025) retrospectively confirmed overall MTA pulpotomy success of 87.69% at 12 months across saline, NaOCl, and KTP laser hemostasis groups, with no statistically significant difference between modalities [[PMID:41121058]].

**Adverse Events**

No serious laser-specific adverse events (perforation, irreversible thermal damage, extrusion injury) were reported in the RCTs and systematic reviews within this evidence base [[PMID:36156804]] [[PMID:40818665]]. Olszewska et al. (2024) reported no side effects attributable to 635-nm diode PBM (100 mW, 200 mW/cm², 4 J) in 86 immature teeth over 90 days [[PMID:37849444]]. The safety profile of LAI regarding irrigant extrusion requires monitoring, particularly in immature teeth — Ribeiro et al. (2025) flagged this as a risk with Er:YAG LAI in regenerative endodontic contexts, though no clinical adverse event data were reported [[PMID:40287048]]. This risk rests on expert review without prospective cohort quantification; treat as a provisional safety signal.

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

**1. Case selection and preoperative imaging**
Confirm AP diagnosis radiographically (PAI ≥ 2). Obtain CBCT for lesions > 5 mm or complex anatomy. Laser adjuncts are most evidence-supported for *pain management*; communicate realistic expectations for AP healing outcomes to the patient [[PMID:36156804]].

**2. Establish working length**
Determine electronically and confirm radiographically. Standard endodontic working length protocols apply; laser adjuncts do not alter this step. Meire et al. did not report specific WL parameters for adjunct-use studies [[PMID:36156804]].

**3. Chemo-mechanical preparation**
Complete rotary/reciprocating canal shaping to the planned apical size. Copious irrigation with sodium hypochlorite is the primary disinfectant; LAI is adjunctive, not a replacement. Doğan et al. did not specify shaping sequence or file system beyond describing "root canal treatment" and "irrigation procedures" [[PMID:38878107]].

**4. Conventional irrigant delivery**
Deliver sodium hypochlorite by side-vented needle to within 2–3 mm of working length before laser activation. Swetha et al. used 2.5% NaOCl in the control arm [[PMID:38157279]]; Doğan et al. did not report NaOCl concentration [[PMID:38878107]].

**5. Laser-activated irrigation — Er:YAG (PIPS or SWEEPS)**
Insert a radially-firing Er:YAG tip in the coronal third (non-touching technique). PIPS and SWEEPS protocols delivered the strongest pain-reduction effect at 24–48 hours (PIPS: SMD = −1.10; SWEEPS: SMD = −1.57) [[PMID:40818665]]. Erkan et al. (2022) used Er:YAG laser in a single-rooted premolar model; specific energy/pulse settings were not extractable from the available abstract [[PMID:35267110]].

**6. Laser-activated irrigation — 810-nm semiconductor diode (LAI mode)**
Insert fiber tip and activate 810-nm semiconductor laser in continuous-wave mode for irrigant activation. Jian et al. (2025) used 810-nm semiconductor laser for the LAI group (n = 48) within standard single-visit treatment; specific power and time parameters were not reported in the available abstract [[PMID:41063319]].

**7. Transcutaneous LLLT (combined protocol)**
Apply 810-nm semiconductor laser irradiation to buccal and lingual/palatal mucosa immediately post-obturation. The combined LAI + LLLT protocol produced the lowest postoperative VAS at days 1–7 and minimum analgesic use (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets) compared to either modality alone or control [[PMID:41063319]]. Specific power density and exposure time per site were not reported by Jian et al. [[PMID:41063319]].

**8. aPDT (antimicrobial photodynamic therapy, if selected)**
Apply phenothiazinium salt photosensitizer (e.g., toluidine blue 0.1 mg/mL or methylene blue) intracanally for a pre-irradiation contact time of 1–5 minutes, then activate with 630–660-nm LED or diode laser [[PMID:39287434]] [[PMID:41388259]]. Das et al. report aPDT reduces 24-h VAS by SMD −1.21 and 72-h VAS by SMD −0.63 [[PMID:41388259]]; pre-irradiation time range from Perin et al. (2024) [[PMID:39663253]].

**9. Final rinse and dry**
Rinse with sterile saline or distilled water after NaOCl/aPDT sequence. Dry with paper points to working length.

**10. Obturation**
Obturate using standard technique. Canal-filling material choice is governed by case factors; no laser adjunct modifies obturation material selection per this evidence base.

**11. Postoperative instructions**
Advise VAS self-monitoring at 24 h, 48 h, and day 7. Analgesic requirements are significantly lower in laser-assisted groups; reserve prescription analgesics for patients with large periapical lesions or high preoperative pain scores — both identified as significant predictors of postoperative pain by multivariate analysis [[PMID:41063319]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the patient has confirmed AP (PAI ≥ 2) in a permanent tooth and the primary goal is optimal *postoperative pain control*
**THEN** use pulsed Er:YAG LAI (PIPS or SWEEPS) as irrigant activator, with or without adjunct transcutaneous LLLT
**BECAUSE** SWEEPS produced SMD = −1.57 and PIPS SMD = −1.10 pain reduction vs. UAI at 24–48 h in a 7-RCT meta-analysis (n = 490) [[PMID:40818665]]

**IF** the primary goal is *periapical lesion healing* in a single-canal permanent tooth at 12 months
**THEN** use standard chemo-mechanical preparation as the primary approach; do not substitute laser adjuncts for evidence-based irrigant protocols
**BECAUSE** LAI-SWEEPS showed 86.9% lesion reduction vs. 74.5% for manual agitation but the difference was not statistically significant (p > 0.05, n = 56) [[PMID:38878107]], and GRADE certainty for laser adjuncts on AP healing is very low to low [[PMID:36156804]]

**IF** the case involves vital pulp therapy (full coronal pulpotomy) in a permanent molar with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis
**THEN** diode laser (GaAlAs, 980 nm) hemostasis is an acceptable alternative to 2.5% NaOCl hemostasis before bioceramic placement
**BECAUSE** 18-month success rates were 93% (laser) vs. 88.4% (NaOCl) with no statistically significant difference, but laser produced significantly less pain at 48 h (p < 0.001) and thicker dentine bridges at 18 months (p = 0.0479) [[PMID:38157279]]

**IF** the case is a *combined endo-perio lesion* with baseline probing depth ≥ 6 mm
**THEN** adjunct water laser therapy combined with RCT and scaling/root planing is preferred over conventional RCT + SRP alone
**BECAUSE** water laser adjunct achieved composite success RR = 1.22 (95% CI: 1.10–1.36) with greater benefit in deep pockets, and fewer acute exacerbations within 7 days (RR = 0.52) [[PMID:42482547]]

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

**Laser adjuncts cannot replace NaOCl — sequence matters.** The foundational principle across all four modules is that conventional chemomechanical preparation with NaOCl remains the disinfection standard; laser systems are strictly adjunctive. When diode laser alone was tested against 5.25% NaOCl + hydrogen peroxide irrigation, bacterial growth was detected in 8/30 vs. 3/30 samples, respectively [[PMID:24326811]], and the 2026 PROSPERO-registered umbrella review classifies laser-assisted irrigation as having "unproven clinical benefit" as a standalone treatment [[PMID:41833582]]. Always irrigate with 5.25% NaOCl between each instrument file before any laser step is activated.

**Er:YAG SWEEPS/PIPS is the only laser modality with robust evidence for postoperative pain reduction.** A 7-RCT meta-analysis (n = 490 teeth) confirmed LAI reduces postoperative pain vs. UAI overall (SMD = −0.58), but subgroup analysis shows SWEEPS drove the effect (SMD = −1.57) and diode LAI contributed nothing significant (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. If the clinical goal is pain management, select pulsed Er:YAG and combine it with transcutaneous LLLT — the combined protocol reduced analgesic consumption to 0.53 ± 0.74 tablets over 14 days [[PMID:41063319]].

**Emerging: Laser adjuncts may improve periapical healing selectively in multi-rooted, anatomically complex teeth.** No laser modality has demonstrated a statistically significant healing advantage over conventional irrigation in single-canal teeth at 12 months [[PMID:38878107]] [[PMID:36156804]]. However, Er:YAG LAI produced significantly greater hydrogel removal from isthmus structures than ultrasonic or sonic activation (p<0.05) [[PMID:38382735]], and one prospective RCT showed statistically significant periapical lesion reduction at 6 months in multi-rooted teeth treated with Er:YAG LAI + PBM vs. chemo-mechanical preparation alone [[PMID:40135760]]. Reserve laser adjuncts for complex anatomy; do not apply them uniformly to all cases expecting a healing benefit.

**Diode laser at 1.2 W (980 nm) is the safest intracanal power setting when Er:YAG is unavailable.** At 2 W the external root surface temperature rise is 10.20 °C; at 3 W it is 16.79 °C; at 1.2 W it is 6.25 °C — with equivalent bactericidal effect on *E. faecalis* across all three settings [[PMID:29378981]]. Nd:YAG at 200 mJ produces a temperature rise of 61.8 ± 4.2 °C and should be avoided for intracanal use given the periradicular thermal risk [[PMID:11806644]]. When only a diode system is available, redirect it to extraoral LLLT or aPDT rather than intracanal LAI.

**Divided: aPDT efficacy as an antimicrobial adjunct remains contested.** Twelve of 17 reviewed studies found effective *E. faecalis* elimination with aPDT [[PMID:24192536]], and meta-analyses confirm postoperative VAS reductions at 24 h (SMD = −1.21) and 72 h (SMD = −0.63) [[PMID:41388259]]. Against this, a 36-patient single-visit retreatment RCT found no significant difference in periapical healing at 12 months between UAI alone and UAI + PDT (p = 0.970) [[PMID:38013016]], and Siddiqui et al. noted 4 of 17 studies found conventional irrigation superior. Use aPDT as a targeted adjunct in retreatment and high-pain cases, not as a universal supplement; apply photosensitiser (methylene blue 0.005% or toluidine blue 0.1 mg/mL) for a 1–5 minute contact time before 630–660-nm activation.

## The Final Verdict

### 1. Decision Rule

**Clinical question:** Should I add a laser adjunct to conventional chemo-mechanical root canal treatment, and if so, which one?

**If** the tooth has confirmed apical periodontitis (PAI ≥ 2) **AND** is single-rooted with straight anatomy **AND** the primary treatment goal is periapical healing → **complete standard chemo-mechanical preparation with NaOCl as the sole disinfection strategy; no laser adjunct is mandated.** Add pulsed Er:YAG LAI (SWEEPS or PIPS: 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs, 300 μm P400FL tip at canal entrance, 3 × 20 s at 3 mL/cycle) only if postoperative pain control is an explicit secondary goal.

**If** the tooth has apical periodontitis (PAI ≥ 3) **AND** is multi-rooted with complex anatomy (isthmuses, lateral canals, curved canals, dens invaginatus) OR is an endodontic retreatment case → **add Er:YAG LAI (SWEEPS or PIPS) to NaOCl-based chemomechanical preparation** as the preferred laser adjunct; supplement with adjunctive aPDT (phenothiazinium photosensitiser, 630–660 nm activation) in retreatment cases where calcium hydroxide dependence reduction is sought.

**If** the primary intraoperative goal is postoperative pain reduction **AND** only a diode laser (810–980 nm) is available → **do not use diode intracanal LAI**; redirect to transcutaneous LLLT (applied to buccal/lingual mucosa post-obturation) or intracanal aPDT as the appropriate diode application.

**If** the case is vital pulp therapy in a permanent molar and haemostasis with 2.5% NaOCl is not achieved within 5 minutes → **switch to diode laser (GaAlAs 980 nm) haemostasis** before bioceramic (Biodentine) placement.

---

### 2. Evidence Anchor

**Branch A (single-rooted, standard anatomy, healing goal — conventional irrigation alone):**
**Anchored in:** Level I systematic review of 13 RCTs + 1 retrospective cohort (Meire et al., 2023; GRADE very low–low certainty) [[PMID:36156804]], corroborated by Level I RCT (n=56, 12-month CBCT, Doğan et al., 2024) showing no statistically significant healing benefit of LAI-SWEEPS vs. manual agitation (p>0.05) [[PMID:38878107]].

**Branch B (multi-rooted / complex anatomy / retreatment — Er:YAG LAI + aPDT adjunct):**
**Anchored in:** Level II prospective RCT (Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025) demonstrating statistically significant 6-month periapical lesion reduction in multi-rooted teeth with Er:YAG LAI + PBM [[PMID:40135760]]; Level II prospective in vitro study showing superior isthmus debridement with Er:YAG LAI vs. all comparators (p<0.05) [[PMID:38382735]]; retrospective cohort (n=214, Level III, Conejero et al., 2021) for aPDT in retreatment [[PMID:33731503]] — no prospective RCT has confirmed the retreatment aPDT branch.

**Branch C (diode-only system — redirect to LLLT/aPDT):**
**Anchored in:** Level I meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n=490, Sabeti et al., 2026) demonstrating diode LAI SMD = 0.03 (non-significant) for pain, versus SWEEPS SMD = −1.57 and LLLT SMD = −0.86 at 24 h [[PMID:40818665]] [[PMID:39287434]].

**Branch D (vital pulp therapy haemostasis — diode laser):**
**Anchored in:** Level II prospective RCT (n=86, Swetha et al., 2024) showing 93% vs. 88.4% 18-month success (p not significant) but significantly less 48 h pain and thicker dentine bridges with 980-nm GaAlAs laser [[PMID:38157279]]. No head-to-head multicentre RCT has yet evaluated diode vs. NaOCl haemostasis in VPT with ≥24-month follow-up.

---

### 3. Where Uncertainty Remains

- **Optimal laser parameters for LAI have not been standardised across trials.** Er:YAG energy settings range from 25 mJ [[PMID:41519256]] to 75–100 mJ [[PMID:38662505]] across cited studies; NaOCl concentration during activation ranges from 2% [[PMID:40135760]] to 5.25% [[PMID:39955574]]; pulse duration, tip size, and tip position vary. No head-to-head RCT has compared parameter permutations directly within the same patient population. A factorial-design multicentre RCT with standardised tip position and concentration would resolve this.

- **The healing benefit of laser adjuncts in multi-rooted teeth beyond 6 months is unconfirmed.** The only positive healing RCT (Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al., 2025) reports 6-month lesion size data in a single centre [[PMID:40135760]]; whether the advantage is sustained at 12 and 24 months is unknown. A minimum 24-month CBCT follow-up RCT specifically powered for multi-rooted teeth is required.

- **The clinical significance of diode LAI dentin structural damage is unquantified.** Ex vivo FTIR data from 42 premolars suggest 980-nm diode LAI causes measurable collagen and mineral degradation [[PMID:41519256]], but no prospective clinical cohort has correlated these microstructural changes with root fracture rate or long-term tooth survival. A prospective cohort tracking teeth treated with vs. without diode LAI over ≥5 years with micro-CT correlation would resolve this.

- **aPDT efficacy in retreatment rests entirely on retrospective evidence.** The claim that aPDT reduces calcium hydroxide interappointment dressing need from 82.7% to 17% is derived from a single retrospective cohort (n=214, no randomisation, no blinding) [[PMID:33731503]]; no prospective RCT evaluating this outcome exists. A multicentre parallel-arm RCT in retreatment cases with confirmed persistent periapical pathosis, powered for calcium hydroxide requirement as a primary endpoint, is needed.

- **Long-term survival data (≥5 years) for laser-treated teeth are absent from the evidence base.** All RCTs and prospective studies in this curriculum have a maximum follow-up of 18 months; the 2023 ESE systematic review [[PMID:36156804]] required only ≥12-month follow-up to be included, and no laser-specific study reported 5-year outcomes. Prospective registry-based cohort studies tracking laser-treated vs. conventionally-treated teeth through electronic dental records would provide the survival data clinicians need.

---

### 4. When NOT to Apply This Rule

- **Vertical root fracture confirmed on CBCT or direct visualisation.** No adjunct laser protocol improves prognosis when structural tooth integrity is compromised. → Action: extract or refer to oral surgery; do not invest in laser adjunct preparation.

- **Active dentoalveolar abscess with systemic signs (fever ≥38 °C, trismus, facial swelling involving deep spaces, lymphadenopathy).** The decision rule assumes a contained endodontic infection amenable to single- or two-visit root canal treatment. Systemic spread requires urgent incision and drainage, antibiotic therapy, and possible hospitalisation. → Action: refer to oral and maxillofacial surgery or emergency medicine; laser disinfection is not the primary intervention.

- **Non-restorable tooth, inadequate ferrule, or severely compromised periodontium (bone loss ≥ 70%, mobility grade III).** Laser adjunct selection presupposes that the tooth is worth treating endodontically. Where restorability is in doubt, extraction and implant-based or prosthetic alternatives should be discussed before any laser protocol is considered. → Action: refer to restorative or prosthodontic specialist for treatment-planning conference before initiating RCT.
