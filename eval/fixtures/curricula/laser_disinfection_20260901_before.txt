# Use of Lasers in Root Canal Disinfection
### A 20-Minute Endodontic Teaching Curriculum

---

## OVERVIEW

Laser-assisted disinfection represents one of the most rapidly evolving adjuncts in contemporary endodontics, yet its clinical evidence base remains heterogeneous and frequently misunderstood. This curriculum equips the clinician to select the appropriate laser platform for a given clinical scenario, execute validated protocols with correct parameters, critically interpret outcome data, and recognise where high-quality evidence ends and emerging or provisional evidence begins. Across four modules — covering laser physics and mechanisms, clinical assessment and device selection, activation protocols and technique, and clinical outcomes with adverse effects — learners will develop a decision-ready framework grounded in the highest-tier available evidence [[PMID:41833582]]. The central lesson: lasers are adjuncts, not replacements, for chemomechanical disinfection, and Er:YAG-based photoacoustic systems currently carry the strongest clinical evidence for pain reduction and irrigant activation.

---

## Module 1 — Laser Physics and Endodontic Disinfection Mechanisms

**The biology of the problem.** Endodontic infections are characterised by complex polymicrobial communities organised into biofilms within a canal system whose lateral canals, isthmuses, and dentinal tubules resist conventional chemomechanical debridement [[PMID:40565094]]. Chemomechanical preparation leaves bacterial reservoirs precisely in these anatomically inaccessible regions [[PMID:40136729]]. Lasers have been introduced as adjuncts to address this limitation — but understanding *which* laser does *what* and *why* is prerequisite to rational clinical use.

**Laser wavelengths and tissue targets.** Four laser systems dominate the endodontic literature: Er:YAG (2,940 nm), Er,Cr:YSGG (2,780 nm), Nd:YAG (1,064 nm), and diode lasers (broadly 400–980 nm) [[PMID:41833582]]. Wavelength determines tissue interaction: erbium wavelengths are strongly absorbed by water, making them the primary choice for photoacoustic irrigant activation, smear-layer removal, and selective dentin ablation with lower thermal risk than other systems [[PMID:40136729]] [[PMID:41505924]]. Nd:YAG (1,064 nm) is absorbed by pigmented tissues and exerts its antimicrobial action principally through photothermal effects — protein denaturation, cell-membrane disruption, and degradation of mixed-species biofilms — with the capacity to penetrate dentinal tubules [[PMID:40565094]]. Diode lasers (most commonly 808–980 nm) are absorbed by chromophores in bacterial cell walls and haemoglobin, giving them bactericidal and haemostatic properties at power outputs ranging from 50 mW to 5,000 mW [[PMID:30836213]].

**Mechanisms of disinfection: three distinct pathways.**

- **Photothermal:** Direct laser energy heats irrigant and tissue, denaturing bacterial proteins and disrupting membrane integrity; Nd:YAG is the principal photothermal agent [[PMID:40565094]].
- **Photoacoustic / cavitation:** Pulsed erbium lasers generate expanding and imploding vapour bubbles around the fibre tip; these cavitation dynamics produce intense fluid streaming (photon-induced photoacoustic streaming, PIPS; shock wave-enhanced emission photoacoustic streaming, SWEEPS) that drives irrigant into lateral canals and dentinal tubules [[PMID:38340037]] [[PMID:40136729]]. Bubble diameter, expansion velocity, and lifetime vary with pulse energy and irrigant composition — in a controlled study, 17% EDTA produced the largest cavitation bubble diameter (4.83 mm at 50 mJ/pulse) while 5.25% NaOCl produced the lowest expansion velocity (16.17 mm/ms at 50 mJ/pulse) under nanosecond Nd:YAG irradiation [[PMID:41704064]].
- **Photodynamic (aPDT):** A photosensitiser (methylene blue 0.005% or toluidine blue 0.1 mg/mL) applied to the canal absorbs wavelength-matched light (630–660 nm), generating reactive oxygen species that oxidise bacterial membranes; this mechanism is oxygen-dependent and does not require photothermal temperatures [[PMID:24192536]] [[PMID:35996480]].

**What the highest-tier evidence actually concludes.** The most comprehensive synthesis available — a PROSPERO-registered umbrella review of 9 systematic reviews evaluating Er:YAG, Er,Cr:YSGG, Nd:YAG, and diode systems (Duarte et al., 2026) — concludes that the available evidence on laser-activated irrigation (LAI) remains **limited and heterogeneous**, with inconsistent findings constrained by variability in laser wavelengths, study designs, and outcome measures [[PMID:41833582]]. The clinical significance statement from this umbrella review is unambiguous: laser-assisted irrigation should currently be regarded as an adjunct with **unproven clinical benefit**, pending well-designed studies with standardised protocols [[PMID:41833582]]. Laboratory data in extracted teeth suggest LAI is superior to conventional irrigation for disinfection, with a trend toward higher antimicrobial efficacy than ultrasonic activation [[PMID:38340037]] — but this has not translated to consistent superiority in clinical trials.

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Laboratory-based systematic reviews consistently report superior bacterial reduction with Er:YAG LAI versus conventional irrigation and ultrasonic methods [[PMID:40136729]] [[PMID:40558896]], and Nd:YAG photothermal irradiation significantly reduces total microbial load though without achieving complete sterilisation [[PMID:40565094]]. PDT against *E. faecalis* presents a more equivocal picture: of 17 studies reviewed by Siddiqui et al. (2013), 12 reported PDT effective, 4 found conventional irrigation superior, and 1 found equivalence — efficacy remains questionable [[PMID:24192536]]. The Duarte et al. umbrella review (2026) — the highest-tier source — supersedes isolated laboratory findings and places all these results within an overarching conclusion of insufficient clinical evidence [[PMID:41833582]]. Current standard-of-care continues to position chemomechanical preparation with NaOCl and EDTA as the primary disinfection strategy; lasers are adjuncts only.

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Establish working length.** Determine electronic apex locator working length confirmed by periapical radiograph before any laser instrumentation. Laser tips must remain ≥1 mm short of working length to prevent apical extrusion.

2. **Complete chemomechanical preparation first.** Shape canals to minimum apical size #25/.06 taper using rotary files. Lasers augment — they do not replace — this step [[PMID:41833582]].

3. **Primary irrigation with 5.25% NaOCl.** Irrigate with 5.25% NaOCl (or 2% NaOCl for LAI protocols per Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al.) using a side-vented needle placed 1 mm short of working length [[PMID:40135760]].

4. **Select laser mode for Er:YAG LAI (if chosen).** Set Er:YAG parameters to 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W output, 300 μs pulse duration, using a 300 μm diameter tip placed passively in the canal [[PMID:40135760]].

5. **Activate irrigant with LAI.** With 2% NaOCl present in the canal, activate using Er:YAG at the parameters above (fluence 71.4 J/cm²; power density 1,428.6 W/cm²); repeat for 17% EDTA as the final rinse [[PMID:40135760]].

6. **For aPDT (when selected as adjunct).** Apply photosensitiser — toluidine blue 0.1 mg/mL or methylene blue 0.005% — coat canal walls for 60 seconds. Irradiate at 630–660 nm, 100 mW, for 90 seconds (total energy: 9.0 J) using a 300 μm light diffusor inserted 2 mm short of working length [[PMID:30797116]].

7. **Final flush.** Irrigate with 17% EDTA (1 mL per canal, 60-second contact time — Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. did not report exact volume) to remove smear layer after laser activation [[PMID:40135760]].

8. **Dry and obturate.** Dry with paper points; obturate with thermoplasticised gutta-percha and AH Plus sealer (epoxy resin-based), as used in the Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. protocol [[PMID:40135760]].

9. **Post-operative photobiomodulation (optional adjunct for retreatment cases).** Apply 635 nm diode at 400 mW, 5 seconds per point, dose 2 J per point (4 J/cm²), 2 application points at apex level, over 4 sessions: day 0, 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the tooth is a vital or necrotic permanent molar requiring primary root canal treatment with no prior instrumentation
**THEN** use conventional chemomechanical preparation + 5.25% NaOCl as primary protocol; offer Er:YAG LAI as adjunct only
**BECAUSE** the highest-tier evidence (umbrella review, 9 SRs) classifies LAI as an adjunct with unproven clinical superiority over optimised conventional irrigation [[PMID:41833582]]

**IF** the tooth requires endodontic retreatment with a confirmed periapical lesion on CBCT
**THEN** add Er:YAG LAI (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μm tip) during 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA irrigation, plus post-operative 635 nm PBM (400 mW, 2 J/point, 4 sessions)
**BECAUSE** this combined protocol produced significantly smaller periapical lesions at 6 months (mean 1.55 ±0.51 mm vs. 1.95 ±0.71 mm in controls, p<0.05) and lower postoperative VAS pain [[PMID:40135760]]

**IF** instrumentation is blocked by a separated file and apical patency cannot be achieved
**THEN** consider aPDT adjunct (660 nm, 100 mW, 90 s, methylene blue 0.005%, 300 μm diffusor tip at 2 mm short of working length) to the accessible canal segment
**BECAUSE** this recommendation rests on a single case report of one tooth (Amaral et al. 2019 [[PMID:30797116]]) demonstrating 5-year periapical healing — no RCT or prospective cohort has evaluated aPDT in canal blockage specifically; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**IF** the patient is a child requiring pulpotomy in a primary tooth
**THEN** diode laser pulpotomy may be used; wavelength did not significantly influence outcome (p>0.05), and diode laser reduced pathological periodontal ligament space widening vs. controls (p<0.05) when ZOE was the capping material
**BECAUSE** a systematic review and meta-analysis (Cao et al., 2026) found equivalent clinical/radiographic success rates vs. conventional methods [[PMID:42092871]]

**IF** the case involves a periodontic-endodontic lesion requiring surgical intervention
**THEN** add dual-wavelength laser adjunct: Nd:YAG for root canal disinfection + Er:YAG for periodontal debridement
**BECAUSE** an RCT (n=106, 6-month follow-up) showed significantly lower bacterial infection rates and greater reductions in IL-1β, IL-6, and MMP-8 in the laser group vs. conventional flap surgery alone (p<0.05) [[PMID:41918875]]

## Module 2 — Clinical Assessment and Laser Selection for Canal Treatment

**Selecting the right laser modality begins with a diagnosis, not a device.** Before any laser enters the operatory, the clinician must characterise pulpal status, periapical pathology, root canal anatomy, and the microbial challenge — each of which directly determines which laser platform and protocol will provide the greatest benefit.

**Laser-Activated Irrigation (LAI): Evidence Summary**

The highest-quality evidence for lasers in canal treatment addresses LAI — the use of pulsed laser energy to activate irrigants within the prepared canal. A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of seven RCTs (n = 490 teeth) found that LAI significantly reduced postoperative pain compared with ultrasonic activation (SMD = −0.58; 95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; P = .0016), with the greatest pain reduction occurring at 24–48 hours post-treatment (SMD = −1.00) [[PMID:40818665]]. Critically, not all laser types performed equally: pulsed Er:YAG modalities — specifically PIPS (SMD = −1.10) and SWEEPS (SMD = −1.57) — demonstrated the strongest analgesic benefit, while diode LAI showed no significant effect (SMD = 0.03) compared with ultrasonic activation [[PMID:40818665]]. This finding directly governs laser selection: when LAI is the primary goal, Er:YAG-based systems are the evidence-supported default.

A complementary 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO-registered) of 22 studies (n = 892 patients) confirmed that LLLT (SMD = −0.86 at 24 h; SMD = −0.64 at 48 h), diode LAI (SMD = −0.27 at 48 h), and aPDT (SMD = −1.12 at 24 h) all reduced postoperative pain intensity compared with conventional treatment, though no significant differences in pain incidence were demonstrated for LLLT versus control [[PMID:39287434]]. These findings should be read alongside the 2016 Cochrane review (Del Fabbro et al.), which found no evidence that LLLT prevented postoperative pain in the surgical retreatment context (very low-quality evidence), noting that its included studies examined a distinctly different clinical scenario (post-surgical irradiation) [[PMID:27759881]].

**Smear Layer Removal and Canal Disinfection: Laser Selection by Mechanism**

An in vitro study of 160 single-rooted premolars compared Er,Cr:YSGG (25 mJ, 50 Hz, radial-firing tip), diode laser (pulsed 50%, 1.5 W), PUI, and conventional syringe-needle irrigation across four 15-second activation cycles [[PMID:41389357]]. Er,Cr:YSGG achieved the lowest smear layer scores across all root thirds, outperforming all other modalities; when combined with NaOCl + EDTA, it produced superior smear layer removal (p < 0.05) [[PMID:41389357]]. Diode laser activation also outperformed PUI, and notably, Er,Cr:YSGG with saline alone surpassed NaOCl without activation, underscoring that photoacoustic/cavitation effects — not irrigant chemistry alone — drive Er:YAG and Er,Cr:YSGG efficacy [[PMID:41389357]].

A 2026 systematic review of the GentleWave system reported that Er:YAG lasers produced comparable debris removal to GentleWave multisonic activation, though consistent superiority of any single device over PUI was not demonstrated across all included studies due to heterogeneity [[PMID:41514415]].

**Antimicrobial Photodynamic Therapy (aPDT): Adjunctive Role**

A 2025 PROSPERO-registered systematic review (11 studies) confirmed that aPDT as an adjunct to chemo-mechanical irrigation significantly reduced postoperative pain at 24 h (SMD: −1.21) and 72 h (SMD: −0.63), and that six of the included studies suggested microbial load reduction, though heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis of microbiological outcomes [[PMID:41388259]]. Only three of the eleven included studies had a low risk of bias — treat aPDT microbiological conclusions as promising but provisional, pending high-quality RCTs.

**Photosensitizer selection for aPDT:** Phenothiazinium salts (methylene blue, toluidine blue) are the most studied photosensitizers in endodontic aPDT [[PMID:37195228]]. One case series used toluidine blue at 0.1 mg/mL activated by an LED lamp at 630 nm, 3 W/cm², and demonstrated periapical healing at 12 months [[PMID:41789272]] — this recommendation rests on a single case report (n = 1, Zargar et al. 2025) — no RCT has yet evaluated this precise aPDT protocol in standard orthograde treatment; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**Laser-Assisted Pulpotomy: Haemostasis and Disinfection**

A retrospective study of 65 patients with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis found overall MTA pulpotomy success of 87.69% at 12 months. KTP laser haemostasis/disinfection produced a 90.5% success rate versus 86.4% for both saline and NaOCl — a difference that was not statistically significant (P > 0.05), indicating that KTP laser is an acceptable but not superior alternative to conventional agents for pulp chamber disinfection [[PMID:41121058]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Establish diagnosis and periapical status.** Record pulp status (symptomatic/asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis, necrosis ± apical periodontitis) using cold testing, percussion, and periapical radiography. Reserve CBCT for cases with complex anatomy or large periapical lesions — Del Fabbro et al. found no healing advantage for CBCT over radiography in straightforward retreatment cases [[PMID:27759881]].

2. **Select laser platform based on primary goal.**
   - Canal disinfection/LAI: Er:YAG or Er,Cr:YSGG (pulsed; PIPS or SWEEPS protocol preferred) [[PMID:40818665]].
   - Haemostasis/pulp chamber disinfection: KTP or diode (810–980 nm) — acceptable alternative to NaOCl; no statistically significant outcome difference [[PMID:41121058]].
   - Postoperative pain reduction (adjunct): LLLT 635–810 nm externally applied, or aPDT with phenothiazinium photosensitizer [[PMID:39287434]] [[PMID:41388259]].

3. **Complete chemo-mechanical preparation before LAI.** Shape canals to working length using NiTi rotary instrumentation. Final apical size and taper are not specified in the cited LAI studies — do not substitute LAI for mechanical debridement.

4. **Irrigate with 2% NaOCl + 17% EDTA sequence prior to laser activation.** Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. used 2% NaOCl and 17% EDTA for LAI with Er:YAG laser parameters: 50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs pulse length, 300 μm tip diameter [[PMID:40135760]].

5. **Activate irrigant with Er:YAG (LAI).** Insert a 300 μm radial-firing tip to working length. Activate for four 15-second cycles per canal [[PMID:41389357]]. Do not press the tip against canal walls — position centrally.

6. **Final flush with 17% EDTA** to chelate residual smear layer; Abaza et al. confirmed NaOCl + EDTA combination with Er,Cr:YSGG produced superior smear layer removal over all other tested irrigant/activation combinations [[PMID:41389357]].

7. **Apply aPDT if adjunctive antimicrobial benefit is desired.** Introduce photosensitizer (phenothiazinium salt; concentration per manufacturer — Das et al. did not report a universal concentration) to canal; pre-irradiation time 1–5 minutes; activate with diode laser at 630–660 nm [[PMID:37195228]] [[PMID:41388259]].

8. **Apply LLLT for postoperative pain reduction.** 810 nm semiconductor laser; irradiate buccal and palatal/lingual mucosa externally at the apex level. Jian et al. found LLLT alone produced the best immediate (day 1) pain reduction versus LAI alone or control; combined LAI + LLLT produced the greatest sustained analgesic effect (0.53 ± 0.74 analgesic tablets used over 14 days) [[PMID:41063319]].

9. **Apply photobiomodulation (PBM) post-obturation if retreatment.** 635 nm, 400 mW, 5 seconds per point, 2 J per point, 4 J/cm², at 2 apical points; repeat at 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h post-treatment [[PMID:40135760]].

10. **Obturate with thermoplastic gutta-percha + appropriate sealer.** Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. used thermocondensed gutta-percha with AH Plus; CBCT at 6 months demonstrated significantly smaller periapical lesion size in the Er:YAG + PBM group (mean 1.55 ± 0.51 mm) versus conventional (1.95 ± 0.71 mm; p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the primary clinical goal is reducing postoperative pain AND the case involves multi-rooted teeth with symptomatic apical periodontitis
**THEN** select combined LAI (Er:YAG/SWEEPS or PIPS) + LLLT over ultrasonic activation alone
**BECAUSE** meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n=490) showed LAI reduced postoperative pain vs. UAI (SMD = −0.58), with SWEEPS showing strongest effect (SMD = −1.57); combined LAI + LLLT yielded lowest analgesic use (0.53 tablets/14 days) [[PMID:40818665]] [[PMID:41063319]]

**IF** the primary goal is smear layer removal and canal disinfection in an anatomically complex case (isthmuses, lateral canals, apical deltas)
**THEN** use Er:YAG or Er,Cr:YSGG LAI with NaOCl + EDTA irrigation sequence
**BECAUSE** Er,Cr:YSGG achieved superior smear layer scores across all thirds, including apical, over PUI and diode laser; NaOCl + EDTA was the most effective irrigant combination (p < 0.05) [[PMID:41389357]]

**IF** the tooth is a permanent molar with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis requiring pulpotomy and haemostasis is difficult
**THEN** KTP laser (or diode) haemostasis/disinfection is an acceptable option equivalent to NaOCl or saline
**BECAUSE** retrospective cohort (n=65, 12-month follow-up) showed no statistically significant difference in MTA pulpotomy success between KTP laser (90.5%), NaOCl (86.4%), and saline (86.4%; P > 0.05) [[PMID:41121058]]

**IF** the case is a diode LAI setup (810–976 nm) and the primary intent is irrigant activation for disinfection
**THEN** switch to Er:YAG/Er,Cr:YSGG or supplement with PUI, because diode LAI showed no significant pain reduction vs. UAI (SMD = 0.03) and inferior smear layer removal versus Er,Cr:YSGG
**BECAUSE** [[PMID:40818665]] [[PMID:41389357]]

**IF** the case involves endo-perio lesion with probing depth ≥ 6 mm
**THEN** adjunctive water laser therapy (Er,Cr:YSGG or similar) is indicated alongside conventional RCT and SRP
**BECAUSE** retrospective cohort (n=634, 12 ± 3 months) showed composite success RR = 1.22 (95% CI: 1.10–1.36), with greatest benefit at baseline PD ≥ 6 mm [[PMID:42482547]]

## Module 3 — Laser Activation Protocols and Disinfection Technique

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** The most current umbrella review [[PMID:41833582]] — encompassing 9 systematic reviews across Er:YAG, Er,Cr:YSGG, Nd:YAG, and diode systems — concludes that evidence for laser-activated irrigation (LAI) remains "limited and heterogeneous," with inconsistent findings across laser wavelengths and outcome measures. Conversely, an Er:YAG-specific systematic review [[PMID:40136729]] found that all 15 included studies reported significantly improved disinfection over conventional or ultrasonic methods. The higher-quality, broader umbrella review [[PMID:41833582]] should govern the clinician's expectation: LAI is currently an adjunct with unproven generalizable clinical benefit, not a replacement for chemomechanical disinfection.

**Mechanism of Laser-Activated Irrigation**

LAI efficacy is driven primarily by cavitation — laser pulses generate expanding and imploding vapour bubbles around the fibre tip, producing intense fluid dynamics that propagate irrigant into lateral canals, isthmuses, and dentinal tubules [[PMID:38340037]]. Wavelength, pulse energy, pulse duration, and fibre-tip geometry each modulate cavitation intensity [[PMID:38340037]]. Er:YAG systems exploit strong water absorption to generate photoacoustic streaming (PIPS) and shock wave-enhanced photoacoustic streaming (SWEEPS), enabling use of reduced NaOCl concentrations without sacrificing efficacy [[PMID:40136729]]. Nd:YAG lasers act primarily through photothermal effects — protein denaturation, membrane disruption, and dentinal tubule penetration — though complete sterilisation is not consistently achieved [[PMID:40565094]].

**LAI vs. Conventional Irrigation: Anatomic Reach**

In a 3D-printed curved root canal model (60° curvature, radius 5 mm, size 25/.06), Er:YAG LAI using a P400FL tip at the canal entrance, activated at 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 μs pulse duration, with 3 × 20 s per canal at 3 mL/20 s, achieved the greatest overall hydrogel removal from the entire canal system compared with conventional needle irrigation, manual agitation, EndoActivator, EDDY sonic, and passive ultrasonic irrigation [[PMID:38382735]]. In the apical isthmus specifically, LAI, sonic (EDDY), and conventional needle irrigation performed comparably — no single technique achieved complete debridement [[PMID:38382735]].

**Irrigant Penetration and Dentinal Tubule Depth**

Diode laser activation of 5.25% NaOCl produced the highest dentinal tubule penetration at all canal levels (coronal, middle, and apical) compared with passive ultrasonic activation and conventional needle irrigation; penetration decreased from coronal to apical third in every group [[PMID:42163172]]. Super-oxidised water showed comparable penetration to 5.25% NaOCl when both were laser-activated, with no statistically significant difference between the two irrigants under activated conditions [[PMID:42163172]].

**Antimicrobial Photodynamic Therapy (aPDT) as an Adjunct**

aPDT after chemomechanical disinfection (CD) significantly improved microbial load reduction versus CD alone in permanent teeth with primary endodontic infection (p = 0.04, 95% CI −1.72 to −0.05), with a particularly pronounced effect on anaerobic microorganisms (p = 0.003) [[PMID:37981598]]. Photosensitizer selection is critical: NaOCl + PDT with **methylene blue** significantly outperformed NaOCl alone in ex-vivo extracted human teeth infected with *E. faecalis* (SMD = 5.64; 95% CI 2.67–8.62; p = 0.0002; I² = 95%), whereas toluidine blue showed no significant additional benefit [[PMID:41692102]]. The high heterogeneity (I² = 95%) and exclusively ex-vivo design of all 7 included studies limit certainty; no clinical RCT in the evidence base directly compares NaOCl+MB-PDT against NaOCl alone for periapical healing outcomes.

Conversely, one RCT (n = 36, CBCT-measured periapical volumes, 1-year follow-up) found that adding PDT after UAI produced no significant improvement in periapical lesion healing during retreatment (median reduction 86.98% UAI vs. 79.72% UAI+PDT; p = 0.970) [[PMID:38013016]]. PDT should therefore be regarded as a potential microbial-load adjunct — its effect on radiographic healing remains unresolved at the clinical trial level.

aPDT does not negatively affect sealer bond strength to intraradicular dentine (p = 0.90; MD −0.03; 95% CI −0.46 to +0.41) [[PMID:36596331]], making it safe to sequence before obturation.

**Postoperative Pain**

PIPS Er:YAG LAI significantly reduced postoperative pain at 48 hours compared with conventional needle irrigation (MD = −0.78; 95% CI −1.39 to −0.17; p = 0.01; I² = 69%), but no significant differences were found at 1, 3, or 7 days; certainty of evidence is rated **low** [[PMID:39849275]]. In an RCT of 202 patients, the combined 810 nm semiconductor LAI + LLLT protocol produced the lowest VAS scores and least analgesic consumption (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets) over 14 days compared with control, LAI-only, and LLLT-only groups [[PMID:41063319]].

## Clinical Application

### 4a. Procedural Protocol

1. **Complete chemomechanical preparation first.** Shape to a minimum apical size 25/.06 with rotary instrumentation under primary irrigant. Laser activation is adjunctive and does not substitute for mechanical preparation [[PMID:41833582]].

2. **Establish and confirm working length.** Confirm electrometrically and radiographically before any laser fibre insertion. The evidence base does not specify a standardised safety margin for fibre-to-apex distance across laser systems; Donnermeyer et al. positioned the P400FL tip at the canal entrance rather than to working length for the LAI protocol studied [[PMID:38382735]].

3. **Primary LAI cycle — Er:YAG PIPS/SWEEPS protocol.** Insert P400FL conical fibre tip at canal entrance. Activate at 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 μs pulse duration. Deliver 3 mL of irrigant per 20-second activation cycle; repeat 3 × 20 s per canal [[PMID:38382735]].

4. **Diode LAI alternative (810–976 nm).** Munteanu et al. used a 200 µm fibre, 0.75 W pulsed mode, 15 Hz, 15 J dosage over 20 seconds (976 nm diode) for endodontic disinfection [[PMID:41470159]]. This rests on a single uncontrolled pilot series of 12 patients with no comparator arm — no RCT has validated this specific dosimetry; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

5. **Irrigant sequence during and after LAI.** Fahim et al. used 2.5% NaOCl with 17% EDTA as the conventional disinfection protocol; both dual-laser and combined laser groups outperformed this combination in bacterial reduction [[PMID:38403667]]. Pažin et al. used 3% NaOCl throughout the UAI protocol before PDT application [[PMID:38013016]]. The evidence base does not specify a single standardised LAI irrigant concentration or contact time applicable across all laser systems.

6. **aPDT adjunct — if selected.** Flood canal with methylene blue at 1.56 μM/mL concentration [[PMID:31301436]]. Allow 3-minute pre-irradiation dwell time. Insert optical fibre to working length. Apply 660 nm red laser at 100 mW continuous for 3 minutes (total energy 18 J) [[PMID:31301436]].

7. **Final canal drying.** Paper points to working length until no exudate is expressed. The evidence base does not report a standardised drying protocol duration.

8. **Proceed to obturation.** aPDT does not negatively affect sealer bond strength; no protocol modification to sealer type is required following aPDT [[PMID:36596331]].

### 4b. Decision Tree

**IF** the canal is accessible to fibre tip placement at the canal entrance and conventional irrigation is the baseline
**THEN** add Er:YAG LAI (P400FL tip at canal entrance; 25 pps, 50 mJ, 300 μs; 3 × 20 s) as a final activation step
**BECAUSE** LAI achieved the greatest overall hydrogel removal from the entire canal system versus all comparators in a standardised curved-canal in vitro model [[PMID:38382735]]

**IF** the case is retreatment with a large periapical lesion (>5 mm on CBCT) and UAI is the primary activation method
**THEN** do not substitute PDT for UAI or expect additive periapical healing benefit from PDT
**BECAUSE** one RCT (n=36, 1-year CBCT follow-up) found no significant difference in periapical lesion volume reduction between UAI and UAI+PDT (p = 0.970) [[PMID:38013016]]

**IF** aPDT is selected as an adjunct to chemomechanical disinfection
**THEN** use methylene blue (1.56 μM/mL, 660 nm, 100 mW, 3 min, 18 J) — not toluidine blue — as the photosensitizer
**BECAUSE** meta-analysis (7 ex-vivo studies, E. faecalis, CFU outcome) showed significant CFU benefit only for methylene blue (SMD=5.64, p=0.0002); toluidine blue showed no significant added effect [[PMID:41692102]]

**IF** the root has severely compromised wall thickness (<1 mm) where rotary instrumentation risk is high
**THEN** minimise mechanical instrumentation and rely on Er:YAG LAI for primary disinfection
**BECAUSE** Er:YAG LAI was the principal debridement strategy in a single case report (n=1) of a tooth with <1 mm wall thickness that achieved 56-month clinical and radiographic success [[PMID:42505761]] — this recommendation rests on a single case report; no RCT or prospective cohort has evaluated this approach; treat as provisional pending higher-tier confirmation.

**IF** postoperative pain reduction is a primary clinical goal alongside disinfection
**THEN** consider combined semiconductor LAI + LLLT (810 nm intracanal + extraoral mucosa application simultaneously)
**BECAUSE** a single-centre RCT (n=202) found combined LAI+LLLT required the fewest analgesics (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets) over 14 days versus LAI-only, LLLT-only, or control [[PMID:41063319]]

**IF** aPDT has been performed immediately before obturation
**THEN** proceed with sealer placement without modification to sealer type or technique
**BECAUSE** meta-analysis (8 in vitro studies) found no significant difference in sealer bond strength following aPDT (p=0.90; MD −0.03; 95% CI −0.46 to +0.41) [[PMID:36596331]]

## Module 4 — Clinical Outcomes, Success Rates, and Adverse Effects

**Postoperative Pain: The Strongest Clinical Signal**

The most consistent and best-evidenced clinical benefit of laser use in root canal disinfection is reduction of postoperative endodontic pain (PEP). A pre-registered systematic review and meta-analysis (Das et al., 2025) of 11 RCTs found that antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT) as an adjunct to chemo-mechanical preparation significantly reduced postoperative pain at both 24 hours (SMD = −1.21) and 72 hours (SMD = −0.63) [[PMID:41388259]]. A second pre-registered network meta-analysis (Luo et al., 2024, n = 892) confirmed that low-level laser therapy reduced pain at 24 h (SMD = −0.86, 95% CI: −1.16 to −0.55) and 48 h (SMD = −0.64), diode laser reduced pain at 48 h (SMD = −0.27), and aPDT reduced pain incidence (pooled RR = 0.47, 95% CI: 0.31–0.72); LLLT did not significantly reduce pain incidence at 12 h (RR = 0.89) or 24 h (RR = 0.57) [[PMID:39287434]].

A 2026 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n = 490 teeth) found LAI reduced PEP versus UAI (SMD = −0.58; 95% CI: −0.94 to −0.22; p = 0.0016), with the greatest effect at 24–48 hours (SMD = −1.00) [[PMID:40818665]]. Among laser modalities, pulsed Er:YAG techniques showed the strongest signal: PIPS (SMD = −1.10) and SWEEPS (SMD = −1.57), while diode LAI showed no significant effect (SMD = 0.03) [[PMID:40818665]]. An RCT by Jian et al. (2025, n = 202) demonstrated that combined 810 nm semiconductor LAI plus transcutaneous LLLT produced the lowest VAS scores throughout 14 days and the least analgesic consumption (0.53 ± 0.74 tablets), though all groups converged by day 14 [[PMID:41063319]].

**The literature is currently divided on this topic.** Regarding LLLT for postoperative pain after *surgical* retreatment, the 2016 Cochrane review by Del Fabbro et al. found no evidence that low-energy level laser therapy prevented postoperative pain after periapical surgery (very low-quality evidence) [[PMID:27759881]]. This contrasts with the positive signal for LLLT and LAI in non-surgical RCT contexts documented above. Current standard of care does not endorse LLLT as a replacement for analgesics in surgical retreatment pending higher-quality evidence.

**Periapical Lesion Healing**

A pre-registered RCT (Doğan et al., 2024, n = 56, 12-month CBCT follow-up) found lesion volume reduction of 86.9% for LAI-SWEEPS, 85.4% for PUI, 80.4% for manual dynamic activation, and 74.5% for controls — but with no statistically significant between-group difference (p > 0.05) in single-rooted teeth [[PMID:38878107]]. Kolberg-Babrzyńska et al. (2025, n = 40, 6-month CBCT) found periapical lesion size was significantly smaller in the group receiving Er:YAG LAI with 2% NaOCl (50 mJ, 25 Hz, 1 W, 300 μs, tip diameter 300 μm, fluence 71.4 J/cm²) plus 635 nm photobiomodulation (mean PL size 1.55 ± 0.51 mm) versus conventional retreatment (1.95 ± 0.71 mm, p < 0.05) [[PMID:40135760]]. In endo-perio lesions (Li et al., 2026, n = 634, 12 ± 3 months), adjunctive water laser yielded a composite success RR of 1.22 (95% CI: 1.10–1.36) and fewer acute exacerbations within 7 days (RR = 0.52, 95% CI: 0.30–0.88), with the greatest benefit when baseline probing depth was ≥6 mm [[PMID:42482547]].

Adding PDT after UAI in periapical retreatment (Pažin et al., 2024, n = 36, 1-year CBCT) produced no additional healing benefit: median periapical lesion reduction was 86.98% for UAI alone versus 79.72% for UAI+PDT (p = 0.970) [[PMID:38013016]].

**Vital Pulp Therapy Outcomes**

Ayhan et al. (2025) reported an overall 12-month MTA pulpotomy success rate of 87.69% (57/65 teeth) in permanent molars with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis; success rates were 86.4% for saline, 86.4% for NaOCl, and 90.5% for KTP laser, with no statistically significant difference (p > 0.05) [[PMID:41121058]]. Swetha et al. (2024, n = 86, 18-month follow-up) found that diode laser pulpotomy (GaAlAs 980 nm) with 2.5% NaOCl achieved 93.0% success versus 88.4% for conventional crown pulpotomy; CBCT-confirmed dentin bridge thickness was greater in the laser group (median 0.89 mm, IQR 1.06; p = 0.048), though overall clinical success difference was not statistically significant [[PMID:38157279]].
