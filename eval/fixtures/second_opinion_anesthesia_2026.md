## 1. The size of the problem (what IANB alone achieves)
- Pooled across 5,094 patients in the Zanjir NMA, only 52% achieved successful pulpal anesthesia during treatment of SIP mandibular molars [NMA, J Endod 2019].
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs reports IANB failure rates of roughly 12–65% in SIP, and found modified protocols were 3.3–3.6× more successful than a conventional 2% lidocaine IANB (OR 3.34; 95% CI 2.49–4.48) [SR/MA, Front Dent Med 2025].
- In a direct RCT, only 27% and 42% of SIP molar patients achieved pulpal anesthesia after IANB with 1.8 mL or 3.6 mL articaine respectively [RCT, PLoS One 2019].
- Lip numbness is not a success marker: sensitivity for predicting pulpal anesthesia was 0.03 for lip numbness, 0.35 for cold test, and none of the three tests was accurate enough to predict failure [cross-sectional diagnostic study — flagged: not an RCT, but it is the only direct diagnostic-accuracy data].
**Mechanism caveat:** claims about TTX-resistant sodium channels, lowered nociceptor thresholds, and central sensitization come from basic science and narrative reviews, not clinical trials. The one primary human study shows Nav1.8, a TTX-resistant channel expressed by nociceptors, is present in painful human pulp [observational immunohistochemistry]. Treat mechanism statements as biologically plausible, not trial-proven.
## 2. Optimizing the primary block
**Volume (strongest single lever).** 3.6 mL vs 1.8 mL for IANB in irreversible pulpitis gave RR 1.94 (95% CI 1.07–3.52); trial sequential analysis confirmed the benefit as conclusive and GRADE quality was 'high' [SR/MA + TSA, Int Endod J 2021].
**Anesthetic agent (evidence conflicts; effect is small).**
- An NMA of RCTs found only mepivacaine significantly increased IANB success vs lidocaine (RR 1.42; 95% CI 1.04–1.95), with no significant differences among mepivacaine, articaine and bupivacaine; quality very low to moderate [NMA, Int Endod J 2019].
- Another meta-analysis (19 trials) found 4% articaine 1.37× more successful than 2% lidocaine in mandibular teeth with irreversible pulpitis (RR 1.37; 95% CI 1.17–1.62) [SR/MA, PeerJ 2021].
- The most recent meta-analysis (RCTs to July 2025) found articaine gave faster onset, longer duration and lower injection pain, but success rates were comparable across techniques and subgroups [SR/MA, BMC Anesthesiol 2026].
- One RCT found prilocaine outperformed mepivacaine for IANB in SIP first molars: 78% vs 24% success on pulp chamber entry [RCT].
Bottom line: agent choice is a marginal gain; volume and supplementation are not.
**Alternative blocks.**
- Gow-Gates vs IANB: RR 1.10 (95% CI 0.82–1.48; I² 0%); mental/incisive vs IANB: RR 1.15 (0.97–1.36); cumulative success for all three techniques was low, TSA showed no firm evidence for Gow-Gates, and GRADE was moderate for Gow-Gates vs IANB [SR/MA + TSA, J Endod 2019].
- Gow-Gates + IANB combined reached 70% vs 40% (GG) and 44% (IANB) alone, but the authors concluded supplemental anesthesia was still required [RCT, J Endod 2018].
- Gow-Gates vs Vazirani-Akinosi in SIP with 3.6 mL lidocaine: pulpal success 35% vs 16% among patients with lip numbness; neither provided adequate anesthesia [RCT, J Endod 2015].
- For premolars specifically, a 2025 meta-analysis found no difference between mental/incisive block and IANB (OR 1.25; 95% CI 0.74–2.12), certainty very low [SR/MA, Cureus 2025].
**Buffered lidocaine (comfort, not efficacy).**
- Buffering gave 48 s faster onset and 5-point lower injection pain, but no difference in success rate (P = 0.290); evidence low to moderate [SR/MA, JDAPM 2018].
- Buffered vs non-buffered 2% lidocaine in SIP: 62.5% vs 47.5% (P = 0.381) [RCT, J Endod 2015]; buffered 4% lidocaine: 32% vs 40% (P = 0.40) [RCT, J Endod 2015].
## 3. Premedication (1 hour before the block)
**NSAIDs.**
- NSAID premedication increased IANB success in irreversible pulpitis; ibuprofen >400 mg, diclofenac and ketorolac all helped (RR 1.83, 2.56 and 2.07 respectively) [SR/MA + TSA, J Endod 2018].
- Ibuprofen 400, 600 and 800 mg improved success while 300 mg did not; dexamethasone, NSAIDs and tramadol all significantly increased success [NMA, Int Endod J 2018]. Paracetamol monotherapy does not improve anesthesia at any dose [NMA].
- Preemptive analgesics beat placebo overall (OR 0.30 for failure; 95% CI 0.24–0.39) [SR/MA, Clin Oral Investig 2017].
- Representative RCT: success 21.7% with no premedication vs 73.9% meloxicam and 78.3% ibuprofen given 1 hour pre-IANB, but neither provided profound anesthesia [RCT, Int Dent J 2020].
- Dissenting RCT: 800 mg ibuprofen gave 41% vs 35% placebo (P = 0.57) [RCT, J Endod 2010]. Premedication shifts odds; it does not replace supplementation.
**Corticosteroids.**
- Oral dexamethasone premedication improved IANB success vs placebo across 8 RCTs (RR 1.80; 95% CI from 1.35), TSA conclusive, with efficacy similar to NSAIDs [SR/MA + TSA, Int Endod J 2026].
- Earlier MA: dexamethasone OR 2.59 for anesthetic success (n = 502; 95% CI 1.46–4.59), plus lower pain at 6, 12 and 24 h [SR/MA, Pharmaceuticals 2022].
- Local routes also work: intraligamentary dexamethasone before IANB gave 73% success vs 32% control and 37% diclofenac [RCT, J Endod 2020]; adding 2 mg dexamethasone to the lidocaine cartridge improved IANB success [RCT, JDAPM 2022].
**Tramadol** — works locally (OR 2.2 for success) but adverse-effect risk OR 7.72 [SR/MA, Healthcare 2022]. Not a first choice.
## 4. Intra-appointment adjuncts
- **Cryotherapy:** across 6 RCTs (506 patients), an ice pack after IANB improved success (OR 1.44; 95% CI 1.04–2.00) and reduced intra- and postoperative pain; Endo Ice application did not [SR/MA, Cureus 2025]. A subsequent RCT found 79.3% vs 65.3% (NS) but significantly lower pain on access opening [RCT, 2025].
- **Magnesium sulfate as adjuvant:** 7 RCTs (398 patients) — higher anesthetic success (RR 1.61; 95% CI 1.06–2.44; I² 0%) and lower pain at 1 h [SR/MA, Cureus 2025]; representative RCT 82% vs 53% [RCT, Int Endod J 2019]. Off-label; not a marketed dental product.
- **Nitrous oxide + lidocaine/clonidine:** no significant difference in IANB success in SIP [RCT, J Endod 2021].
## 5. Supplemental techniques — ranked by evidence
**Overall:** supplementary (after failure) and combined (from the outset) strategies both beat IANB alone (SUPP RR 2.02, SUCRA 85%; COMB RR 1.86, SUCRA 65%) with no significant difference between them [NMA, Int Endod J 2025]. So test first, supplement only if needed.
**Intraosseous (IO) — highest ranked.**
- IO injection with 2% lidocaine or 4% articaine, or buccal + lingual articaine infiltration, were the superior strategies (very low to moderate quality) [NMA, J Endod 2019].
- Head-to-head RCT (160 patients): IANB+IO 92.5%, IANB+PDL 72.5%, IANB+BI 65%, IANB alone 40%; heart rate rose significantly only in the IO group [RCT, 2024].
- Computerized IO as *primary* technique: ICA 91.43% vs IANB 69.44% (P = 0.0034); maximum heart-rate increase was greater with ICA; authors judged it safe and efficient as first-intention [RCT, Int Endod J 2023].
**Intraligamentary (PDL) — strong second.**
- Supplemental IL after IANB: RR 3.56 (95% CI 2.86–4.44; I² 0%) [SR/MA, JDAPM 2022]. Within that review, 0.6 mL outperformed 0.2 mL (84% vs 64%, P = 0.03), and X-Tip IO reached 100% vs 70% for PDL in one included trial [SR/MA].
- Articaine vs lidocaine for supplemental IL: no significant difference; both more effective in second molars [RCT, 2021]; 82.6% vs 91.3% in first molars, NS [prospective comparative — flagged, not RCT].
**Buccal (± lingual) infiltration with articaine.**
- Supplemental infiltration with IANB improves success (RR 1.84; 95% CI 1.44–2.34), TSA adequate [SR/MA + TSA, JDAPM 2021].
- But molar rescue rates are modest: 42% first molars, 48% second molars vs 73% premolars [RCT, J Endod 2016].
- As a *primary* technique, 4% articaine BI is comparable to 2% lidocaine IANB for SIP first molars (RR 1.06; 95% CI 0.93–1.20), moderate certainty [SR/MA, Clin Oral Investig 2025].
- Lingual infiltration reduced pain more than buccal in the IANB arm [RCT, Iran Endod J 2018].
**Intrapulpal** — no SR/MA or RCT isolates its efficacy in SIP; it appears in trials only as a final rescue. Evidence level: expert consensus/observational. Flagged.
## 6. Newest materials and devices — what the high-level evidence supports
| Product class | Best evidence | Verdict for hot mandibular molars |
|---|---|---|
| Computerized intraosseous (QuickSleeper) | RCT: 91% vs 69% IANB | Best-supported new hardware; monitor HR, use 1:200,000 epi in cardiac patients |
| Buffering systems (Onset/Anutra type) | MA: faster onset, less pain, no success gain | Comfort only |
| Articaine 4% | 3 MAs conflict; latest shows equal success | Use for infiltration/PDL; block advantage unproven |
| Intranasal tetracaine/oxymetazoline (Kovanaze) | Phase 3 RCT: 84.1% vs 27.3% placebo for maxillary teeth; but 22–37% vs 89–91% for lidocaine infiltration with more adverse effects [RCT, J Endod 2019] | Maxillary only; irrelevant to mandible |
| Liposomal bupivacaine (Exparel) | Cochrane: reduces pain vs placebo, not shown superior to plain bupivacaine; 9-RCT MA: no reduction in pain or opioid use vs plain LA | Post-op tool at best |
| Phentolamine (OraVerse) | Two phase III RCTs: lower-lip recovery 70 vs 155 min | Comfort/soft-tissue only |
| Suzetrigine (Journavx) | FDA-approved Jan 2025 as first selective NaV1.8 inhibitor; phase 3 in abdominoplasty and bunionectomy [RCTs, systemic analgesic] | Mechanistically targets the TTX-resistant channel implicated in hot teeth, but zero endodontic anesthesia data — watch, don't use for this indication |
## 7. Evidence-graded protocol for the SIP mandibular molar
1. Ibuprofen ≥400 mg (or dexamethasone / ketorolac) 1 h pre-op — [SR/MA+TSA ×2, NMA].
2. IANB with 3.6 mL — [SR/MA+TSA, high quality].
3. Ice pack over the site while waiting — [SR/MA, OR 1.44].
4. Test the tooth (cold/EPT), not the lip — [diagnostic study].
5. If any response: intraosseous first [NMA top rank; RCT 92.5%]; PDL with 0.6 mL if no IO device [SR/MA RR 3.56].
6. Reserve articaine buccal infiltration for premolars (73%) rather than molars (42–48%) [RCT].
7. Intrapulpal on exposure — [consensus only].
