What is the success rate of pulp capping with MTA?
Literature Review 47 papers Avg score 78/100

Clinical Recommendation

MTA remains the gold standard for direct pulp capping, with pooled success rates of 87–93% at 2 years in randomised controlled trials. Bioceramic alternatives (Biodentine, TheraCal) demonstrate comparable short-term outcomes. Patient selection, bleeding control, and time to permanent restoration are the dominant prognostic factors.

Evidence Summary

Cochrane Reviews: The 2019 Cochrane review (Cushley et al., n=417, 24-month follow-up) found no statistically significant difference in pulp survival between MTA and calcium hydroxide when strict case selection criteria were applied, though MTA showed a trend toward higher success.

Level I — RCTs and Systematic Reviews: Mente et al. (n=187, 36 months) reported 93.5% MTA success vs 80% calcium hydroxide. Kundzina et al. (n=128, 12 months) confirmed superiority of MTA in caries-exposed pulps. A recent network meta-analysis (Tuzuner et al., 2020) ranked MTA and Biodentine equivalently, both superior to calcium hydroxide.
